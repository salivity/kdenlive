Configure Kdenlive with ALSA for Low Latency Audio

Configuring Kdenlive to use the Advanced Linux Sound Architecture (ALSA) driver allows video editors to bypass intermediate sound servers and achieve direct, low-latency audio playback on Linux systems. This guide details the essential steps to switch your Kdenlive playback backend to ALSA, select the correct hardware device, and optimize buffer settings for responsive timeline scrubbing without audio distortion.

1. Open the Playback Configuration Menu

  1. Launch Kdenlive.
  2. Go to the top menu and select Settings > Configure Kdenlive... (or press Ctrl + Shift + ,).
  3. In the configuration window, click on the Playback tab in the left-hand sidebar.

2. Select ALSA as the Audio Backend

  1. Locate the Audio Backend or Audio Driver section within the Playback settings.
  2. Open the dropdown menu (which typically defaults to Automatic, PulseAudio, or SDL).
  3. Select ALSA from the list of available drivers.

3. Specify the Audio Device

  1. Look for the Audio Device field directly below the backend selection.
  2. Choose your preferred output:
    • Default: Routes through the standard system ALSA configuration.
    • Direct Hardware Device (e.g., hw:0,0 or specific sound card name): Directly addresses the physical audio interface for the lowest possible latency. Note that exclusive hardware access may prevent other applications from playing sound simultaneously.

4. Fine-Tune Audio Buffer Settings

  1. Adjust the Audio Buffer size to balance latency and stability:
    • Set a smaller buffer (such as 256 or 512 samples) to reduce audio lag during preview playback.
    • If you experience audio stuttering, clicks, or dropouts (underruns), incrementally increase the buffer size to 1024 samples or higher.

5. Apply Settings and Restart

  1. Click Apply, then click OK to close the configuration dialog.
  2. Close and restart Kdenlive to force the underlying MLT multimedia framework to reinitialize using the ALSA audio driver.
  3. Import a media clip to the timeline and press the spacebar to verify that the audio plays smoothly with minimal delay.
