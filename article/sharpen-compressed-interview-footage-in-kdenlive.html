Sharpen Compressed Interview Footage in Kdenlive

Compressed interview footage often suffers from soft edges, macroblocking, and reduced detail. Applying the "Sharpen (Unsharp Mask)" filter in Kdenlive restores perceived crispness to facial features and background elements without over-accentuating compression artifacts. This guide covers how to locate the filter, apply it to your timeline, and configure the essential parameters for balanced, professional results.

1. Add the Footage to the Timeline

Import your compressed interview clip into the Project Bin and drag it onto your target video track in the Timeline.

2. Locate and Apply the Effect

  1. Open the Effects panel (go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  2. Search for Sharpen or Unsharp Mask in the search bar. Alternatively, browse to the Blur and Sharpen category.
  3. Drag the Sharpen (Unsharp Mask) effect directly onto the interview clip in your timeline, or select the clip and double-click the effect.

3. Adjust the Effect Settings

Select the clip and open the Effect/Composition Stack panel to fine-tune the parameters. Because the source footage is compressed, conservative adjustments are crucial to avoid highlighting noise or creating harsh halos around your subject.

  • Radius / Size: Set this to a low value, typically between 0.5 and 1.5. A smaller radius targets fine details like eyelashes, hair, and clothing texture without creating visible white borders around high-contrast edges.
  • Amount / Strength: Start low (around 30% to 70%). Increase this gradually until the speaker's eyes and facial contours look distinct. Avoid setting this too high, as extreme values will make compression blocks and skin imperfections overly pronounced.
  • Threshold: This parameter is essential for compressed video. Increase the threshold slightly to tell the filter to ignore minor color variations (such as background compression noise or flat skin tones) and only sharpen strong, distinct edges.

4. Verify at Full Resolution

Set your Project Monitor zoom level to 100% (1:1) and toggle the effect on and off using the eye icon in the Effect Stack. Check the subject's eyes, hair, and edges against the background to ensure the sharpening looks natural and free of halo artifacts or enhanced digital noise.
