How to Toggle Video Thumbnails in Kdenlive

If you are experiencing lag, stuttering, or slow rendering while scrubbing through your video editing project, disabling video thumbnails on the timeline can significantly boost your system's performance. This guide provides a quick, step-by-step walkthrough on how to toggle the "Show Video Thumbnails" option in Kdenlive to ensure smoother timeline scrolling and a highly responsive editing experience.

Method 1: Using the Timeline Hamburger Menu (Quickest)

The fastest way to toggle video thumbnails on and off is directly from the timeline interface:

  1. Look at the toolbar directly above your Kdenlive timeline.
  2. Locate and click on the Timeline Menu icon (represented by three horizontal lines, often called the hamburger menu, on the far right of the timeline toolbar).
  3. In the dropdown menu, find the Show Video Thumbnails option.
  4. Click it to check (enable) or uncheck (disable) the option.

Once unchecked, the video strips on your timeline will display as solid color blocks with text labels instead of continuous image previews, instantly freeing up system resources.

Method 2: Adjusting Global Settings

If you want to set your default thumbnail preferences for all future projects, you can do so through the main configuration menu:

  1. Open Kdenlive and go to the top menu bar.
  2. Select Settings > Configure Kdenlive (on Linux or Windows) or Kdenlive > Preferences (on macOS).
  3. In the configuration window, click on the Timeline tab in the left sidebar.
  4. Look for the Thumbnails or Display section.
  5. Check or uncheck Video thumbnails according to your preference.
  6. Click Apply and then OK to save your changes.

Why Disabling Thumbnails Improves Performance

Every time you scroll, zoom, or scrub through the timeline, Kdenlive has to decode the video files in real-time to generate the visual frame previews shown on the tracks. On high-resolution footage (like 4K) or slower storage drives, this process causes severe bottlenecking. Disabling video thumbnails stops this constant background rendering, allowing your CPU and storage drive to focus entirely on smooth video playback.
