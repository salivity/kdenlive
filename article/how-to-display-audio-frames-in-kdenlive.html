How to Display Audio Frames in Kdenlive

This guide explains how to configure Kdenlive's timeline ruler and project settings to switch from standard timecode displays to frame-accurate and audio-sample views. Following these steps enables you to achieve sub-frame precision and perfectly align multiple audio tracks or sync external audio with video clips.

Step 1: Switch the Timeline Timecode Format

By default, Kdenlive displays the timeline ruler in standard timecode format (Hours:Minutes:Seconds:Frames). You can change this to show exact frame numbers:

  1. Right-click anywhere on the Timeline Ruler at the top of the timeline track area.
  2. Select Show Frames from the context menu (or toggle between Timecode and Frames depending on your Kdenlive version).
  3. Alternatively, click on the Timecode Display box located above the timeline track headers to toggle the view between standard timecode (HH:MM:SS:FF) and raw frame numbers.

Step 2: Enable High-Precision Audio Waveforms

To visually align audio at the sample level, ensure that high-resolution waveforms are active on your audio tracks:

  1. Go to the top menu and select Settings > Configure Kdenlive.
  2. Navigate to the Timeline tab.
  3. Under the Audio section, check the box for Show audio thumbnails.
  4. Set the thumbnail layout to Separate channels if you are working with stereo or multi-channel audio for clearer visual peaks.
  5. Click Apply and then OK.

Step 3: Zoom In for Sample-Level Alignment

Kdenlive allows you to zoom down to individual audio samples within frames:

  1. Hover your cursor over the timeline area.
  2. Hold Ctrl and scroll up with your mouse wheel, or drag the Zoom Slider at the bottom-right of the window all the way to the right.
  3. Once fully zoomed in, the ruler displays sub-frame intervals, and the audio waveform reveals individual sample peaks.

Step 4: Disable Snapping for Micro-Adjustments

When aligning audio tracks to exact samples, the automatic grid snapping might restrict movement:

  1. Click the Snap icon (magnet symbol) in the timeline toolbar to turn it off, or press the shortcut key Shift + F10.
  2. Drag your audio clip to align the peaks of the waveforms.
  3. For minute adjustments, select the clip and use the left or right arrow keys (or the configured nudge shortcuts) to shift the audio track by single-frame or sub-frame increments.
