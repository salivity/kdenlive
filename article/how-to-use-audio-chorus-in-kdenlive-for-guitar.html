How to Use Audio Chorus in Kdenlive for Guitar

Applying a chorus effect to a guitar track can transform a flat, mono recording into a lush, wide, and textured sound. In this guide, you will learn the exact steps to locate, apply, and adjust the built-in Chorus audio effect in Kdenlive to enhance the stereo width and thickness of your guitar tracks.

Step 1: Add Your Guitar Track to the Timeline

  1. Open Kdenlive and import your guitar audio file or video recording into the Project Bin.
  2. Drag and drop the clip onto an audio track in the timeline.

Step 2: Open the Effects Panel

  1. Navigate to the Effects tab in the interface (if it is not visible, go to View > Effects in the top menu bar).
  2. Click on the Audio Effects category or use the search bar at the top of the Effects panel.
  3. Type Chorus into the search bar to locate the chorus filter.

Step 3: Apply the Chorus Effect

  1. Click and drag the Chorus effect directly onto your guitar clip in the timeline.
  2. Alternatively, select the guitar clip first and double-click the Chorus effect in the panel.
  3. Select the clip to view the effect's controls in the Effect/Composition Stack panel.

Step 4: Configure Parameters for Thickness and Width

To achieve a thick, wide guitar tone without sounding overly detuned, adjust the following parameters:

  • Delay: Set this between 10 ms and 30 ms. Shorter delay times produce a tighter doubling effect, while longer times create a more noticeable echo separation.
  • Frequency / Rate: Set a slow modulation speed (typically between 0.5 Hz and 1.5 Hz) to create a gentle, natural movement without an aggressive warble.
  • Depth: Adjust the modulation depth to around 20% to 40% to introduce subtle pitch variation for thickness.
  • Stereo Spread / Phase: Increase the stereo phase difference (if available on the specific chorus plugin) to push the left and right channels apart, expanding the stereo field.
  • Wet / Dry Mix: Balance the original sound with the processed sound. A mix of 30% to 50% wet signal retains the core attack of the guitar while blending in the wide, chorused layer.

Step 5: Preview and Export

  1. Play back the timeline using headphones or stereo monitors to evaluate the stereo image.
  2. Fine-tune the wet/dry balance and depth until the guitar sounds full without phase cancellation issues.
  3. Once satisfied, proceed to render your project via Project > Render.
