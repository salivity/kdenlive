How to Use the Bandpass Audio Filter in Kdenlive

The Bandpass audio filter in Kdenlive allows video editors to isolate specific sound frequencies by simultaneously cutting off frequencies above and below a selected range. This guide explains how to apply the Bandpass filter to your audio, understand its primary parameters, configure the center frequency and bandwidth for precise isolation, and boost the resulting signal for clean playback.

1. Apply the Bandpass Filter

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and select the audio clip (or video clip with attached audio) on the timeline.
  2. Navigate to the Effects panel (usually located alongside the Project Bin or accessible via Menu > Settings > Audio Editing layout).
  3. Search for Bandpass in the search bar.
  4. Drag and drop the Bandpass effect directly onto your target clip on the timeline, or onto the whole audio track header if you wish to apply it globally.

2. Configure the Key Parameters

Once applied, access the effect controls in the Effect/Composition Stack panel:

  • Frequency (Center Frequency): This sets the exact frequency (measured in Hertz, Hz) that you want to preserve at maximum volume. For example:
    • Low-end isolation (bass, kicks): 60 Hz – 250 Hz
    • Vocal presence/clarity: 1,000 Hz – 3,500 Hz
    • Telephone / Walkie-talkie effect: 1,000 Hz – 2,000 Hz
    • High-end isolation (sibilance, cymbals): 5,000 Hz – 10,000 Hz
  • Bandwidth (Width / Q Factor): This defines how wide or narrow the frequency range is around your center frequency.
    • A lower bandwidth (higher Q) narrows the passband to isolate a very specific tone or hum.
    • A higher bandwidth (lower Q) broadens the passband to allow a natural range of frequencies around the center point.

3. Fine-Tune with Real-Time Playback

  1. Set an In/Out zone around the audio section on the timeline and enable Loop Playback (Spacebar or the Loop button).
  2. Slowly adjust the Frequency slider until the target sound (such as a voice or a specific instrument) is clearly audible while unwanted lows and highs fade away.
  3. Adjust the Bandwidth control outward until the audio sounds natural enough without letting unwanted background rumble or high-frequency hiss back into the mix.

4. Compensate for Lost Gain

Because the Bandpass filter removes significant portions of the audio spectrum, the overall clip volume will decrease. Add the Volume (Keyframeable) or Gain effect below the Bandpass filter in the Effect Stack and raise the dB level until the audio meter peaks safely between -12 dB and -6 dB.
