Toggle GPU Timeline Processing in Kdenlive

This guide outlines how to toggle GPU-based timeline processing in experimental and nightly builds of Kdenlive. Enabling this feature offloads video compositing and real-time effects processing from the CPU to the graphics card, improving playback performance on supported hardware. Below, you will find direct instructions to locate the experimental setting, enable or disable it, and ensure your system meets the necessary hardware requirements.

Prerequisites

Before adjusting timeline acceleration settings, ensure you meet the following conditions:

  • You are running an experimental or daily build of Kdenlive.
  • Your graphics drivers are updated and support OpenGL 3.2 or newer.
  • Your build includes the MLT framework compiled with Movit support.

Steps to Toggle GPU Timeline Processing

  1. Open Kdenlive Settings: Launch Kdenlive and navigate to the top menu bar. Click on Settings and select Configure Kdenlive (on macOS, select Kdenlive > Preferences).

  2. Access the Playback Tab: In the configuration window, select the Playback tab from the left sidebar. In some experimental builds, this may alternatively appear under the Environment or Timeline tab.

  3. Locate the GPU Processing Option: Look for the section labeled GPU processing (Movit library) or Experimental GPU Timeline Processing.

  4. Toggle the Setting:

    • To Enable: Check the box next to GPU processing.
    • To Disable: Uncheck the box to revert timeline compositing to software rendering via the CPU.

  5. Apply and Restart: Click Apply, then OK to close the preferences window. Close Kdenlive completely and restart the application for the changes to take effect.

Troubleshooting and Considerations

  • Stability Issues: Because Movit-based GPU timeline processing is experimental, it can cause crashes, visual artifacts, or missing transitions depending on your project complexity. If you experience persistent crashes after launching, disable the option and restart.
  • Render Profiles: Timeline GPU processing handles real-time playback compositing, which is separate from GPU-accelerated video encoding (such as NVENC or VAAPI) configured in the final rendering dialog.
