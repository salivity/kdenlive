How to Adjust Keyframe Smoothing in Kdenlive

Adjusting keyframe smoothing in Kdenlive allows you to transform rigid, linear motion into fluid, natural animations. This guide explains how to access Kdenlive’s keyframe interpolation options within the Transform effect, switch between different motion curves, and fine-tune your animations for professional-looking video edits.

Step 1: Apply the Transform Effect

  1. Select the clip on your timeline that you want to animate.
  2. Open the Effects tab (typically located in the project panel or via Menu > View > Effects).
  3. Search for Transform and double-click or drag it onto your selected clip.
  4. Open the Effect/Composition Stack panel to view the Transform controls.

Step 2: Create Your Keyframes

  1. Move the timeline playhead within the Transform effect panel to where you want the motion to start.
  2. Adjust your clip's position, scale, or rotation to set the initial state, which automatically creates your first keyframe.
  3. Move the playhead forward to the target time and adjust the clip's properties again to generate a second keyframe.

Step 3: Change Keyframe Interpolation (Smoothing)

By default, Kdenlive applies Linear interpolation, resulting in sudden stops and starts. To apply smoothing:

  1. In the Effect Stack keyframe ruler, click on the specific keyframe you want to adjust to select it.
  2. Locate the keyframe interpolation icon or dropdown menu (found directly above or beside the keyframe timeline in the effect controls).
  3. Select your desired interpolation type:
    • Smooth: Automatically calculates a curved velocity profile to ease in and out of the keyframe.
    • B-Spline: Creates a softer, sweeping curve across multiple keyframes for complex paths.
    • Catmull-Rom: Passes precisely through each keyframe while maintaining smooth curvature.
    • Linear: Maintains a constant speed directly between points without easing.
    • Discrete: Immediately jumps values at the keyframe without any transition.

Step 4: Fine-Tune the Motion

  1. Set the first keyframe to Smooth to create an ease-out effect where the motion starts gently.
  2. Set the destination keyframe to Smooth to create an ease-in effect so the movement decelerates naturally to a stop.
  3. Play back the timeline in the Project Monitor to preview the adjusted velocity and make position or timing changes as needed.
