How to Lock Tracks in Kdenlive to Prevent Ripple Edits

This guide explains how to secure your media clips in Kdenlive by enabling timeline track locks. When using ripple editing tools or inserting footage, unlocked tracks often shift unintentionally, disrupting audio synchronization and visual pacing. By learning how to engage track locks and adjust editing modes, you can safeguard your project timeline from accidental modifications.

Locking Individual Tracks in Kdenlive

The primary method for protecting a sequence against ripple alterations is the track lock feature located in the track header on the left side of the timeline.

  1. Locate the Track Header: Look at the left side of the timeline where track names (e.g., V1, V2, A1, A2) are displayed.
  2. Toggle the Padlock Icon: Click the Lock (padlock) icon on any track you wish to protect.
  3. Verify the Locked State: Once clicked, the icon will appear closed, and the entire track will turn slightly darker or display diagonal stripes.

When a track is locked, clips on that track cannot be cut, moved, trimmed, or shifted by ripple edits performed on adjacent tracks.

Preventing Unintended Ripple Shifts

Ripple edits—such as deleting a region with Extract (Shift + X) or inserting clips with Insert (V)—affect all unlocked tracks by default. To prevent ripple shifts without locking entire tracks:

  • Toggle Edit Modes: In the bottom toolbar of the timeline, switch from Ripple Edit Mode to Normal Edit Mode when performing standard cuts and trims.
  • Use Track Target Settings: Ensure active target tracks are explicitly chosen. Tracks that are not targeted or are locked will remain unaffected during insert operations.
  • Configure Spacer Tool Behavior: If using the Spacer tool (M), hold Shift to move clips only on the active track rather than moving clips across every unlocked track simultaneously.

Unlocking Tracks for Future Edits

To make changes to a protected track later in your workflow:

  1. Click the closed padlock icon in the corresponding track header.
  2. The track will return to its standard color, re-enabling editing, trimming, and ripple responses for all clips residing on that layer.
