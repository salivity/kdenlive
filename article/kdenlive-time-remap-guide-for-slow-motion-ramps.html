Kdenlive Time Remap Guide for Slow-Motion Ramps

The Time Remap keyframe editor in Kdenlive allows video editors to dynamically alter the playback speed of a clip across its duration using a visual graph. When producing extreme slow-motion speed ramps, this tool provides precise control over the acceleration and deceleration of footage without requiring cuts, enabling smooth transitions from real-time playback to ultra-slow motion and back.

The Mechanism of the Time Remap Graph

The primary function of the Time Remap editor is to plot the relationship between the project timeline (the X-axis, representing output time) and the clip's original recorded frames (the Y-axis, representing source time). The slope of the line between keyframes dictates playback speed:

  • Shallow Slope: Represents slow motion. The flatter the curve, the slower the playback, as fewer source frames are displayed over a longer period of timeline time.
  • Horizontal Line: Represents a freeze frame (zero playback speed).
  • Steep Slope: Represents fast-forward motion.
  • Negative Slope: Plays footage in reverse.

Keyframe Control and Speed Ramping

To execute a speed ramp, you place keyframes at the exact moments where the speed transition should begin and end. The editor lets you define:

  • Source Time and Output Time: By moving a keyframe horizontally or vertically, you designate precisely which source frame appears at which moment on the timeline.
  • Interpolation Modes: You can toggle between linear transitions and smooth (Bézier/spline) curves. For extreme slow-motion ramps, smooth interpolation creates an ease-in and ease-out effect, preventing abrupt, jarring shifts in motion.

Managing Extreme Slow-Motion Playback

When stretching a small number of source frames over a long timeline duration (such as dropping speed to 1% to 10%), the Time Remap editor works alongside Kdenlive's frame-interpolation settings:

  • Frame Duplication/Blending: By default, extreme slow motion repeats frames, which can result in a choppy cadence if the original footage was not shot at a high frame rate (e.g., 60fps, 120fps, or higher).
  • Optical Flow / Motion Estimation: To smooth out extreme ramps on lower frame-rate footage, Kdenlive can generate intermediate synthetic frames to bridge the gap between keyframed points on the remap curve.

Audio Handling During Remapping

The Time Remap tool directly impacts audio synchronization. During an extreme slow-motion ramp, the editor either stretches the audio (lowering pitch or using time-stretching algorithms) or mutes it, allowing the visual ramp to remain the central focus while preserving the integrity of the project timeline.
