How to Correct GoPro Lens Distortion in Kdenlive

GoPro cameras are famous for their ultra-wide-angle "fisheye" perspective, but this barrel distortion isn't always desirable for every video project. This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step tutorial on how to use Kdenlive's built-in correction tools to flatten your footage, remove the fisheye look, and achieve a natural, straight-lined horizon.

Step 1: Add Your Footage to the Timeline

Open Kdenlive and import your GoPro video clip into the Project Bin. Drag and drop the clip onto your timeline where you want to perform the edit.

Step 2: Locate the Lens Correction Effect

Navigate to the Effects tab (usually located in the bottom-left or side panel). In the search bar at the top of the Effects panel, type "Lens Correction" or "Lens Distortion".

Note: Kdenlive offers a few variations depending on your version, but "Lens Correction" is the most effective tool for wide-angle camera adjustment.

Step 3: Apply the Effect to Your Clip

Click and drag the Lens Correction effect directly onto your GoPro clip in the timeline. Once applied, the effect options will appear in the Properties (or Effect Stack) panel, typically located in the top-right of the interface.

Step 4: Adjust the Correction Parameters

To fix the curved "barrel" distortion of the GoPro, you need to adjust the horizontal and vertical correction sliders. Look for the following settings in the Effect Stack:

  • Correction Center: Keep this at the default center coordinates (usually 500 for both X and Y) unless your camera sensor was misaligned.
  • Correction Horizontal: Drag this slider to the left (negative values). For typical GoPro footage, a value between -10 and -25 is usually the sweet spot to straighten vertical lines on the sides of the frame.
  • Correction Vertical: Adjust this slightly if you also need to correct tilt distortion, though keeping it close to the horizontal value yields the most symmetrical results.

Watch the Project Monitor in real-time as you adjust these sliders. Focus on objects that should be perfectly straight, such as buildings, trees, or the horizon, and adjust the values until those lines appear straight.

Step 5: Zoom to Remove Black Edges (Optional)

Correcting lens distortion pushes the corners of the video inward, which can sometimes leave black, empty spaces or heavily warped pixels at the edges of your frame.

To fix this:

  1. Locate the Brighten/Zoom slider within the Lens Correction effect, or add a separate Transform effect to your clip.
  2. Increase the Size or Zoom parameter slightly (usually between 105% and 110%) until the black borders are pushed off-screen.

Once the edges are clean and the horizon is flat, your GoPro footage is successfully corrected and ready for rendering.
