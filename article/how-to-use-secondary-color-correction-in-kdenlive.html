How to Use Secondary Color Correction in Kdenlive

This guide demonstrates how to use the "Secondary Color Correction" effect in Kdenlive to isolate and change the color of a specific object, such as a car, without affecting the rest of your video. You will learn how to select the target color, fine-tune the selection mask, and adjust the hue, saturation, and brightness to achieve a seamless and realistic color swap.

Step 1: Apply the Effect to Your Clip

  1. Import your video footage into Kdenlive and drag it onto the timeline.
  2. Go to the Effects tab (usually located in the bottom-left or top-right pane).
  3. Search for Secondary Color Correction.
  4. Drag and drop the effect directly onto your video clip on the timeline.

Step 2: Select the Target Color

To change the color of an object like a car, you must first tell Kdenlive which color to target.

  1. Select the clip on your timeline to open the Effect/Composition Stack panel.
  2. Inside the "Secondary Color Correction" settings, look for the Color picker (the eyedropper tool).
  3. Click the eyedropper tool, then click on the most prominent color of the car in the project monitor.

Step 3: Isolate the Object Using the Mask

To ensure your color changes only apply to the car and not the background, you need to refine the selection mask.

  1. Check the box for Show Mask. Your video preview will change, showing the selected color area in white or color, and the unselected areas in black or grayscale.
  2. Adjust the Hue width, Saturation width, and Value width sliders.
    • Increase or decrease these values until only the car is highlighted.
    • If parts of the background (like the sky or road) are also highlighted, narrow the widths.
  3. Use the Softness or Smoothing sliders to soften the edges of the selection, which prevents harsh, pixelated color borders.

Step 4: Change and Refine the Color

Once the car is isolated, you can change its color.

  1. Uncheck the Show Mask box so you can see the actual colors of your video again.
  2. Locate the Modify section within the effect parameters.
  3. Adjust the Hue slider to rotate the color wheel. Dragging this slider will shift the car's color (e.g., from red to blue or green).
  4. Use the Saturation slider to make the new color more vibrant or more muted.
  5. Adjust the Gain or Value slider to match the brightness of the new color to the environmental lighting of the original scene.
