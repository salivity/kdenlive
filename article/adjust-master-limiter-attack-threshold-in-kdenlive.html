Adjust Master Limiter Attack Threshold in Kdenlive

This guide explains how to configure the master channel limiter in Kdenlive, specifically focusing on adjusting the attack and threshold parameters. Applying a limiter to the master audio track allows you to catch dynamic peaks, eliminate audio clipping, and maintain consistent loudness across your entire video project before final export.

Step 1: Open the Master Channel Effects

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive.
  2. In the Timeline panel, locate the track header on the far left.
  3. Click on the Master track tab (located at the top or bottom of your track list, depending on your layout) to select it. Alternatively, open the Audio Mixer via View > Audio Mixer and select the Master strip.
  4. Ensure the Effects tab and Effect/Composition Stack panel are visible in your workspace.

Step 2: Add the Limiter Effect

  1. In the Effects panel, select the Audio category or use the search bar.
  2. Search for Limiter or Fast Lookahead Limiter.
  3. Drag and drop the effect directly onto the Master track header, or double-click the effect while the Master track is selected.

Step 3: Adjust the Threshold

  1. Select the Master track so its applied effects appear in the Effect/Composition Stack panel.
  2. Locate the Threshold (or Input Gain / Target Level) parameter.
  3. Adjust the threshold slider or enter a numerical decibel (dB) value.
    • Setting a lower threshold forces the limiter to engage earlier on quieter peaks.
    • For general video mastering, set the threshold to catch only the loudest transients to avoid unnecessary dynamic flattening.

Step 4: Adjust the Attack Time

  1. Locate the Attack (or Release / Lookahead) parameter in the limiter's settings.
  2. Adjust the attack duration (measured in milliseconds or seconds):
    • Fast Attack (Short duration): Catches rapid transient peaks immediately, providing strict protection against clipping.
    • Slow Attack (Longer duration): Lets initial transients pass through for a more natural, punchy sound, but increases the risk of momentary peaks exceeding your ceiling.
  3. Balance the attack time to ensure distortion-free limiting without introducing audio pumping artifacts.

Step 5: Test and Monitor Audio Levels

  1. Play your project timeline from the loudest sections of your sequence.
  2. Watch the Master Audio Meter in the Audio Mixer.
  3. Ensure the output stays below 0 dBFS (ideally peaking around -1.0 dB to -2.0 dB true peak) to guarantee no digital distortion occurs during rendering.
