Create Overlapping Text Layers in Kdenlive

Creating depth and unique visual styles in your videos often requires overlapping text layers. This guide provides a step-by-step walkthrough on how to create and arrange multiple overlapping text layers within a single title clip in Kdenlive. You will learn how to add multiple text boxes, adjust their layering order using stacking tools, and style them to achieve a professional, multi-layered typographic effect.

Step 1: Create a New Title Clip

  1. Open Kdenlive and locate the Project Bin.
  2. Right-click an empty space in the Project Bin and select Add Title Clip (or use the shortcut Ctrl + T). This opens the Kdenlive Title Clip editor window.

Step 2: Add Your First Text Layer

  1. Click the Add Text icon (represented by a "T" letter) in the toolbar at the top of the title editor window.
  2. Click anywhere on the black canvas preview area.
  3. Type your first word or letter.
  4. Use the properties panel on the right to adjust the font, size, color, and weight. This layer will serve as either your background or foreground text.

Step 3: Add Additional Text Layers

  1. To create an overlapping effect, you need a separate text box. Click the Add Text icon again.
  2. Click on a different area of the canvas to create a new, independent text box.
  3. Type your second piece of text and style it. Using contrasting colors or different font weights helps make the overlap visually distinct.

Step 4: Overlap and Position the Text

  1. Click the Selection Tool (the arrow icon) in the toolbar.
  2. Click and drag your second text box directly over the first text box.
  3. Align them to your preference. You can use the alignment buttons on the right panel to center them horizontally or vertically if needed.

Step 5: Adjust the Layer Stacking Order (Z-Index)

Kdenlive determines which text sits on top of the other based on the layer stacking order. If your background text is accidentally covering your foreground text, you can change their order:

  1. Click on the text box you want to move.
  2. Look at the toolbar at the top of the Title Clip editor. You will see four layering buttons:
    • Bring to Front: Moves the selected text to the very top layer.
    • Raise: Moves the selected text up by one layer.
    • Lower: Moves the selected text down by one layer.
    • Send to Back: Moves the selected text to the very bottom layer.
  3. Click Bring to Front on the text layer you want to appear on top, or Send to Back on the layer you want behind.

Step 6: Apply Styling for Better Separation

To make overlapping text highly readable, add visual separation:

  • Add a Shadow: Select the top text layer, check the Shadow option in the right-hand properties panel, and adjust the blur and color to create depth.
  • Add an Outline: Select your text and increase the Outline width (contour) to create a border that separates it from the text layer underneath.

Step 7: Save and Add to Timeline

  1. Once you are satisfied with the overlapping arrangement, click the Create Clip (or OK) button at the bottom right of the title editor window.
  2. Drag the newly created title clip from your Project Bin onto your timeline.
