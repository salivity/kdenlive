Adjust Low-Cut Filter for Dialogue in Kdenlive

Applying a low-cut filter to dialogue tracks in Kdenlive is essential for eliminating low-frequency background rumble, mic handling noise, and heavy plosives. By utilizing Kdenlive’s built-in audio effects stack, you can quickly insert a high-pass filter, isolate the unwanted low frequencies, and fine-tune the cutoff frequency to preserve natural vocal clarity while improving overall audio quality.

Step 1: Select the Dialogue Clip or Track

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and locate your dialogue audio in the Timeline.
  2. To apply the filter to a single audio event, click directly on the audio clip.
  3. To apply the filter to all dialogue clips at once, click the Track Header (the left-hand control area of the audio track) to add track-level effects.

Step 2: Open the Effects Stack and Locate the Filter

  1. Open the Effects tab in your workspace. If it is hidden, enable it via View > Effects.
  2. Select the Audio Effects category or use the search bar at the top of the Effects panel.
  3. Search for High Pass (which functions as a low-cut filter by cutting low frequencies and letting high frequencies pass).
  4. Drag and drop the High Pass effect onto your selected clip or directly into the Properties / Effect Stack panel.

Step 3: Configure the Cutoff Frequency

Once the effect is visible in the Effect Stack:

  1. Locate the Frequency (Hz) slider.
  2. Set the baseline frequency based on the speaker's vocal range:
    • Male Dialogue: Set the cutoff frequency between 80 Hz and 100 Hz.
    • Female Dialogue: Set the cutoff frequency between 100 Hz and 130 Hz.
    • High Rumble/Wind Interference: Increase the cutoff toward 150 Hz, monitoring carefully to avoid making the voice sound thin or hollow.
  3. If available in the specific filter version (such as LADSPA/SWH plugins), adjust the Poles or Roll-off Slope to determine how steeply the frequencies below the threshold are reduced.

Step 4: Preview and Fine-Tune

  1. Play back the timeline segment with the speaker talking.
  2. Toggle the effect on and off using the eye icon or checkmark next to the effect name in the Effect Stack to compare the filtered audio against the original.
  3. Adjust the frequency value incrementally until the low-end mud is eliminated while the speaker's voice maintains its warmth and presence.
