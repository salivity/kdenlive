How to Import External Subtitles into Kdenlive

Using external AI transcription tools like OpenAI's Whisper, Descript, or auto-captioning web services allows video editors to generate accurate captions quickly. Integrating these external subtitle files into Kdenlive is a straightforward process that involves preparing standard subtitle formats, importing them into Kdenlive’s dedicated subtitle track, and synchronizing them with your timeline for final styling and rendering.

1. Export the Subtitle File from the AI Tool

Before opening Kdenlive, ensure your AI transcription model exports your captions into a compatible format.

  • Kdenlive primarily supports SubRip (.srt) and WebVTT (.vtt) formats.
  • Choose .srt whenever possible, as it provides the most consistent compatibility with timeline framerates.

2. Open the Subtitle Track in Kdenlive

To manage subtitles in your Kdenlive project:

  1. Open your project with your edited video and audio already placed on the timeline.
  2. Go to the top menu and select Project > Subtitles > Add Subtitle Track, or click the Subtitle Tool icon (speech bubble) located directly above the timeline toolbar.
  3. A dedicated subtitle track will appear at the very top of the timeline.

3. Import the External Subtitle File

Once the track is active:

  1. Click Project > Subtitles > Import Subtitle File from the top menu, or click the Import Subtitle button within the Subtitle panel.
  2. Browse your file system and select the .srt or .vtt file generated by your AI model.
  3. In the import dialog, configure the following options:
    • Framerate: Ensure the framerate matches your project settings to prevent drift over time.
    • Start Position: Choose whether the subtitles should import starting at the playhead position or at the timeline's beginning (00:00:00:00).
  4. Click OK to populate the track.

4. Synchronize and Edit Captions

AI models occasionally introduce slight timing offsets or transcription errors:

  • Timing Adjustments: Zoom into the timeline to compare the subtitle blocks against the audio waveform. Click and drag subtitle blocks to align them precisely with spoken words.
  • Text Corrections: Open the Subtitles dock (Menu > View > Subtitles) to edit text, split long sentences, or merge multiple lines.

5. Style and Render Subtitles

Decide how you want the final video to handle the subtitles:

  • Burned-In (Hardcoded): Use the Subtitle tool panel to customize font, size, color, background, and alignment. When rendering (Project > Render), check the option to burn subtitles directly into the video frames.
  • Separate Stream (Soft Subtitles): In the Render dialog, enable subtitles as a separate stream (available for containers like .mkv or .mp4) so viewers can toggle them on and off in media players.
