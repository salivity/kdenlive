How to Apply Audio Flanger in Kdenlive

The Audio Flanger effect in Kdenlive allows video editors and sound designers to introduce a classic, psychedelic, swirling modulation to their tracks by mixing two identical signals together with a constantly varying delay. This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step walkthrough on how to locate, apply, and configure the Audio Flanger effect within Kdenlive to achieve vintage, sweeping acoustic textures.

1. Place Your Media on the Timeline

Open your project in Kdenlive and drag the audio track or video clip containing audio onto your timeline. Ensure the clip is selected so the effects you add are mapped correctly.

2. Locate the Audio Flanger Effect

  1. Navigate to the Effects tab in the interface (usually located in the bottom-left or center panel, or accessible via View > Effects).
  2. Click on the Audio category icon, or use the search bar at the top of the Effects panel.
  3. Type Flanger into the search box to find the Audio Flanger (or Flanger) effect.

3. Add the Effect to the Clip

Drag the Flanger effect from the Effects panel directly onto your target clip on the timeline. Alternatively, with the clip selected, double-click the effect to apply it.

4. Adjust the Flanger Parameters

Select your clip and switch to the Effect/Composition Stack panel to tweak the parameters to achieve the desired vintage swirling sound:

  • Delay: Controls the base delay time. Lower settings (1–5 ms) produce sharper, metallic tones, while slightly higher values (5–10 ms) yield warmer, vintage sweep sounds.
  • Depth: Determines the amplitude of the delay modulation. Increasing this value deepens the intensity of the "swirl."
  • Frequency / Speed (LFO): Controls how fast the flanging effect cycles up and down. Lower frequencies (0.1 Hz to 0.5 Hz) create slow, dramatic sweeps typical of 1970s audio recordings.
  • Regeneration / Feedback: Routes a portion of the processed output back into the input. Increasing feedback intensifies the resonance and creates the signature "jet plane" sweeping effect.
  • Mix / Wet-Dry: Sets the balance between the unprocessed (dry) signal and the modulated (wet) signal. A 50/50 mix typically delivers the most pronounced phase-cancellation swirl.

5. Preview and Fine-Tune

Play the timeline to preview the audio in real time. Adjust the Speed and Feedback sliders until you reach the exact vintage character required for your project. Once satisfied, continue editing or proceed to render your final video.
