How to Add Chromatic Aberration in Kdenlive

Chromatic aberration is an optical distortion where colors fringe along high-contrast edges, a visual hallmark of vintage lenses and imperfect analog camera optics. This guide provides a straightforward walkthrough on how to recreate this aesthetic in Kdenlive using built-in effects, allowing you to add subtle optical imperfections or heavy stylized color fringing to your footage.

Method 1: Using the Built-in RGB Shift Effect

The quickest way to introduce chromatic aberration is by using Kdenlive's built-in color-shifting effects.

  1. Import and Select Your Clip: Drag your video clip onto the timeline and click to select it.
  2. Open the Effects Panel: Go to the Effects tab (typically located in the bottom-left or center panel).
  3. Apply the Effect: Search for RGB Shift or Glitch in the search bar. Drag and drop the RGB Shift effect directly onto your timeline clip.
  4. Adjust the Values:
    • In the Effect/Composition Stack panel, adjust the horizontal and vertical shift sliders for the Red, Green, or Blue channels.
    • For a realistic vintage lens look, keep the values extremely low (between 1 and 4 pixels).
    • Shift red slightly to one side and blue to the opposite side to mimic authentic optical dispersion.

Method 2: Channel Splitting for Realistic Radial Aberration

Real lenses produce chromatic aberration that expands outward from the center (radial distortion) rather than shifting uniformly across the entire frame. You can build this manually using track compositing:

  1. Duplicate the Footage:

    • Place your video clip on track V1.
    • Duplicate the exact same clip to tracks V2 and V3, aligning them perfectly in time.

  2. Isolate Color Channels:

    • Apply the Color Matrix or RGB Adjustment effect to each track.
    • On V3 (Top Track): Set Green and Blue to 0, leaving only the Red channel active.
    • On V2 (Middle Track): Set Red and Blue to 0, leaving only the Green channel active.
    • On V1 (Bottom Track): Set Red and Green to 0, leaving only the Blue channel active.

  3. Set Blend Modes:

    • Change the composition/blend mode of V3 and V2 to Screen or Addition. The composite image will now look normal as the primary colors combine.

  4. Simulate Optical Scale Distortion:

    • Add the Transform effect to the top track (Red channel).
    • Set the scale slightly higher (e.g., 100.3%).
    • Add the Transform effect to the bottom track (Blue channel) and scale it down slightly (e.g., 99.7%).
    • Leave the middle track (Green) at 100%.

Because the center remains aligned while the edges stretch apart, this generates a realistic, radial color fringe that intensifies toward the corners of the frame.

Fine-Tuning the Vintage Look

To complete the vintage optic simulation, combine your chromatic aberration with these complementary effects:

  • Vignette: Add the Vignette effect to darken the edges of the frame, simulating the light falloff common in vintage glass.
  • Gaussian Blur (Subtle): Apply a very low blur (0.5–1%) to take off the digital sharpness of modern camera sensors.
  • Film Grain: Overlay a grain texture or apply a noise filter to emulate physical film stock.
