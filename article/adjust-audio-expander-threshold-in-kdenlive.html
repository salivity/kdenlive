Adjust Audio Expander Threshold in Kdenlive

Adjusting the clipping thresholds on the Audio Expander effect in Kdenlive allows you to manage dynamic range by reducing audio signals that fall below a specific volume, effectively eliminating background hiss and preventing distortion. This guide provides a step-by-step walkthrough on how to apply the Audio Expander to your timeline, locate the threshold parameters in the Effect Stack, and fine-tune the settings to achieve balanced, clean audio.

Step 1: Add the Audio Expander Effect

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and place your audio or video clip on the timeline.
  2. Go to the Effects panel (usually located in the bottom-left or tabbed alongside the Project Bin).
  3. Search for Expander in the search bar under the Audio Effects category.
  4. Drag and drop the Expander (or Audio Expander) effect directly onto your target audio clip or track.

Step 2: Access the Effect Stack

  1. Click on the audio clip in the timeline to select it.
  2. Open the Properties / Effect Stack panel (typically found on the right or middle workspace window).
  3. Confirm that the Expander effect is active and visible in the stack list.

Step 3: Adjust the Threshold Setting

The threshold determines the decibel (dB) level at which the expander starts attenuating the signal:

  1. Locate the Threshold slider (often measured in decibels from 0 dB down to -60 dB or lower).
  2. Lower the Threshold (e.g., -40 dB to -50 dB): Use this if you only want very quiet background noise (like room tone or fan noise) to be cut off.
  3. Raise the Threshold (e.g., -20 dB to -30 dB): Use this if you want a more aggressive reduction to isolate louder signals, such as direct speech.
  4. Move the slider left or right, or double-click the numerical value to type an exact decibel value.

Step 4: Fine-Tune the Complementary Parameters

To ensure the threshold transition sounds natural without abruptly clipping off the tail ends of words:

  • Ratio: Adjust the expansion ratio (e.g., 2:1 or 4:1). A higher ratio creates a sharper drop-off below the threshold, acting closer to a noise gate.
  • Attack Time: Set how quickly the expander opens when the sound exceeds the threshold. A fast attack (between 1 ms and 10 ms) prevents the start of words from getting cut off.
  • Release / Decay Time: Set how quickly the expander closes after the signal falls back below the threshold. A moderate release (around 100 ms to 300 ms) prevents jarring "pumping" artifacts.

Step 5: Monitor the Audio Output

  1. Open the Audio Mixer window (View > Audio Mixer).
  2. Play back the timeline over the quiet and loud sections of your track.
  3. Watch the VU meters to ensure the audio does not clip into the red zone (above 0 dB) and that background silence remains smooth. Readjust the Threshold slider until the cutoff point sounds seamless.
