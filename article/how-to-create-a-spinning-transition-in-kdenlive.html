How to Create a Spinning Transition in Kdenlive

Creating a dynamic spinning transition in Kdenlive is an excellent way to add energy and professional flair to your video edits. This article provides a direct, step-by-step guide on how to use the "Rotate" (also known as "Sovev" in some language localizations) effect combined with keyframes to seamlessly spin one video clip into another. You will learn how to apply the effect to adjacent clips, configure the keyframes for a continuous spinning motion, and ensure a smooth visual flow.

Step 1: Prepare Your Clips on the Timeline

  1. Import your footage into Kdenlive and drag the two clips you want to transition between onto your timeline.
  2. Place them adjacent to one another on the same track, creating a clean cut between Clip A (the outgoing clip) and Clip B (the incoming clip).

Step 2: Apply the Rotate Effect

  1. Go to the Effects tab (usually located in the bottom-left or top-left panel).
  2. In the search bar, type Rotate (or Sovev if you are using the Hebrew localization interface).
  3. Drag and drop the Rotate (Keyframable) effect onto both Clip A and Clip B.

Step 3: Configure the Spin Out for Clip A

  1. Select Clip A on the timeline to open its properties in the Properties/Effect Stack panel.
  2. Move the timeline playhead to about 10 to 15 frames before the end of Clip A.
  3. In the Effect Stack, click the clock/stopwatch icon next to the rotation parameters to enable keyframes.
  4. Add a keyframe at this position. Leave the rotation value at 0 (or the default starting position). This marks the beginning of the spin.
  5. Move the playhead to the very last frame of Clip A.
  6. Add a second keyframe and change the rotation value to 1800 (which represents 180 degrees in Kdenlive's rotation scale) or 3600 (for a full 360-degree spin) depending on how fast you want the spin to be.

Step 4: Configure the Spin In for Clip B

  1. Select Clip B on the timeline.
  2. Move the timeline playhead to the very first frame of Clip B.
  3. In the Effect Stack for Clip B, enable keyframes for the Rotate effect.
  4. Add a keyframe at this first frame. Set the rotation value to the negative equivalent of your Clip A ending value (e.g., -1800 or -3600). This ensures the clip enters from the same direction the previous clip was spinning.
  5. Move the playhead 10 to 15 frames into Clip B.
  6. Add a final keyframe and reset the rotation value back to 0.

Step 5: Smooth the Motion

To make the transition look natural rather than robotic, you need to adjust the keyframe interpolation:

  1. In the keyframe ruler of the Effect Stack, right-click on your keyframes.
  2. Change the keyframe interpolation type from Linear to Smooth (or Centripetal) for both clips. This creates an "ease-in" and "ease-out" effect, making the acceleration and deceleration of the spin look professional.
