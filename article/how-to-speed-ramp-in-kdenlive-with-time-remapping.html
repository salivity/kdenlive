How to Speed Ramp in Kdenlive with Time Remapping

Speed ramping is a dynamic video editing technique that seamlessly transitions footage between fast motion, slow motion, and real-time playback. In Kdenlive, this effect is achieved using the built-in Time Remapping tool, which allows you to place keyframes, adjust playback speed across specific segments, and smooth out acceleration using curve interpolation. This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step method for applying and smoothing speed ramps in your Kdenlive projects.

Step 1: Add the Clip to the Timeline

  1. Import your high-frame-rate footage (e.g., 60fps or 120fps for optimal slow-motion quality) into the Project Bin.
  2. Drag and drop the video clip onto a video track in the timeline.

Step 2: Enable the Time Remapping Tool

  1. Right-click the video clip in the timeline.
  2. Select Change Speed or navigate to Add Effect > Motion > Time Remapping (depending on your Kdenlive version, right-clicking the clip and choosing Time Remapping is the quickest shortcut).
  3. The Time Remapping interface will open in the clip properties or effect stack window.

Step 3: Set Keyframes for the Speed Change

  1. Move the playhead in the Time Remapping window to the point where you want the speed change to begin and click the Add Keyframe button.
  2. Move the playhead to the point where the speed ramp should end and add a second keyframe.
  3. For a complete ramp (e.g., normal speed to slow motion and back to normal), set additional keyframes defining the end of the slow-motion section and the return to normal speed.

Step 4: Adjust Speed and Timing

  1. In the Time Remapping graph, drag keyframe points horizontally or vertically to compress or stretch time.
    • Dragging keyframe points closer together horizontally speeds up the footage between them.
    • Spreading keyframe points farther apart stretches time, creating slow motion.
  2. Alternatively, select a keyframe segment and enter a specific speed percentage (e.g., 200% for fast motion, 40% for slow motion).

Step 5: Smooth the Speed Transitions (Ramping)

  1. By default, keyframe transitions may be linear, resulting in abrupt speed shifts.
  2. Select a keyframe on the timeline graph.
  3. Change the interpolation type from Linear to Smooth (or Bezier).
  4. Use the Bezier curve handles on each keyframe to create a smooth, curved line representing the change in velocity. A gradual S-curve ensures that the video accelerates and decelerates naturally without jarring stops.

Step 6: Handle Audio Playback

  1. Extreme speed changes can distort audio tracks.
  2. Unlink or mute the original clip audio in the timeline if necessary, and use sound effects (such as whooshes, risers, or pitch-shifted ambient noise) underneath the ramped section to enhance the transition effect.

Step 7: Preview and Render

  1. Set an In/Out zone around the modified clip on the timeline.
  2. Use Timeline Preview Rendering (Shift + R) to render a real-time preview of the speed ramp.
  3. Fine-tune the Bezier handles until the acceleration and deceleration feel completely natural.
