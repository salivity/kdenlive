Isolate Luma Range with Kdenlive Lift Gain Gamma

This article provides a step-by-step guide on how to isolate and adjust specific luminance (luma) ranges in Kdenlive using the "Lift/Gamma/Gain" color correction tool. By understanding how these three parameters target different exposure levels, you can precisely color grade and manipulate the shadows, midtones, and highlights of your video footage.

Step 1: Apply the Lift/Gamma/Gain Effect

To begin, import your video clip into the Kdenlive timeline. Go to the Effects tab, search for "Lift/Gamma/Gain" (often categorized under Color and Image Correction), and drag the effect onto your clip. This tool provides three color wheels and corresponding sliders representing different luminance ranges.

Step 2: Understand the Luma Ranges

The "Lift/Gamma/Gain" tool isolates the luma ranges of your footage into three distinct bands:

  • Lift (Shadows): Controls the darkest areas of the image. Adjusting Lift isolates and alters the black levels and deep shadows without significantly affecting the highlights.
  • Gamma (Midtones): Targets the middle exposure values, which usually contain skin tones and the bulk of the image detail.
  • Gain (Highlights): Controls the brightest areas of the image. Adjusting Gain isolates the white levels and bright highlights.

Step 3: Monitor the Isolation with Video Scopes

To accurately isolate these ranges, you must use Kdenlive's analytical tools. Go to View > Creative Headers and enable the Waveform and RGB Parade monitors. These scopes display the brightness levels of your clip from 0 (pure black/floor) to 100 (pure white/ceiling). Watching the scopes as you adjust the Lift, Gamma, and Gain controls allows you to see exactly which luma range is being modified.

Step 4: Adjust the Targeted Ranges

With your scopes visible, you can now isolate your adjustments to specific ranges:

  • To modify only the shadows, adjust the Lift wheel or slider. Moving it down darkens the shadows, while moving the color wheel tints only the dark areas.
  • To adjust the midtones, tweak the Gamma controls. This shifts the overall exposure and contrast of the image center.
  • To adjust the highlights, manipulate the Gain controls. This allows you to pull down overexposed areas or push the whites higher.

Step 5: Complete Advanced Isolation with Masking (Optional)

If you want to completely isolate a luma range so that other ranges become transparent or black, combine the Lift/Gamma/Gain effect with a Luma Key or Secondary Color Corrector effect. Apply the Luma Key first to filter out unwanted brightness levels, then use Lift/Gamma/Gain to color grade the remaining, isolated luma range.
