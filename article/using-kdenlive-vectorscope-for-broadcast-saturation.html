Using Kdenlive Vectorscope for Broadcast Saturation

This article explains the function of the Vectorscope monitor in Kdenlive when configuring legal broadcast saturation limits. It covers how the scope visually represents chrominance data, the significance of broadcast target graticules, and how video editors use this tool to prevent illegal color clipping and ensure regulatory compliance for television standards.

What the Vectorscope Measures

The Vectorscope is a specialized color monitor that plots chrominance (color information) on a circular graph, completely independent of luminance (brightness). The angle around the circle indicates the hue (the specific color, such as red, yellow, green, cyan, blue, or magenta), while the distance from the center outward represents the saturation (color intensity). A completely desaturated, monochrome pixel rests directly in the center, while vivid, highly saturated colors stretch toward the outer edges.

Identifying Legal Broadcast Boundaries

Television broadcast standards (such as SMPTE and EBU) mandate strict limits on color saturation to prevent signal degradation, visual distortion, and technical transmission errors. The Vectorscope provides standardized target boxes on its graticule, typically indicating 75% and 100% saturation limits for the primary and secondary colors:

  • 75% Targets: The standard legal threshold for most broadcast delivery environments. Keeping color traces within these boxes ensures colors will not bleed or cause illegal gamut errors.
  • 100% Targets: The absolute ceiling for digital color space. Any trace extending beyond these boundaries indicates over-saturation that will be clipped or rejected by automated quality control (QC) broadcast checks.

Preventing Gamut Clipping and Signal Violations

If a video contains colors that push past standard broadcast limits—common with vibrant graphics, stage lighting, or neon signs—the Vectorscope immediately displays these spikes extending beyond the safe graticule targets. Broadcast transmitters and consumer television sets cannot reliably render these out-of-gamut values, resulting in color smearing, audio buzz in legacy analog systems, and crushed digital color detail.

Workflow in Kdenlive

When grading video in Kdenlive to meet broadcast compliance, the Vectorscope acts as the real-time reference guide:

  1. Monitor the Trace: Open the Vectorscope monitor (View > Vectorscope) while playing or scrubbing through the timeline.
  2. Detect Outliers: Identify if any part of the color cloud crosses the designated 75% or 100% saturation markers.
  3. Apply Corrections: Apply color grading tools—such as the Saturation, Color Curves, or Lift, Gamma, Gain effects—to pull excessive peaks back within safe target boxes.
  4. Final Verification: Ensure that the entire color distribution remains enclosed within legal bounds across the entire timeline duration.
