Apply a LUT to a Single Track in Kdenlive

Applying a Look-Up Table (LUT) to an individual timeline track in Kdenlive allows you to color grade an entire sequence of clips simultaneously without affecting other tracks or altering the master project output. This non-destructive workflow ensures that your source media remains untouched while giving you the flexibility to toggle, adjust, or remove color transformations at any point during your edit.

Step 1: Select the Target Track Header

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive with your footage placed on the timeline.
  2. Locate the track header on the left side of the timeline corresponding to the video track you want to grade (for example, V1 or V2).
  3. Click on the track header to highlight and select it. A selected track typically displays an outline or a highlighted title area to indicate it is active.

Step 2: Add the Apply LUT Effect to the Track

  1. Navigate to the Effects tab (typically located in the upper-left panel area alongside the Project Bin).
  2. Use the search bar to search for Apply LUT or locate it under the Color / Image adjustment category.
  3. Drag the Apply LUT effect from the Effects panel and drop it directly onto the header of your chosen track. Alternatively, double-click the effect while the track header is selected.

Step 3: Load Your LUT File

  1. Open the Effect/Composition Stack panel (found under View > Effect/Composition Stack if it is not currently visible).
  2. Ensure the track header is still selected so the panel displays track-level effects rather than clip-level effects.
  3. In the Apply LUT settings, click the file browser icon next to the LUT file input.
  4. Browse your local storage and select your .cube, .3dl, or supported LUT file, then click Open.

Step 4: Adjust and Manage the Effect Non-Destructively

Once loaded, the LUT applies instantly to all current and future clips placed on that specific track.

  • Disable/Enable: Click the eye icon next to the Apply LUT effect in the Effect Stack to preview your footage with and without the grade.
  • Interpolation: Choose between Nearest, Trilinear, or Tetrahedral interpolation methods depending on the precision and performance balance your system requires.
  • Master Isolation: Because the effect sits strictly on the track level, graphics, titles, adjustment layers, or secondary camera angles on separate video tracks will remain completely unaffected by this color profile.
