How to Use Kdenlive RGB Adjustment to Boost Colors

This article provides a step-by-step guide on how to use the "RGB Adjustment" filter in Kdenlive to isolate and boost specific primary colors in your video footage. You will learn how to locate the filter, manipulate individual red, green, and blue color channels, and apply practical color-grading techniques to make specific hues stand out in your projects.

Step 1: Add your Footage and Apply the Filter

To begin, import your video clip into Kdenlive and drag it onto your timeline.

  1. Navigate to the Effects tab (typically located in the bottom-left or top-left panel of the default workspace).
  2. In the search bar of the Effects panel, type RGB Adjustment.
  3. Drag and drop the RGB Adjustment effect directly onto the video clip in your timeline.

Step 2: Understand the Effect Controls

Once the effect is applied, select your video clip to open its properties in the Effect/Composition Stack panel. You will see three primary sliders:

  • Red: Controls the intensity of the red channel.
  • Green: Controls the intensity of the green channel.
  • Blue: Controls the intensity of the blue channel.

These sliders allow you to scale the input of each color channel individually, shifting the overall color balance of the video.

Step 3: Isolate a Primary Color

To isolate a specific primary color, you must decrease the influence of the other two colors.

For example, to isolate Red:

  1. Locate the Green and Blue sliders in the RGB Adjustment effect stack.
  2. Drag both the Green and Blue sliders down toward zero.
  3. As you lower these values, the green and blue wavelengths are filtered out, leaving only the red tones visible in your video preview.

Apply the same logic for other colors: to isolate Green, lower Red and Blue; to isolate Blue, lower Red and Green.

Step 4: Boost the Isolated Color

Once you have isolated your chosen color, you can boost its presence to make it more vibrant.

  1. Locate the slider of the isolated color (e.g., Red).
  2. Drag the slider to the right to increase its value beyond the default 100% mark.
  3. Monitor the project monitor closely to ensure the color does not "clip" or blow out, which can cause a loss of detail in the highlights of your footage.

Step 5: Fine-Tune the Balance

Because complete isolation can sometimes result in an overly dark or monochromatic image, you can fine-tune the sliders to achieve a stylized look rather than absolute isolation.

  • Gently raise the sliders of the non-dominant colors (to about 10% to 20%) to reintroduce subtle details and contrast to the shadows and highlights.
  • Use the Keyframe icon next to the RGB Adjustment sliders if you want to animate the color boost over time, allowing a specific color to gradually saturate or fade throughout the duration of the clip.
