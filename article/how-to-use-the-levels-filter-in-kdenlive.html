How to Use the Levels Filter in Kdenlive

The Levels filter in Kdenlive is an essential color correction tool used to balance exposure, fix contrast issues, and correct tonal ranges across your footage. By manipulating input and output values for blacks, midtones (gamma), and highlights, you can transform flat or poorly exposed clips into punchy, professional-looking video.

Applying the Levels Filter

  1. Select the clip on your timeline that requires adjustment.
  2. Open the Effects tab (typically located in the bottom-left panel).
  3. Search for Levels under the Color and Image Correction category.
  4. Drag and drop the Levels effect directly onto your video clip or into the Effect/Composition Stack panel.

Key Controls in the Levels Filter

The Levels interface provides controls for both Input (interpreting source values) and Output (mapping resulting values) across the brightness spectrum:

1. Black Levels (Input Black / Minimum)

  • What it does: Sets the threshold for what is considered pure black (value 0).
  • How to adjust: Increase the Input Black value to darken the shadows and crush dark gray areas into deep black. This adds contrast to washed-out footage. Be careful not to raise it too high, or you will lose shadow detail.

2. Highlights (Input White / Maximum)

  • What it does: Sets the threshold for what is considered pure white (value 255).
  • How to adjust: Decrease the Input White slider to make the bright areas brighter and define the maximum white point. If an image is underexposed, lowering this value brings the brightest points closer to full white, adding brightness and clarity.

3. Midtones (Gamma)

  • What it does: Controls the overall brightness distribution between pure black and pure white without clipping either extreme.
  • How to adjust:
    • Increase the Gamma value to brighten midtones, lift facial features, and reveal detail in the middle exposure range.
    • Decrease the Gamma value to darken midtones, creating a moodier or higher-contrast look while leaving the deepest blacks and brightest whites intact.

4. Output Levels (Output Black / Output White)

  • Output Black: Raising this limits how dark the image can get, turning deep blacks into grays (ideal for a faded, vintage film look).
  • Output White: Lowering this limits the peak brightness, ensuring highlights never reach pure white (useful for legal broadcast limits).

Step-by-Step Adjustment Workflow

  1. Open Scopes: Enable the Vectorscope and RGB Parade or Histogram via View > Add Tool > Color Scopes to visually monitor changes accurately.
  2. Anchor the Blacks: Increase the Input Black value until the lowest points on your histogram/waveform sit just above the baseline (0).
  3. Set the Highlights: Decrease the Input White value until your brightest elements reach the top threshold without clipping past the ceiling (255 or 100%).
  4. Balance Midtones: Adjust the Gamma slider to achieve a natural skin tone and overall balanced scene exposure.
  5. Set Output Limits (Optional): If producing for broadcast or applying a stylistic fade, trim the Output Black and Output White sliders accordingly.
