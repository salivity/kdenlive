How to Use Phaser Audio Effect in Kdenlive

This article provides a step-by-step guide on how to apply and configure the Phaser audio effect in Kdenlive to create a dynamic, sweeping sound. You will learn where to find the effect, how to apply it to your audio tracks, and how to adjust its key parameters—such as speed, depth, and feedback—to achieve the classic "whoosh" or psychedelic sweeping transition in your video projects.

Step 1: Add Your Audio to the Timeline

Before applying any effects, import your audio or video clip into Kdenlive and drag it onto an audio track in the timeline. Ensure the track is selected so you can apply effects directly to it.

Step 2: Locate and Apply the Phaser Effect

  1. Open the Effects tab (usually located in the bottom-left or top-left panel of the default workspace). If you cannot see it, enable it via the top menu by selecting View > Effects.
  2. In the search bar of the Effects panel, type Phaser.
  3. Under the Audio or Modulation category, drag the Phaser effect and drop it directly onto your audio clip in the timeline. Alternatively, you can drag it to the entire audio track header if you want the effect applied to all clips on that track.

Step 3: Configure the Phaser Settings

Once applied, the Phaser parameters will appear in the Effect/Composition Stack panel. To get a distinct sweeping sound, adjust the following key sliders:

  • LFO Frequency (Speed): This controls how quickly the sweep moves up and down the frequency spectrum. Lower values (around 0.1 Hz to 1 Hz) create a slow, dramatic, rolling sweep. Higher values create a fast, vibrato-like effect.
  • Depth (Amount): This determines the range of the frequency sweep. Set this to a higher percentage (70% to 100%) to make the sweeping sound prominent and noticeable.
  • Feedback: This feeds a portion of the output signal back into the effect's input. Increasing feedback sharpens the peaks and valleys of the sweep, giving it a metallic, resonant, jet-like "whoosh" sound.
  • Stages: This defines the number of filters used. More stages create a more complex, richer sweeping sound, while fewer stages result in a subtler effect.
  • Dry/Wet Mix: This controls the balance between the original audio (Dry) and the processed audio (Wet). For the strongest sweeping effect, set this close to 50% to ensure maximum phase cancellation and reinforcement.

Step 4: Preview and Fine-Tune

Press the Spacebar to play the timeline and listen to the effect in real-time. Make micro-adjustments to the LFO Frequency and Feedback sliders while the audio plays until you achieve the exact sweeping intensity and speed required for your project.
