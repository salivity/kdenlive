How to Apply Reverb in Kdenlive

This article provides a straightforward, step-by-step guide on how to use the Reverb effect in Kdenlive to simulate different acoustic environments. You will learn how to locate the effect, apply it to your audio tracks, and adjust key parameters like room size and decay to make your audio sound like it was recorded in a small room, a large hall, or a spacious cavern.

Step 1: Locate the Reverb Effect

To begin, ensure your video or audio clip is already placed on the timeline.

  1. Navigate to the Effects tab (usually located in the bottom-left or top-left pane of the default workspace). If you cannot see it, enable it by going to View > Effects in the top menu.
  2. In the search bar of the Effects tab, type Reverb.
  3. Kdenlive offers a few variations depending on your operating system's installed audio plugins (such as LADSPA or LV2). Look for the standard Reverb or Room Reverb effect under the "Audio" category.

Step 2: Apply the Effect to Your Clip

Once you have found the effect, you need to apply it to your audio:

  1. Click and drag the Reverb effect from the list.
  2. Drop it directly onto the audio clip in your timeline.
  3. Alternatively, you can drag the effect onto an entire track header if you want the reverb to apply to every clip on that specific track.

Step 3: Adjust the Parameters

With the audio clip selected, look at the Properties (or Effect/Composition Stack) panel. Here, you will see several sliders that allow you to customize the acoustic environment:

  • Room Size: This slider determines the simulated size of the space. Lower values simulate small spaces like closets or offices, while higher values simulate large spaces like churches, concert halls, or stadiums.
  • Reverb Time (Decay): This controls how long the reverberation lasts before fading into silence. Larger rooms naturally have longer decay times.
  • Dampening: This adjusts how quickly high frequencies are absorbed. High dampening simulates soft surfaces (like carpets or curtains) that absorb sound, while low dampening simulates hard surfaces (like concrete or tile) that reflect sound sharply.
  • Dry/Wet Mix: This is the most crucial control for realism. Dry represents the original, unaffected audio, while Wet represents the reverb effect. For a subtle, realistic environment, keep the Dry signal high and mix in a lower percentage of the Wet signal. For an ethereal or distant effect, increase the Wet signal and decrease the Dry signal.

Step 4: Preview and Export

Play back your timeline to hear the effect in real-time. Because audio rendering can sometimes lag, you can make minor adjustments to the sliders during playback to fine-tune the sound. Once you are satisfied with the simulated environment, you can proceed to render your project as usual.
