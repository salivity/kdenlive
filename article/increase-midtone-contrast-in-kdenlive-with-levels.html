Increase Midtone Contrast in Kdenlive with Levels

This guide demonstrates how to restore dynamic range and increase midtone contrast on flat or Log video footage using Kdenlive. By applying the Levels effect to expand the luminance range and adjusting midtone distribution, you can transform washed-out, low-contrast video into a balanced, vibrant image without clipping your highlights or crushing your shadows.

Step 1: Open the Scopes for Accurate Monitoring

Before making any color or luminance adjustments, enable the video scopes to accurately measure your dynamic range:

  1. Go to View > Waveform or View > RGB Parade.
  2. Flat footage typically clusters in the middle of the waveform (between 20 IRE and 80 IRE) with empty space at the absolute black (0) and white (100 or 255) thresholds.

Step 2: Apply the Levels Effect

  1. Select the flat clip on your timeline.
  2. In the Effects tab, navigate to Color and Image Correction.
  3. Search for Levels and double-click or drag it onto your clip.

Step 3: Normalize the Black and White Points

To create room for midtone contrast, first stretch the overall luma range:

  1. Input Black Level (Shadows): Increase the Input Black Level slider until the bottom of your waveform just touches the 0 mark. Stop before the data compresses flat against the baseline to prevent shadow crushing.
  2. Input White Level (Highlights): Decrease the Input White Level slider until the top of the waveform reaches near the 255 / 100% mark. Ensure bright areas retain detail without hard clipping.

Step 4: Adjust Midtone Contrast Using Gamma

Once the full luma range is expanded, target the midtones:

  1. Locate the Gamma (or middle input) slider in the Levels effect.
  2. Flat footage often pushes skin tones and core scene details into a compressed, milky midtone range.
  3. Lowering the Gamma value deepens lower midtones, while slightly increasing it brightens the upper midtones.
  4. To achieve an effective midtone "S-curve" contrast response when using Levels in conjunction with curve tools, pair the expanded Levels baseline with the Bézier Curve or Color Curves effect set to the Luma channel. Place an anchor in the lower midtones (pull slightly down) and upper midtones (push slightly up) while keeping shadows and highlights pinned.

Step 5: Check and Refine

  1. Toggle the effect on and off using the eye icon in the Effect Stack to compare before and after.
  2. Verify in the Waveform monitor that skin tones fall appropriately into the 50–70 IRE range.
  3. Fine-tune the Input Black, Input White, and Gamma controls until midtone elements like faces, foliage, and textures pop with natural separation and clarity.
