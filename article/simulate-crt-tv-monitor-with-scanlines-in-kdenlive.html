Simulate CRT TV Monitor with Scanlines in Kdenlive

This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step tutorial on how to use the "Scanlines" video effect in Kdenlive to recreate the nostalgic look of an old CRT television monitor. You will learn how to apply the effect, adjust its parameters for realism, and combine it with other built-in Kdenlive tools to enhance the retro aesthetic.

Step 1: Add Your Footage to the Timeline

Open Kdenlive and import the video clip you want to transform. Drag and drop the footage from your Project Bin onto the video track in your timeline.

Step 2: Apply the Scanlines Effect

  1. Navigate to the Effects tab (usually located in the bottom-left or center-left panel).
  2. In the search bar of the Effects panel, type Scanlines.
  3. Drag the Scanlines effect from the list and drop it directly onto your video clip in the timeline.

Step 3: Adjust the Scanline Settings

Once the effect is applied, select the video clip to open the Effect Stack panel. Tweak the parameters to match the resolution of your video:

  • Line Spacing / Frequency: Adjust this setting to control how close the horizontal lines are to one another. For a standard CRT look, the lines should be thin and closely packed.
  • Thickness: Reduce the thickness so the scanlines do not completely block out your footage. They should act as a subtle overlay.
  • Opacity / Alpha: Lower the opacity of the scanlines so the original video shines through naturally. The lines should be visible but not overpowering.

Step 4: Add Lens Distortion for the CRT Curve

Old CRT monitors had curved, bulbous glass screens. To simulate this curvature:

  1. Search for the Lens Correction effect in the Effects panel.
  2. Drag and drop it onto your clip. Ensure it sits below the Scanlines effect in your stack.
  3. Adjust the Distortion or Correction parameters slightly into negative values to create a subtle "barrel distortion" that bulges the center of the image.

Step 5: Enhance the Retro Aesthetic

To complete the analog television look, apply these additional effects from the Kdenlive library:

  • Vignette: Add the Vignette effect to darken the outer corners of the frame, mimicking the natural light falloff of older screens.
  • Blur: Apply a very mild Box Blur (around 1 or 2 pixels) to soften the sharp digital edges, simulating the low-resolution nature of analog signals.
  • RGB Shift: Apply the RGB Shift effect to slightly separate the color channels, replicating the chromatic aberration and color bleeding common to vintage television sets.
