How to Adjust Audio Gate Threshold in Kdenlive

This guide explains how to configure and adjust the gain threshold on the Audio Gate effect in Kdenlive to remove unwanted background noise and isolate clean dialogue. By understanding how the threshold interacts with your audio's dynamic range, you will learn how to silence ambient room noise during pauses in speech without clipping natural vocal delivery.

Step 1: Add the Audio Gate Effect

  1. Select the audio clip or track containing your dialogue on the timeline.
  2. Open the Effects tab (if not visible, enable it via View > Effects).
  3. Search for Audio Gate or Gate in the audio effects category.
  4. Drag and drop the effect directly onto your audio clip or track.

Step 2: Identify Your Audio Levels

Before adjusting the threshold, determine the decibel (dB) levels of your speech versus the background noise:

  1. Open the Audio Spectrum or monitor the master audio meters during playback.
  2. Play a section of the clip where there is only silence or background noise (the "noise floor") and note the dB level (e.g., -45 dB to -40 dB).
  3. Play a section where the person is speaking normally and note the lowest level of their voice (e.g., -20 dB to -15 dB).

Step 3: Set the Gain Threshold

The threshold setting dictates the volume level below which the gate closes (mutes the audio) and above which the gate opens (allows audio to pass through).

  1. Go to the Effect/Composition Stack panel to view the Audio Gate controls.
  2. Locate the Threshold (or Gate / Gain Threshold) parameter.
  3. Set the threshold value slightly above your measured noise floor, but below the quietest parts of your dialogue.
    • Example: If your background noise sits at -42 dB and the quietest vocal sounds register at -25 dB, a threshold between -35 dB and -30 dB provides a reliable starting point.
  4. Play back the track. Ambient room hum, hiss, or keyboard clicks during pauses should now be muted.

Step 4: Fine-Tune to Prevent Choppy Dialogue

If the speech sounds cut off at the beginning or end of words, adjust the secondary gate parameters:

  • Attack: Controls how quickly the gate opens when speech begins. Keep this low (between 1 ms and 10 ms) to avoid cutting off initial consonant sounds.
  • Hold: Keeps the gate open for a set duration after the volume drops below the threshold. Increase this slightly (50 ms to 100 ms) to prevent the gate from closing between brief pauses in sentences.
  • Release (Decay): Determines how smoothly the gate fades to silence. Set this between 100 ms and 250 ms so the ends of words fade naturally rather than abruptly cutting out.

Step 5: Test and Verify

Listen to the audio through headphones. If words sound robotic or clipped, lower the threshold or increase the release time. If background noise still bleeds through during pauses, raise the threshold in small increments until only the spoken dialogue is audible.
