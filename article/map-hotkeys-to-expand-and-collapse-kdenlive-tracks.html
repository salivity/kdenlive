Map Hotkeys to Expand and Collapse Kdenlive Tracks

In Kdenlive, customizing keyboard shortcuts for expanding and collapsing timeline tracks streamlines your editing workflow and significantly speeds up timeline navigation. This guide explains how to access the shortcut configuration menu in Kdenlive, locate track height commands, and assign custom keybindings to instantly minimize or expand your audio and video tracks.

Step 1: Open the Shortcut Configuration Menu

  1. Launch Kdenlive.
  2. Navigate to the top menu bar and click Settings.
  3. Select Configure Keyboard Shortcuts... (or press the default shortcut Ctrl + Alt + , depending on your version and OS).

Step 2: Locate Track Expansion Commands

Inside the shortcut configuration dialog:

  1. Use the Search bar at the top of the window to filter commands.
  2. Type Track or Expand to view track-related options.
  3. Look for the following actions:
    • Expand All Tracks: Expands all audio and video tracks to full view.
    • Collapse All Tracks: Minimizes all tracks to compact header views.
    • Toggle Track Minimized: Toggles the height of the currently selected track.
    • Make Track Taller / Make Track Shorter: Incrementally adjusts track heights.

Step 3: Assign Custom Hotkeys

  1. Click on the action you want to modify (e.g., Expand All Tracks).
  2. Select the Custom radio button underneath the action list.
  3. Click the button next to "Custom" (it will display None or the current key combination).
  4. Press the hotkey combination on your keyboard that you wish to assign (e.g., Alt + Down to collapse, Alt + Up to expand).
  5. If a conflict warning appears, choose whether to overwrite the existing shortcut or select a different key combination.
  6. Repeat the process for Collapse All Tracks or any other track height shortcuts.

Step 4: Save and Test

  1. Click OK or Apply at the bottom of the dialog box to save your changes.
  2. Return to the timeline and press your newly assigned hotkeys to verify that tracks expand and collapse instantly.
