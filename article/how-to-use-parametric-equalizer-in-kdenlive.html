How to Use Parametric Equalizer in Kdenlive

Applying a parametric equalizer with adjustable Q-factors in Kdenlive allows you to sculpt your audio by boosting or cutting specific frequency ranges with precise bandwidth control. This guide walks you through locating the parametric equalizer effect, applying it to your timeline audio, and dialing in the frequency, gain, and Q-factor settings to clean up vocals or balance your mix.

Step 1: Select Your Audio Clip

Open your Kdenlive project and place your media on the timeline. Click directly on the audio clip—or the video clip containing the embedded audio track—that requires equalization.

Step 2: Locate the Parametric Equalizer Effect

  1. Open the Effects panel (go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  2. Click the Audio tab or use the search bar at the top of the Effects panel.
  3. Search for Equalizer or Parametric Equalizer (often listed as 3-Band Parametric Equalizer or the FFmpeg Equalizer filter depending on your Kdenlive version).
  4. Drag and drop the effect onto your selected clip on the timeline, or double-click the effect while the clip is selected.

Step 3: Configure Frequency, Gain, and Q-Factor

Once added, go to the Properties / Effect Stack panel to adjust the equalizer parameters:

  • Frequency (Hz): Set the target center frequency you want to modify (e.g., 80 Hz for low rumble, 1 kHz for mid-range speech, or 8 kHz for high-end sparkle).
  • Gain (dB): Increase (boost) or decrease (cut) the volume level of the selected frequency range.
  • Q-Factor (Width/Bandwidth): Adjust the Q-factor slider to determine how narrow or wide the filter's influence is around the center frequency:
    • High Q (Narrow): Targets a very specific frequency with minimal impact on neighboring frequencies. Ideal for removing narrow-band hums, whistles, or resonance.
    • Low Q (Wide): Affects a broad range of frequencies surrounding your target. Ideal for natural-sounding tone adjustments and broad tonal balance.

Step 4: Preview and Fine-Tune

Play the timeline to monitor the changes in real time. Use the effect's bypass/enable checkbox in the Effect Stack panel to compare the equalized audio against the original track. Make subtle adjustments to the Q-factor and Gain to ensure the audio remains clean and free of distortion or unwanted phase artifacts.
