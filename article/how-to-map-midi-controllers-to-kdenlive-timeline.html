How to Map MIDI Controllers to Kdenlive Timeline

This guide explains how to connect and map a MIDI control surface to timeline navigation and editing controls in Kdenlive. By configuring your MIDI hardware through Kdenlive's built-in controller settings, you can assign physical knobs, sliders, and buttons to functions like timeline scrubbing, playback, zooming, and cutting clips for a more tactile and efficient editing workflow.

Step 1: Connect and Verify Your MIDI Device

  1. Connect your MIDI controller to your computer via USB before launching Kdenlive.
  2. Ensure your operating system recognizes the device as an active MIDI input.
  3. Open Kdenlive.

Step 2: Access Controller Settings in Kdenlive

  1. Go to the top menu and select Settings > Configure Kdenlive... (or Kdenlive > Preferences on macOS).
  2. In the left sidebar of the configuration window, select Media Controller (or JogShuttle in older versions).

Step 3: Enable and Select Your Controller

  1. Check the box labeled Enable Device or Enable Media Controller.
  2. Select your device type: choose MIDI or select your specific device from the device dropdown list if it is automatically detected.
  3. Ensure the MIDI input port corresponds to your connected hardware.

Step 4: Map MIDI Controls to Timeline Actions

  1. Select the action table within the controller settings window.
  2. Choose a timeline function you want to map (such as Play/Pause, Shuttle Left/Right, Jog Forward/Backward, Zoom In/Out, or Cut Clip).
  3. Click the Learn button next to the desired command, then press the corresponding physical button or turn the designated knob/jog wheel on your MIDI controller. Kdenlive will register the incoming MIDI Control Change (CC) or Note data.
  4. Alternatively, manually assign the specific MIDI channel, Control number, or Note value if you already know your hardware's output profile.

Step 5: Adjust Jog and Shuttle Sensitivity

  1. If you are mapping an endless rotary encoder or a jog wheel for timeline scrubbing, adjust the Sensitivity and Deadzone sliders in the controller options.
  2. Lower sensitivity provides frame-by-frame precision, while higher sensitivity enables rapid scanning across long timelines.

Step 6: Apply and Test

  1. Click Apply, then click OK to close the configuration window.
  2. Open a project with video tracks on the timeline to test the response of your knobs, buttons, and faders.
  3. If a control behaves unexpectedly (e.g., inverted scrubbing or rapid skipping), return to the Media Controller settings to reverse the direction or recalibrate the sensitivity values.
