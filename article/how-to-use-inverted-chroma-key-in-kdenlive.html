How to Use Inverted Chroma Key in Kdenlive

This guide explains how to apply an inverted chroma key in Kdenlive to isolate specific colored elements and remove the surrounding background. By modifying the standard chroma keying workflow, you can flip the matte selection so that the chosen color is preserved while the rest of the frame becomes transparent.

Step 1: Add Your Media to the Timeline

  1. Import your target video clip and the background you want to place underneath it into the Project Bin.
  2. Place the clip you want to key out onto an upper video track (e.g., Video 2).
  3. Place your new background clip or image directly beneath it on a lower video track (e.g., Video 1).

Step 2: Apply the Chroma Key Effect

  1. Select the clip on the upper track.
  2. Open the Effects panel and search for Chroma Key: Advanced (recommended for better control) or Chroma Key: Basic.
  3. Drag and drop the effect onto the clip in the timeline, or double-click the effect with the clip selected.

Step 3: Sample the Color

  1. In the Effect/Composition Stack panel, locate the color picker (eyedropper tool) under the applied Chroma Key effect.
  2. Click the eyedropper and click on the color in the Clip Monitor that you want to target.

Step 4: Invert the Selection

Standard chroma keying removes the selected color and keeps the rest of the image. To achieve an inverted key:

  1. In the effect settings, look for the Invert or Invert Selection checkbox.
  2. Check the box to flip the alpha matte. The targeted color will now remain visible, while all other colors become transparent, exposing the lower video track.

Alternative method using Alpha Shapes / Alpha Operations: If you are using an effect like Color Selection or basic keyers that lack a direct invert toggle, search for the Alpha Operations effect in the Effects panel, add it directly below the chroma key effect in the stack, and set the Operation dropdown to Invert.

Step 5: Fine-Tune the Key

  1. Adjust the Variance (or Delta/Tolerance) sliders to expand or contract the range of shades included in the key.
  2. Adjust the Edge Softness or Feathering sliders to remove harsh pixelation around the borders of the isolated subject.
  3. If necessary, apply a Key Spill Mop-up effect to eliminate any remaining color fringing around the edges of the retained element.
