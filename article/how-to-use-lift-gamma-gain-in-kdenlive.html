How to Use Lift Gamma Gain in Kdenlive

Adjusting the color balance using the Lift, Gamma, Gain wheels in Kdenlive is a fundamental technique for color correction and grading. This guide explains how to apply the 3-way color balance effect, understand what each wheel controls, and follow an efficient workflow to correct color casts, set proper contrast, and achieve a professional cinematic look in your video projects.

Applying the Effect

  1. Select your target video clip on the Kdenlive timeline.
  2. Open the Effects tab (if not visible, enable it via View > Effects).
  3. Search for Lift, Gamma, Gain in the search bar.
  4. Drag and drop the effect onto your clip or double-click it to apply.

Understanding the Three-Way Color Wheels

The Lift, Gamma, Gain tool divides your image into three distinct tonal ranges:

  • Lift (Shadows): Controls the darkest areas of the image. Moving the center point alters the color cast of the shadows (e.g., adding cool blues or warm tones to dark areas). The associated slider or vertical value adjusts the black levels (pedestal).
  • Gamma (Midtones): Controls the middle tonal range, including skin tones and general exposure. Adjusting this wheel shifts the overall color temperature and tint of your scene without severely clipping shadows or blowing out highlights.
  • Gain (Highlights): Affects the brightest parts of the image, such as skies, direct light sources, and bright objects. Adjusting this wheel colors the highlights and controls the maximum white point.

Step-by-Step Adjustment Workflow

1. Enable Monitoring Scopes

Before making changes by eye, open Kdenlive's built-in scopes by navigating to View > Vectorscope and View > RGB Parade. The RGB Parade helps you see whether red, green, and blue channels are evenly balanced across shadow, midtone, and highlight regions.

2. Balance Exposure (Luminance)

  • Lower or raise the Lift level until shadows are sitting just above zero on the waveform monitor to prevent crushed blacks.
  • Adjust the Gain level so highlights reach an optimal level without clipping past 100%.
  • Adjust the Gamma slider to balance the overall scene brightness to a natural exposure.

3. Correct the Color Casts

  • Neutralize Shadows (Lift): If dark areas have an unwanted tint (for example, a green or blue cast), drag the center controller of the Lift wheel in the opposite direction on the color circle to neutralize it.
  • Fix Highlights (Gain): Look at a white or bright object in your shot. If it leans toward yellow or orange, pull the Gain wheel toward cyan or blue until the highlights read as pure white.
  • Refine Skin Tones and Mood (Gamma): Use the Gamma wheel to fine-tune skin tones. Drag the center point toward warm or cool tones to finalize the overall mood and temperature of the shot.

4. Creative Grading

Once the footage is properly balanced and neutralized, you can use the wheels for stylistic grading. A common look is the "teal and orange" grade, achieved by pushing the Lift slightly toward cyan/teal and the Gamma or Gain toward orange/warm tones.
