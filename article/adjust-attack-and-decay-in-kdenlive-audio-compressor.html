Adjust Attack and Decay in Kdenlive Audio Compressor

This guide explains how to configure the attack and decay settings on the Audio Compressor effect in Kdenlive. By adjusting these dynamic parameters, you can control how quickly the compressor responds to volume peaks and how smoothly it releases the gain reduction, resulting in a balanced and professional audio mix for your video projects.

Step 1: Apply the Audio Compressor Effect

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and select the audio clip on your timeline that requires dynamic range management.
  2. Go to the Effects panel (usually located in the bottom-left or tabbed alongside the Project Bin).
  3. Search for Compressor or Audio Compressor in the audio effects category.
  4. Drag and drop the effect directly onto your target audio clip or track.

Step 2: Access the Effect Settings

  1. Select the clip on the timeline to open the Properties / Effect Stack panel.
  2. Ensure the Audio Compressor effect is visible and active in the stack.

Step 3: Adjust the Attack Time

The Attack parameter controls the time (in milliseconds) it takes for the compressor to start attenuating the signal once the audio level exceeds the set threshold.

  • Locate the Attack Slider: Find the Attack property within the compressor controls.
  • Fast Attack (1 ms – 20 ms): Use this to clamp down immediately on sudden, sharp transients, such as loud drum hits or sudden percussive noises.
  • Slow Attack (20 ms – 100+ ms): Use this to allow initial transients through for a more natural, dynamic sound on spoken dialogue and vocals.
  • How to change: Click and drag the slider, or double-click the numerical value box to type a specific millisecond value directly.

Step 4: Adjust the Decay (Release) Time

The Decay (often referred to as Release in audio terminology) controls how long it takes for the compressor to stop reducing gain and return the audio to its normal level after the volume drops below the threshold.

  • Locate the Decay Slider: Find the Decay property in the effect options.
  • Fast Decay (50 ms – 100 ms): Restores original volume quickly. If set too fast, it can create an unnatural "pumping" or "breathing" artifact in the audio.
  • Slow Decay (100 ms – 500+ ms): Creates a smoother, transparent transition, which works well for spoken narration, podcasts, and background music.
  • How to change: Drag the slider or type the desired millisecond value into the input field.

Step 5: Test and Fine-Tune

Play back your timeline while monitoring the master audio meters to ensure the audio levels remain consistent without audible distortion or pumping. Adjust the Threshold and Ratio sliders in conjunction with the attack and decay times to achieve optimal results.
