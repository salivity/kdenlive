Kdenlive Time Remapping for Reverse Playback Ramps

This article provides a comprehensive overview of how Kdenlive's Time Remapping curve editor functions when creating reverse playback ramps. It explains the mechanics of the time-versus-time graph, the role of negative curve slopes in reversing footage, and how Bézier curve handles enable seamless, dynamic speed transitions from forward to backward motion without cutting the clip.

Understanding the Time Remapping Graph

The Time Remapping tool in Kdenlive operates on a two-dimensional coordinate system that maps the clip's source time against the project's timeline (output time).

  • Horizontal Axis (X-axis): Represents the progression of time on your sequence timeline.
  • Vertical Axis (Y-axis): Represents the original frame position within the source video clip.

In standard playback, the graph displays a straight line sloping upward from left to right at a 45-degree angle, indicating a 1:1 forward playback speed.

Creating Reverse Playback with Negative Slopes

The primary function of the curve editor in generating reverse playback is altering the direction of the slope:

  • Positive Slope: Moving from lower-left to upper-right results in normal forward playback.
  • Horizontal Line: A flat line means timeline time is passing, but source frames are not advancing, creating a freeze-frame effect.
  • Negative Slope: Pulling a keyframe downward so the curve slopes from upper-left to lower-right forces the source frames to play in reverse as the timeline moves forward.

The steepness of the downward slope dictates the speed of the reverse action. A steep drop produces high-speed reverse playback, while a gradual downward slope results in slow-motion reverse.

Creating Smooth Speed Ramps with Curve Handles

A hard reverse transition can look jarring. The curve editor resolves this by allowing users to change linear keyframes into smooth Bézier curve points.

  1. Deceleration into Reverse: By adjusting the tangent handles on a keyframe before the reverse section, the positive slope gradually flattens (slowing the forward motion to a stop) before dipping downward into reverse.
  2. Acceleration out of Reverse: Adjusting the tangent handles at the end of the reverse section curves the graph smoothly from a negative slope back into a flat or positive slope, easing the video back into standard forward playback.

Precision Frame Control

The curve editor allows editors to pinpoint the exact frame where a motion should peak and invert. By placing keyframes at precise moments of action (such as a jump, an impact, or a camera whip), the editor allows you to ramp seamlessly into reverse and back to forward playback within a single, continuous clip on the timeline, eliminating the need to slice clips or manually apply separate speed effects.
