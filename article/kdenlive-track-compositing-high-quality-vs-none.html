Kdenlive Track Compositing: High Quality vs None

This article explains the key differences between the "High Quality" and "None" track compositing settings in the Kdenlive video editor. Understanding these options is essential for optimizing your video editing workflow, as they directly impact timeline preview performance, system resource usage, and how overlapping video tracks blend together.

What is Track Compositing in Kdenlive?

Track compositing determines how Kdenlive processes multiple layers of video stacked on top of each other in the timeline. When you have overlapping tracks—such as a title, a logo, or a green-screen video placed above a background track—the compositing engine decides how to blend these layers based on your selected setting.

You can toggle this setting via the track compositing dropdown menu, usually located in the timeline toolbar.

"High Quality" Track Compositing

The High Quality setting enables automatic, full-resolution compositing across all tracks in your timeline.

  • How it works: Kdenlive automatically detects alpha channels (transparency) in your clips. If you place a transparent title, a PNG image, or a video with a chroma key effect on an upper track, Kdenlive automatically blends it with the track beneath it.
  • Visual Accuracy: This mode uses high-quality scaling and blending algorithms, ensuring that what you see in the Project Monitor is a highly accurate representation of your final rendered video.
  • Performance Impact: Because the software is constantly calculating real-time blends and transparency for every frame, "High Quality" mode is resource-intensive. If you are working with high-resolution footage (like 4K) or have many overlapping tracks, this setting can cause timeline playback to lag or stutter.

"None" Track Compositing

The None setting completely disables automatic track compositing.

  • How it works: When set to "None," Kdenlive treats every video track as completely opaque, regardless of whether the files contain transparency or alpha channels. An upper track will entirely block out the track beneath it.
  • Manual Compositing Required: To blend tracks or show transparency in this mode, you must manually add a composition transition (such as the "Composite and Transform" or "Affine" effect) between the overlapping clips.
  • Performance Impact: Disabling automatic compositing drastically reduces the processing load on your CPU and GPU. Playback in the Project Monitor becomes much smoother, making this an ideal setting for editing complex, multi-layered timelines on lower-end computers.

Key Differences at a Glance

Feature "High Quality" Setting "None" Setting
Automatic Transparency Yes (titles and alpha channels blend automatically) No (upper tracks completely block lower tracks)
Playback Performance Demanding (may cause lag on complex timelines) Very fast (smooth playback with minimal CPU/GPU usage)
Workflow Speed Faster for editing, as no manual transitions are needed Slower, as you must manually add composition effects
Preview Accuracy Highly accurate to the final render Basic draft preview (unless manual transitions are added)

Which Setting Should You Choose?

  • Choose High Quality if you have a powerful computer, are working on relatively simple projects, or are doing final visual checks, color grading, and positioning of overlays.
  • Choose None if your timeline playback is lagging, you are editing on a laptop or older hardware, or you prefer to have absolute, manual control over how and when tracks composite using specific transition effects.
