Kdenlive Old Film Effect: Add Scratches and Grain

This article provides a straightforward, step-by-step guide on how to use the "Old Film" effect in Kdenlive to give your modern videos a vintage, retro aesthetic. You will learn how to locate the effect within the software, apply it to your video clips on the timeline, and adjust the specific settings to customize the appearance of artificial scratches, dust, hair, and film grain.

Step 1: Add Your Footage to the Timeline

Open Kdenlive and import the video clip you want to edit into your Project Bin. Drag and drop the clip from the Project Bin down onto the video track of your timeline.

Step 2: Locate the Old Film Effect

Navigate to the Effects tab (typically located in the tabbed panel next to the Project Bin). In the search bar at the top of the Effects panel, type "Old Film" (or "Old Cinema" depending on your version of Kdenlive). The effect is powered by the Frei0r plugin library and is categorized under "Stylize" or "Artistic" effects.

Step 3: Apply the Effect to Your Clip

Click on the Old Film effect from the search results and drag it directly onto your video clip in the timeline. Alternatively, select your video clip first, then double-click the effect in the panel to apply it. The Project Monitor will immediately display a default vintage preview of your footage.

Step 4: Customize Scratches, Dust, and Grain

To fine-tune the look, select the video clip on your timeline and look at the Effect/Composition Stack panel (usually on the top-right or properties area). You will see several sliders to customize the artificial imperfections:

  • Delta / Speed: Controls the speed and frequency at which the dust and scratch patterns change frame by frame.
  • Scratches: Adjust this slider to increase or decrease the number of vertical scratch lines that appear on the virtual film strip.
  • Dust: Raise this value to generate more random black specks and dust particles across the frame.
  • Hairs: Turn this up to add simulated organic hairs and fibers that occasionally flicker onto the screen, mimicking debris caught in a projector gate.
  • Flicker (Brightness Fluctuation): Adjust this setting to simulate the unstable lighting and frame-rate fluctuations of old analog projectors.

Step 5: Enhance with Color Grading and Grain (Optional)

To make the effect look even more authentic, you can stack a couple of complementary effects:

  • Sepia or Vignette: Search for "Sepia" or "Vignette" in the Effects panel and drag them onto the clip to give the footage a faded, brown-toned color palette and darkened edges.
  • Grain / Noise: If you want a more textured, gritty look, search for the "Noise" or "Grain" effect. Add it to the clip and lower the variance/intensity so it subtlely mimics real silver halide film grain.

Step 6: Preview and Render

Play back the timeline in the Project Monitor to ensure the movement of the scratches and grain looks natural. When you are satisfied with the vintage style, go to the top menu, select Project > Render, choose your preferred export format, and render your final video.
