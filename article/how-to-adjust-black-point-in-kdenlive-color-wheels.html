How to Adjust Black Point in Kdenlive Color Wheels

Milky or washed-out shadows in video footage occur when the darkest parts of an image sit above true black, reducing overall contrast. This guide explains how to use the Lift, Gamma, Gain color wheels alongside the Waveform scope in Kdenlive to lower your black point, eliminate milkiness, and restore rich contrast to your shadows.

1. Open the Waveform Scope

Before adjusting color wheels, enable the Waveform monitor to accurately measure your luminance levels:

  1. Go to the top menu and select View > Scopes > Waveform.
  2. Look at the lower boundary of the waveform graph. Milky shadows will show the lowest cluster of data floating well above the 0 (black) baseline.

2. Apply the Lift/Gamma/Gain Effect

  1. Select the clip on your timeline.
  2. Open the Effects panel.
  3. Search for Lift, Gamma, Gain (or Color Wheels) and double-click to apply it to your clip.

3. Lower the Lift to Fix Milky Shadows

The Lift wheel directly controls the black point and shadow regions of your footage.

  1. Locate the Lift wheel in the effect settings in the Properties panel.
  2. Use the vertical slider next to the Lift wheel (or the master value underneath) to lower the luminance value.
  3. Watch the Waveform scope while pulling the Lift value down.
  4. Stop lowering when the lowest traces of the waveform graph just touch the 0 line on the scope.

Avoid pulling the data significantly below the 0 line, as this will crush the shadows and permanently clip detail.

4. Compensate with Gamma (Midtones)

Lowering the Lift can occasionally drag down your midtones, making the overall image look too dark.

  • Locate the Gamma wheel, which controls midtones.
  • Slightly increase the Gamma brightness slider until midtone details and skin tones return to their desired exposure levels.

5. Check for Color Casts in Shadows

If your shadows look tinted instead of a neutral black:

  • Look at the center point inside the Lift color wheel.
  • Drag the center point in the opposite direction of the unwanted tint (for example, push away from blue/cyan toward warm tones if the shadows look cold) until the shadows appear neutral.
