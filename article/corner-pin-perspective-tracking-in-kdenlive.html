Corner Pin Perspective Tracking in Kdenlive

Adjusting corner pin coordinates to match dynamic perspective shifts in Kdenlive requires applying the Corners effect and animating its four control points using keyframes. This guide outlines the exact workflow for mapping an overlay clip to a moving, angled surface—such as a phone screen, billboard, or wall—by manually tracking and adjusting the perspective frame by frame or through key action points.

Step 1: Arrange the Timeline

  1. Place your base footage (the background video containing the target surface) on a lower video track (e.g., Track V1).
  2. Place the video or image you want to pin on the track directly above the base footage (e.g., Track V2).
  3. Trim both clips to ensure the overlay aligns with the duration of the target scene.

Step 2: Apply the Corners Effect

  1. Select the overlay clip on Track V2.
  2. Open the Effects tab and search for Corners.
  3. Drag and drop the Corners effect onto the overlay clip in the timeline or into the Effect/Composition Stack.

Step 3: Align the Initial Frame

  1. Move the timeline playhead to the very first frame where the overlay needs to appear.
  2. In the Effect Stack, look at the four corner coordinate groups: Top Left, Top Right, Bottom Left, and Bottom Right.
  3. In the Clip Monitor (or Project Monitor), drag each of the four corner control handles directly onto the four corners of the target surface in the background footage.
  4. Alternatively, use the numerical X/Y offset sliders in the effect settings to achieve pixel-perfect alignment.

Step 4: Keyframe Dynamic Perspective Shifts

  1. In the effect timeline within the Effect Stack, ensure a keyframe exists at the starting point.
  2. Advance the playhead forward to the point where the background surface changes angle, zooms, or moves.
  3. Add a new keyframe (or simply move the corner handles in the monitor, which automatically creates a keyframe).
  4. Re-align each of the four corner pins to the new positions of the target surface.
  5. Repeat this process at every significant change in perspective. For fast, erratic movements, add keyframes closer together; for smooth, linear camera motion, keyframes can be placed further apart.

Step 5: Refine Interpolation

  1. Review the motion between keyframes to ensure the overlay stays locked to the plane.
  2. Right-click the keyframes in the effect timeline and choose the appropriate interpolation mode:
    • Linear: Best for steady, straight-line camera pans and predictable motion.
    • Smooth: Best for handheld or easing camera movements to prevent abrupt directional shifts.
  3. Play back the sequence to verify that the perspective shift remains natural throughout the duration of the clip.
