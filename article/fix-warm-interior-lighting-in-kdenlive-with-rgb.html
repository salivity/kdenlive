Fix Warm Interior Lighting in Kdenlive with RGB

Indoor video footage frequently suffers from an unnatural yellow or orange cast caused by warm tungsten or incandescent interior bulbs. This guide explains how to neutralize these unwanted warm tones in Kdenlive using the "Color Balance (RGB)" effect. By making targeted adjustments to the red, green, and blue channels across shadows, midtones, and highlights, you can achieve a balanced, natural-looking color temperature.

Step 1: Select the Video Clip

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive.
  2. Drag your video clip onto the timeline.
  3. Click on the clip in the timeline to make sure it is selected (highlighted).

Step 2: Add the "Color Balance (RGB)" Effect

  1. Open the Effects panel (usually located in the left or lower-left interface tabs).
  2. Use the search bar at the top of the Effects tab and type Color Balance (RGB).
  3. Double-click the Color Balance (RGB) effect or drag and drop it directly onto your selected timeline clip.
  4. The effect controls will now appear in the Effect/Composition Stack panel.

Step 3: Understand the Balance Controls

The Color Balance (RGB) effect divides color adjustments into three tonal ranges:

  • Shadows (Lift): Affects the darkest areas of the frame.
  • Midtones (Gamma): Affects the overall body of the image, including skin tones.
  • Highlights (Gain): Affects the brightest areas and direct light sources.

Warm lighting typically over-saturates the Red and Green channels (creating yellow/orange) while lacking sufficient Blue.

Step 4: Neutralize the Warm Cast

  1. Boost the Blue Channel: In the Midtones (Gamma) and Highlights (Gain) sections, slightly increase the Blue sliders above their default value (100% or 1.0).
  2. Reduce Red and Green: If the image still looks too orange or yellow, slightly decrease the Red slider in the Midtones and Highlights, followed by a minor decrease in Green if a yellow-green tint persists.
  3. Correct Shadows: Check the dark areas of your footage. If the shadows look muddy or brown, slightly increase the Shadows Blue slider to cool down dark areas, or ensure the shadows remain neutral without turning overly blue.

Step 5: Preview and Fine-Tune

  1. Use the Effect Toggle icon (the eye/power icon next to the effect name in the Effect Stack) to turn the filter on and off.
  2. Compare the corrected version with the original footage to ensure skin tones look natural rather than pale or overly cold.
  3. Make small, incremental adjustments until whites appear neutral and the warm interior cast is effectively neutralized.
