How to Maximize Kdenlive Audio Waveforms

Accurately editing audio in Kdenlive requires a clear view of audio waveforms. This guide explains the step-by-step process for expanding track heights, maximizing audio waveform visualization, and optimizing audio display settings in the Kdenlive timeline for precise audio synchronization and trimming.

Resizing Track Height Manually

The quickest way to increase an individual audio track's height is by manually dragging its boundary in the timeline track header area (the control panel on the far left of each track).

  1. Move your mouse cursor over the timeline track header on the left side.
  2. Hover over the bottom border line of the audio track you want to expand until the cursor turns into an up-and-down resize arrow.
  3. Click and drag downward to increase the track height.
  4. Release the mouse button once the waveform reaches your desired visibility.

Using the Track Context Menu

Kdenlive provides preset options to quickly expand or collapse tracks via the right-click menu.

  1. Right-click on the empty space of the track header for the audio track.
  2. Hover over or select Track Height.
  3. Choose a larger preset or select Fit to Screen / Make Track Big depending on your Kdenlive version to expand the track immediately.

Resizing Using Mouse and Keyboard Shortcuts

You can resize tracks globally or individually using timeline shortcuts:

  • Single Track Resize: Hover your cursor over the track header of the target audio track, hold down the Shift key, and scroll your mouse wheel up to expand the track height or down to reduce it.
  • Global Track Resize: Hold Ctrl + Shift while scrolling the mouse wheel over any track header to resize all timeline tracks simultaneously.

Enhancing Waveform Visibility

Increasing track height is often paired with specific waveform settings to make quiet audio peaks more legible.

  1. Open Kdenlive's timeline settings by going to Settings > Configure Kdenlive > Timeline (or clicking the Timeline Settings icon).
  2. Under the Audio options, look for the waveform display style.
  3. Switch the waveform representation from Linear to dB (Decibel). This amplifies the visual height of quieter sections, making low-volume speech and background transients easier to locate on the timeline.
  4. Enable Separate Channels if you are working with stereo or multichannel audio and need distinct waveforms for each channel.
