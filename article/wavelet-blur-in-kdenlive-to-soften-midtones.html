Wavelet Blur in Kdenlive to Soften Midtones

Wavelet Blur in Kdenlive is a frequency-separation filtering tool that decomposes video footage into distinct spatial frequency layers, allowing editors to blur specific detail ranges without destroying fine high-frequency sharpness or broad low-frequency contrast. This guide explains how to apply the Wavelet Blur effect in Kdenlive, configure its frequency scales, and selectively soften midtone image frequencies to achieve natural skin smoothing and texture reduction.

Step 1: Add the Wavelet Blur Effect

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and place your target clip onto the timeline.
  2. Go to the Effects tab (typically located in the bottom-left or side panel).
  3. Search for Wavelet Blur in the search bar.
  4. Drag and drop the Wavelet Blur effect onto your timeline clip, or select the clip and double-click the effect.

Step 2: Understand Wavelet Frequency Bands

The Wavelet Blur algorithm breaks down the image into octave layers (detail levels):

  • High Frequencies (Fine Details): Smallest scale levels (fine hair, skin pores, sharp edge boundaries).
  • Midtone/Medium Frequencies (Texture): Intermediate scale levels (skin blemishes, surface roughness, fabric textures).
  • Low Frequencies (Shapes and Gradients): Largest scale levels (overall lighting, shadows, skin tone gradients, broad shape outlines).

To soften midtones without creating a blurry, out-of-focus look, you must apply the blur exclusively to the intermediate wavelet scales while leaving the smallest and largest scales untouched.

Step 3: Configure Effect Settings for Midtones

  1. Select the clip on the timeline and open the Effect/Composition Stack panel.
  2. Locate the Wavelet Blur parameters:
    • Radius / Amount: Sets the overall intensity of the blur.
    • Scales / Detail Levels (Passes): Controls which frequency bands are targeted.
  3. Lower or disable the blur intensity on the smallest scales (pass 1 and 2) to preserve fine edge contrast and natural skin texture.
  4. Increase the blur amount on the intermediate scales (typically pass 3 to pass 5). This targets medium-sized variations and softens unwanted surface textures.
  5. Keep the lowest-frequency scales unmodified to prevent color bleeding and preserve original lighting depth.

Step 4: Constrain the Effect with Secondary Masking (Optional)

If the midtone blur affects areas outside your subject (such as background elements or clothing):

  1. Add a Mask: Apply or Rotoscoping effect above the Wavelet Blur in the Effect Stack.
  2. Draw a matte around the specific area requiring softening (e.g., a subject's face).
  3. Adjust the mask's Feather settings to blend the softened midtone area seamlessly with the rest of the frame.
