How to Adjust Audio Reverb Decay in Kdenlive

Matching your audio acoustics to changing visual scenes is essential for realistic sound design, and adjusting the decay rate on the Audio Reverb filter in Kdenlive makes this transition seamless. This article explains how to locate the Audio Reverb effect, modify the decay time parameters, and use keyframes to dynamically adapt the reverberation as your video transitions between different physical spaces.

1. Add the Audio Reverb Filter

  1. Select the audio clip or video clip with audio on your timeline.
  2. Open the Effects tab (typically located in the workspace panels or via View > Effects).
  3. Search for Reverb (such as Audio Reverb, Freeverb, or Plate Reverb depending on your installed MLT/LADSPA plugins).
  4. Drag and drop the reverb filter onto your chosen clip or double-click to apply it.

2. Locate the Decay Parameter

Once applied, access the effect settings in the Effect/Composition Stack panel:

  • Look for parameters labeled Decay Time, Room Size, or RT60 (the time it takes for reflections to decrease by 60 dB).
  • Low Decay (0.2s - 0.8s): Best for small, dry, or acoustically treated spaces like bedrooms, offices, or car interiors.
  • Medium Decay (1.0s - 2.5s): Ideal for moderate environments such as classrooms, hallways, or living rooms.
  • High Decay (3.0s+): Suitable for expansive or highly reflective environments like churches, tunnels, caves, or large empty warehouses.

3. Keyframe the Decay Rate for Dynamic Scenes

When a character moves from an indoor room to a vast open space (or vice versa), the decay rate must change dynamically over time rather than remaining static.

  1. In the Effect/Composition Stack, look for the small clock icon or keyframe bar associated with the reverb parameters.
  2. Enable keyframing for the decay or room size parameter.
  3. Position the timeline playhead at the exact moment the environment begins to change. Add a keyframe and set the initial decay value.
  4. Move the playhead to where the scene transition completes, add a second keyframe, and adjust the decay value to match the new acoustic environment.
  5. Choose your interpolation mode (Linear or Smooth) to ensure the transition between spaces sounds natural rather than abrupt.

4. Balance Dry/Wet Levels

Adjusting the decay rate alone can sometimes overpower the primary dialogue. To maintain vocal clarity:

  • Adjust the Dry/Wet mix alongside the decay.
  • Keep the Dry level high for primary intelligibility.
  • Increase the Wet (processed signal) level slightly when moving into larger spaces to reinforce the extended decay time, and lower it in compact spaces.
