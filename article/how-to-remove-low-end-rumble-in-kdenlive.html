How to Remove Low-End Rumble in Kdenlive

Low-frequency background noise, such as microphone handling, air conditioning hum, and traffic rumble, can significantly degrade dialogue clarity. This guide explains how to locate, apply, and configure the high-pass audio filter in Kdenlive to effectively cut out unwanted sub-bass rumble while preserving natural-sounding voice tracks.

Step 1: Select Your Audio Source

You can apply a high-pass filter to an individual audio clip or an entire audio track.

  • For a single clip: Click on the target audio clip in the timeline to select it.
  • For an entire track: Click on the track header on the left side of the timeline to apply the effect across all dialogue clips placed on that track.

Step 2: Add the High-Pass Filter Effect

  1. Open the Effects tab in the main interface panels (if hidden, enable it via View > Effects).
  2. Click the Audio icon at the top of the Effects panel to filter audio-specific tools.
  3. In the search bar, type High Pass (or highpass).
  4. Double-click the High Pass effect or drag and drop it directly onto your selected clip or track header.

Step 3: Configure the Cutoff Frequency

Once applied, the effect properties will appear in the Effect/Composition Stack panel.

  1. Locate the Frequency (or Cutoff Frequency) parameter.
  2. Set the starting cutoff value based on the voice type:
    • Male Dialogue: 80 Hz to 100 Hz.
    • Female Dialogue: 100 Hz to 120 Hz.
    • Thin or High-Pitched Voices: Up to 150 Hz if severe rumble persists.
  3. If an adjustment curve/rolloff (or Q-factor/Poles) setting is available, keep it at standard slope (typically 12 dB/octave or 2-pole) to prevent unnatural phase distortion.

Step 4: Preview and Fine-Tune

  1. Play back the timeline with headphones to accurately monitor low-end changes.
  2. Toggle the effect on and off using the eye or power icon in the Effect Stack to compare the processed audio against the original.
  3. If the voice begins to sound thin, hollow, or "tinny," lower the cutoff frequency slightly until the vocal body is restored while keeping the rumble filtered out.
