Enhance Vocals in Kdenlive with Audio Compressor

Using the Audio Compressor effect in Kdenlive allows you to balance dynamic range, increase the overall volume of quieter phrases, and ensure vocal clarity cuts through dense background noise or music. By attenuating the loudest peaks and boosting the overall signal, vocals remain consistently audible throughout the entire mix. The following steps guide you through applying and configuring the Audio Compressor effect in Kdenlive for optimal vocal presence.

Step 1: Add the Audio Clip to the Timeline

Place your video or audio recording containing the vocal track onto an audio track in the Kdenlive timeline. If your vocal is part of a video clip with integrated audio, ensure the audio stream is properly separated or selected.

Step 2: Open the Effects Panel

Navigate to the Effects tab, typically located in the left or central panel dock. If the panel is hidden, enable it via the top menu by selecting View > Effects.

Step 3: Search and Apply the Compressor Effect

In the Effects panel search bar, type Compressor. Locate the Compressor (or Audio Compressor) effect under the Audio Correction or Audio Dynamics categories. Drag and drop the effect directly onto the vocal audio clip in your timeline, or select the clip and double-click the effect.

Step 4: Access the Effect Stack

Click on the audio clip in the timeline to view its applied effects inside the Effect/Composition Stack panel. You will see the parameters available for tweaking the audio compression.

Step 5: Adjust the Core Compression Parameters

Fine-tune the following settings to bring presence to the vocal track:

  • Threshold: Set this to determine when compression kicks in. For speech in noisy environments, set the threshold between -18 dB and -24 dB so the compressor catches typical vocal peaks.
  • Ratio: Determines how aggressively the volume is reduced once it crosses the threshold. A ratio between 3:1 and 5:1 is ideal for spoken vocals to provide control without creating noticeable distortion.
  • Attack Time: Controls how quickly the compressor reacts to volume spikes. Set a fast attack time between 5 ms and 15 ms to prevent sudden vocal bursts from overpowering the mix.
  • Release Time: Controls how quickly the audio returns to normal volume after dropping below the threshold. A setting between 50 ms and 150 ms ensures a natural decay without unnatural volume pumping.
  • Makeup Gain (Output Gain): Because compression lowers the loudest parts, increase the makeup gain by 2 dB to 6 dB to bring the overall vocal level back up to a competitive volume against background noise.

Step 6: Preview and Fine-Tune

Play back the timeline containing both the vocal track and background noise. Adjust the Makeup Gain and Threshold in real-time until the dialogue sounds consistently distinct and sits comfortably on top of the accompanying audio.
