How to Show Separate Audio Channels in Kdenlive

This guide explains how to configure the timeline in Kdenlive to display multi-channel audio as individual, distinct waveform tracks. By enabling separate channel visualization, you can easily monitor left and right stereo channels or multi-track recordings independently, improving mixing accuracy and making it simpler to identify audio issues directly within your edit.

Enable Separate Audio Channels Globally

To configure Kdenlive so that all multi-channel audio clips display separate waveforms on the timeline, use the application settings:

  1. Open Kdenlive.
  2. Navigate to the top menu and select Settings > Configure Kdenlive... (or Kdenlive > Preferences on macOS).
  3. In the settings window, select the Timeline tab from the left sidebar.
  4. Locate the Audio section in the timeline display options.
  5. Check the box labeled Separate channels (or Draw audio channels separately depending on your Kdenlive version).
  6. Click Apply and then OK to save the changes.

The timeline will refresh, and any stereo or multi-channel audio clip will now display distinct waveform layers within its audio track.

Adjust Audio Track Height for Better Visibility

After enabling separate channel waveforms, individual channels may appear small if your track height is collapsed:

  1. Hover your cursor over the bottom boundary of the audio track header on the left side of the timeline until the resize arrow appears.
  2. Click and drag downward to expand the height of the audio track.
  3. Alternatively, right-click the track header, choose Track Height, and select a larger preset to view each channel's waveform clearly.

Splitting Audio Channels into Independent Tracks

If you need to edit or apply effects to each channel individually rather than just viewing the separated waveforms:

  1. Right-click the video or audio clip in the Project Bin.
  2. Navigate to Clip Jobs and select Set Audio Channels.
  3. Configure the mapping to split stereo channels into separate mono tracks.
  4. Drag the clip to the timeline to place each audio channel onto its own dedicated audio track.
