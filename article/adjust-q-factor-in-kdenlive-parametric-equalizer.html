Adjust Q-Factor in Kdenlive Parametric Equalizer

Adjusting the Q-factor (Quality factor) in Kdenlive's Parametric Equalizer allows you to control the bandwidth of specific frequency adjustments. By setting a high Q-factor to narrow the bandwidth, you can surgically sweep through the audio spectrum to find harsh resonances, electrical hums, or whistling sounds, and cut them out without degrading the overall tonal quality of your audio track.

1. Apply the Parametric Equalizer Effect

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and select the audio or video clip on the timeline.
  2. Go to the Effects panel (usually located in the bottom-left or tabbed with Project Bin).
  3. Search for Parametric Equalizer in the audio effects category.
  4. Drag and drop the effect onto your clip or the entire audio track.

2. Understand the Q-Factor Parameter

In equalizer settings, the Q-factor controls how wide or narrow the frequency bell curve is:

  • Low Q Value (e.g., 0.5 – 1.5): Affects a broad range of surrounding frequencies. Best used for general, gentle tone shaping.
  • High Q Value (e.g., 4.0 – 10.0+): Affects a very narrow, sharp slice of frequencies. Ideal for surgical audio repair and isolating problem sounds.

3. Use the "Boost and Sweep" Method to Find Troublesome Frequencies

  1. In the Effect/Composition Stack panel for the selected clip, choose an active frequency band (such as Band 2 or Band 3).
  2. Narrow the Q: Increase the Q slider to a high value (e.g., 6.0 to 10.0) to create a sharp peak.
  3. Boost the Gain: Raise the Gain slider significantly (around +10 dB to +15 dB).
  4. Play and Sweep: Play the audio clip on a loop and slowly drag the Frequency slider across the spectrum.
  5. Listen carefully. When the annoying noise (such as microphone feedback, a high-pitch whistle, or low hum) suddenly becomes intensely loud and exaggerated, you have identified the exact frequency of the problem.

4. Apply the Cut to Remove the Frequency

  1. Keep the Frequency slider locked at the identified position.
  2. Lower the Gain slider down into negative values (e.g., -6 dB to -18 dB, or down to the minimum if you need a full notch filter).
  3. Fine-tune the Q parameter:
    • If the noise is still audible around the edges, slightly decrease the Q value to widen the cut.
    • If the cut makes the main voice or audio sound hollow or unnatural, increase the Q value to make the cut narrower and more precise.

5. Compare the Results

Click the effect enable/disable toggle (the eye or checkbox icon) in the Effect Stack to bypass the filter and compare your processed audio against the original to ensure the troublesome frequency has been removed cleanly.
