Adjust Master Limiter Release Time in Kdenlive

This guide explains how to locate, configure, and adjust the release time on the master output limiter in Kdenlive. Modifying the release time allows you to control how quickly the limiter stops attenuating audio after peaks drop below the threshold, preventing clipping while eliminating unwanted audio pumping in your final video export.

Step 1: Open the Master Audio Track

  1. Look at the timeline interface in Kdenlive.
  2. Locate the Master track header, typically positioned at the very bottom or top of the track list depending on your layout.
  3. Alternatively, open the Audio Mixer panel by navigating to Menu > View > Audio Mixer.

Step 2: Access the Master Effects Stack

  1. Click directly on the Master track header in the timeline or select the Master channel strip inside the Audio Mixer panel.
  2. Open the Effect/Composition Stack panel (navigate to Menu > View > Effect/Composition Stack if it is not visible).
  3. If a limiter is not already present, go to the Effects library, search for Limiter (or Fast Lookahead Limiter), and drag it onto the Master track.

Step 3: Adjust the Release Time

  1. In the Effect Stack panel for the Master track, locate the applied Limiter effect.
  2. Find the parameter labeled Release, Release Time, or Decay.
  3. Adjust the slider or type a precise numerical value:
    • Shorter Release (e.g., 0.05s to 0.1s / 50ms to 100ms): Best for fast, dynamic audio with sudden transients (like drum hits or sound effects) where you need volume to recover immediately. Setting this too low may cause audible distortion.
    • Longer Release (e.g., 0.3s to 0.5s / 300ms to 500ms): Best for dialogue, background music, and overall full-mix mastering to ensure smooth, natural gain reduction without noticeable volume fluctuations ("pumping").

Step 4: Preview and Fine-Tune

  1. Play back the loudest sections of your project timeline while monitoring the master volume meters.
  2. Ensure the limiter is catching signal peaks before they hit 0 dB (ideally keeping your peak ceiling around -0.5 dB to -1.0 dB).
  3. Listen closely to the transition after loud peaks to ensure the volume recovers smoothly according to your adjusted release time.
