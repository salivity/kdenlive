How to Remove 60Hz Hum in Kdenlive with Notch Filter

Ground loop hum is a common audio issue caused by electrical interference that introduces a persistent 60Hz buzz into recordings. This guide provides a straightforward method for identifying and eliminating this low-frequency noise in Kdenlive by applying the Notch Filter audio effect, effectively cleaning up your audio track without compromising the quality of your main sound or dialogue.

Step 1: Add Your Clip to the Timeline

  1. Open Kdenlive and load your project.
  2. Drag and drop the affected video or audio clip from the Project Bin onto your timeline.
  3. If the audio is embedded in a video clip, you can work on it directly or right-click the clip and select Split Audio to separate the visual and audio streams.

Step 2: Apply the Notch Filter Effect

  1. Open the Effects tab (typically located in the bottom-left or side panel).
  2. Use the search bar to look for Notch Filter (listed under Audio Effects / Libavfilter).
  3. Drag and drop the Notch Filter effect directly onto the audio clip on the timeline.

Step 3: Configure the Frequency and Bandwidth

Once the effect is applied, select the clip to view its properties in the Effect/Composition Stack panel.

  1. Frequency: Set this parameter to 60. This targets the exact 60Hz frequency of the ground loop hum.
  2. Width / Bandwidth (Q-factor): Adjust the bandwidth to a narrow setting (usually between 1 and 5, or a high Q value depending on the specific Kdenlive version). A narrow bandwidth ensures that only the 60Hz noise is removed while preserving the low-end frequencies of voices or background music.

Step 4: Address Harmonics (If Necessary)

Ground loop buzz often generates secondary harmonic frequencies at multiples of 60Hz (such as 120Hz, 180Hz, and 240Hz). If a faint buzz remains after filtering 60Hz:

  1. Add a second Notch Filter to the same clip and set the Frequency to 120.
  2. Add a third Notch Filter set to 180 if needed.
  3. Keep the width narrow on each filter to avoid thinning out the overall sound.

Step 5: Preview and Export

Play the timeline to monitor the changes. Toggle the effect on and off using the eye icon in the Effect Stack to verify that the buzz is gone while dialogue and background audio remain natural. Once satisfied, proceed to render your project as normal.
