How to Use Keyframes to Animate in Kdenlive

Keyframes are essential tools in video editing that allow you to change the parameters of an effect over time. This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step walkthrough on how to apply effects to your clips in Kdenlive and use keyframes to animate properties like position, size, opacity, and color.

Step 1: Apply an Effect to Your Clip

Select a video clip on your timeline. Open the Effects panel and search for an effect that supports keyframing, such as Transform or Brightness. Drag and drop the chosen effect directly onto your timeline clip.

Step 2: Enable Keyframing

With the clip selected, look at the Effect/Composition Stack panel (usually located in the top-middle or top-left area of the interface). Here, you will see the parameters of the applied effect. To enable keyframing, click the Show/Hide Keyframes icon (which looks like a small clock or stopwatch) next to the effect parameters. This action will reveal the keyframe ruler directly beneath the effect settings.

Step 3: Add and Adjust Keyframes

The keyframe ruler represents the exact duration of your selected clip. To animate the effect:

  1. Set the start point: Move the playhead on the keyframe ruler to the time where you want the animation to begin. Double-click on the ruler, or click the Add keyframe (plus sign) button. Adjust the parameter values (such as changing the size to 50%).
  2. Set the end point: Move the playhead further down the keyframe ruler to where you want the animation to stop. Create a second keyframe and change the parameter values again (such as increasing the size to 100%).

Kdenlive will automatically calculate the transition and animate the values between these two points.

Step 4: Fine-Tune Keyframe Interpolation

You can control how smoothly the animation transitions from one keyframe to the next. Right-click on any keyframe in the ruler to change its interpolation mode:

  • Linear: Creates a constant, steady speed of change.
  • Discrete: Causes the value to jump instantly to the next setting without a smooth transition.
  • Smooth: Automatically eases the animation in and out for a more natural, professional look.

You can drag your keyframes left or right along the ruler at any time to adjust the timing and speed of your animation.
