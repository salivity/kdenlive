Fix Breathing Sounds in Kdenlive with Noise Gate

Unwanted breathing artifacts can distract listeners and lower the production value of your video project. This guide provides a straightforward method for using Kdenlive’s built-in Noise Gate audio effect, detailing how to find the optimal threshold levels and adjust accompanying settings to eliminate breath sounds naturally without clipping dialogue.

1. Add the Noise Gate Effect to Your Audio

  1. Select your audio track or the specific audio clip in the Kdenlive timeline.
  2. Open the Effects tab (typically located in the workspace sidebar).
  3. Search for Noise Gate in the audio effects category.
  4. Drag and drop the Noise Gate effect onto your target audio clip or track.

2. Identify the Decibel Level of the Breathing

Before adjusting the threshold, you must determine the volume of the breath sounds compared to the spoken voice:

  1. Play a section of your timeline where you take a breath between words.
  2. Observe the Audio Mixer or the master dB meter.
  3. Note the peak decibel (dB) level of the breath (typically falling between -35 dB and -45 dB).
  4. Note the lowest decibel level of your normal speech (typically between -18 dB and -12 dB).

3. Adjust the Threshold Setting

The threshold determines the volume level below which audio is muted.

  1. Open the Effect/Composition Stack panel to view the Noise Gate properties.
  2. Locate the Threshold slider (often set in dB).
  3. Set the Threshold value roughly 2 to 5 dB higher than the peak breathing level you identified, but safely below the quietest parts of your spoken words.
    • Example: If your breathing peaks at -35 dB and your voice speaks at -18 dB, set the threshold between -30 dB and -28 dB.
  4. Play back the audio. The gate should close (mute) during breaths and open (unmute) as soon as you speak.

4. Fine-Tune Attack, Hold, and Release

Improper timing parameters can cause the voice to sound clipped or robotic. Adjust these complementary parameters to ensure smooth transitions:

  • Attack: Set between 1 ms and 5 ms. A fast attack ensures the gate opens instantly when you start speaking so the first consonants of words are not cut off.
  • Hold: Set between 20 ms and 50 ms. This keeps the gate open momentarily during brief micro-pauses within words.
  • Release (Decay): Set between 100 ms and 250 ms. A moderate release gently fades the audio out rather than cutting it abruptly at the end of a sentence.

5. Verify the Final Output

Listen to the entire dialogue track with headphones. If the beginnings or endings of words sound clipped, lower the threshold slightly (move closer to negative infinity, e.g., from -28 dB to -32 dB) or increase the release time. If breath sounds still leak through, raise the threshold slightly.
