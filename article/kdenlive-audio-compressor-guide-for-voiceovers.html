Kdenlive Audio Compressor Guide for Voiceovers

Applying an audio compressor to your voiceover in Kdenlive evens out the dynamic range by lowering the loudest peaks and raising quieter sections. This creates a broadcast-ready, punchy, and consistent sound that keeps spoken commentary clear throughout a video. The following guide provides the exact steps and recommended settings to configure Kdenlive's built-in compressor effect for high-quality voice tracks.

Step 1: Add the Audio Compressor Effect

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and place your voiceover audio onto an audio track in the timeline.
  2. Go to the Effects tab in the interface.
  3. In the search bar, type Compressor (or navigate to Audio Correction > Compressor).
  4. Drag and drop the Compressor effect directly onto the individual voiceover clip, or onto the track header if you want the effect applied across all clips on that track.

Step 2: Set the Threshold

The Threshold determines the decibel level at which the compressor starts working.

  • Locate the Threshold slider in the effect settings panel.
  • Set the threshold between -18 dB and -24 dB as a starting point.
  • The goal is to catch only the louder peaks of natural speech without compressing the entire signal.

Step 3: Configure the Compression Ratio

The Ratio defines how much the signal is reduced once it crosses the threshold.

  • For voiceover content, adjust the ratio between 3:1 and 4:1.
  • A ratio of 3:1 provides gentle smoothing, while 4:1 creates a tighter, more modern, and punchier vocal delivery.
  • Avoid ratios above 6:1, as extreme settings can sound unnatural or squashed.

Step 4: Adjust Attack and Release Times

  • Attack Time: Set this to approximately 10 ms to 20 ms. A fast attack catches sudden spikes quickly while still allowing initial consonant sounds (transients) to punch through cleanly.
  • Release Time: Set this between 100 ms and 200 ms. This allows the compressor to smoothly release the volume reduction between words without creating noticeable "pumping" artifacts.

Step 5: Apply Makeup Gain

Because compression reduces the volume of loud peaks, you must boost the overall signal to make the track punchy and loud.

  • Locate the Gain (or Makeup Gain) slider.
  • Increase the gain by 3 dB to 6 dB, compensating for the volume lost during compression.
  • Watch the Kdenlive audio mixer to ensure your voice peaks comfortably between -6 dB and -3 dB, avoiding 0 dB to prevent digital clipping.

Step 6: Preview and Fine-Tune

  1. Play back the timeline and monitor the audio meter.
  2. Ensure quiet words are easily intelligible alongside louder vocal emphasis.
  3. If the voice sounds muffled, slightly decrease the ratio or increase the attack time to restore natural dynamics.
