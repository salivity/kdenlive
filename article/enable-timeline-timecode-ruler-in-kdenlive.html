Enable Timeline Timecode Ruler in Kdenlive

This article provides a quick, step-by-step guide on how to enable, configure, and use the timeline timecode ruler in Kdenlive for precise frame-by-frame video editing. You will learn how to switch between different timecode formats, zoom in on individual frames, and navigate your project with frame-level accuracy.

Displaying the Timecode Ruler

By default, the timeline ruler is always visible at the top of the timeline area in Kdenlive. However, to utilize it for precise editing, you must configure it to display the exact format you need.

  1. Locate the Timecode Display: Look at the top-left area of the timeline panel, just above the track headers. You will see a digital counter showing the current position of the playhead.
  2. Switch Formats (Timecode vs. Frames): Right-click on this digital timecode display. From the context menu, you can toggle between Timecode (HH:MM:SS:Frames) and Frames (absolute frame count). Choosing "Frames" is often preferred for high-precision VFX or animation work.

Zooming in for Frame-by-Frame Navigation

To see individual frames marked on the timeline ruler, you must adjust the timeline zoom level:

  • Using the Mouse: Hold the Ctrl key and scroll up with your mouse wheel while hovering over the timeline.
  • Using the Zoom Slider: Drag the zoom slider located at the bottom-right corner of the Kdenlive window all the way to the right.
  • Using Keyboard Shortcuts: Press Ctrl + + (plus) to zoom in, and Ctrl + - (minus) to zoom out.

Once zoomed in fully, the ruler tick marks will represent individual frames, allowing you to see exactly where cuts and transitions align.

Precision Navigation Controls

Once your ruler is set up and zoomed in, use these keyboard shortcuts to navigate frame-by-frame with absolute precision:

  • Move One Frame Forward: Press the Right Arrow key.
  • Move One Frame Backward: Press the Left Arrow key.
  • Move One Second Forward: Press Shift + Right Arrow.
  • Move One Second Backward: Press Shift + Left Arrow.
  • Snap to Cuts: Use the Alt + Right Arrow or Alt + Left Arrow keys to jump the playhead directly to the nearest edit point on the timeline.
