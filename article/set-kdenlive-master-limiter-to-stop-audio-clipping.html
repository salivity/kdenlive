Set Kdenlive Master Limiter to Stop Audio Clipping

Inter-sample clipping occurs when digital audio transitions into an analog waveform, causing peaks between individual samples to exceed 0 dBFS and introduce harsh distortion. In video editing, preventing these true peaks is vital for meeting streaming loudness standards and maintaining pristine playback across various sound systems. This guide explains how to properly apply and configure a limiter on the Master audio bus in Kdenlive to restrict ceiling levels and eliminate inter-sample distortion.

1. Open the Audio Mixer and Master Effects Stack

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive.
  2. Go to View in the top menu and ensure Audio Mixer is checked.
  3. Locate the Master channel on the far right of the Audio Mixer panel.
  4. Alternatively, click on the Master track header on the timeline to reveal the Master Effect Stack in the Properties dock.

2. Apply a Limiter Effect to the Master Channel

  1. Open the Effects tab.
  2. Search for the Limiter effect (or Fast Limiter / LADSPA: Fast Lookahead Limiter, depending on your installed MLT/LADSPA plugins).
  3. Drag and drop the Limiter effect directly onto the Master audio track header or into the Master effect stack.

3. Adjust the Ceiling (Limit/Threshold) Parameter

To completely prevent inter-sample clipping during export and post-upload encoding (such as YouTube's AAC conversion), you must create a margin below 0.0 dBFS:

  • Ceiling / Limit Output: Set this to -1.0 dB (or -1.0 dBFS). For aggressive lossy encoding (e.g., low-bitrate AAC/MP3), set it to -1.5 dB or -2.0 dB.
  • Setting the ceiling to -1.0 dB guarantees that when the digital signal is converted to analog or re-encoded to a lossy format, the reconstructed inter-sample peaks will not exceed 0 dBFS.

4. Fine-Tune Limiter Settings

  • Input Gain (Pre-Gain): Balance your mix so the loudest passages lightly hit the limiter. You should ideally see no more than 1 to 3 dB of gain reduction during normal peaks.
  • Release Time: Set the release time between 50 ms and 150 ms. Faster times (50 ms) prevent pumping on percussive material, while slightly longer times (100–200 ms) sound smoother for dialogue and acoustic audio.
  • Lookahead: If available, set lookahead to 5 ms to 10 ms. This allows the limiter to react before transients arrive, preventing peak slippage.

5. Verify Metering

Play back the loudest sections of your timeline and observe the Master meter. The output levels should strictly top out at your defined ceiling (e.g., -1.0 dBFS), ensuring your exported file remains clean and free of inter-sample distortion across all playback devices.
