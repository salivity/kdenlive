How to Fix Cold Shadows with White Balance in Kdenlive

Outdoor footage shot in open shade or during overcast days often exhibits unwanted blue or cyan casts in shaded areas while the direct light remains neutral. This guide explains how to effectively use Kdenlive's White Balance and color tools to adjust the color temperature, neutralize cold shadows, and achieve a balanced, natural look for your outdoor video clips.

Step 1: Add the White Balance Effect

  1. Place your outdoor video clip on the Kdenlive timeline and select it.
  2. Open the Effects tab in the project panel.
  3. Search for White Balance in the search bar.
  4. Drag and drop the White Balance effect onto your selected clip in the timeline or into the Effect Stack panel.

Step 2: Use the Temperature Slider to Warm the Scene

  1. Go to the Effect Stack panel to view the controls for the White Balance effect.
  2. Locate the Color Temperature (or Green / Temperature) slider.
  3. Drag the slider toward the right (higher Kelvin values / amber-orange side) to counteract the cold blue tones in the shadows.
  4. Monitor the Project Monitor to see real-time updates. Stop when the dark and shadowed areas shift from a chilly blue to a neutral, natural tone.

Step 3: Monitor with the Vectorscope and RGB Parade

To ensure accuracy beyond visual estimation on your monitor:

  1. Open the video scopes by navigating to View > Vectorscope and View > RGB Parade.
  2. Observe the bottom portion of the RGB Parade trace, which represents the shadows (darks).
  3. If the Blue channel is significantly higher in the lower third of the graph compared to Red and Green, continue increasing the temperature until the bases of all three channels align evenly.

Step 4: Isolate Shadows (Advanced Fine-Tuning)

If adjusting the global White Balance slider warms up your highlights (such as direct sunlight or clouds) too much while correcting the shadows:

  1. Add the Color Correction (Lift, Gamma, Gain) or Color Wheels effect to the clip.
  2. Keep the White Balance set to neutral or balanced for the overall scene.
  3. Navigate to the Lift wheel or sliders (which control shadow regions).
  4. Shift the Lift slightly toward yellow/red to warm only the darkest parts of the image without altering the highlight exposure or color.
