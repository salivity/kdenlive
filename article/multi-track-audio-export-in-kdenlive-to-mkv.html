Multi-Track Audio Export in Kdenlive to MKV

Configuring multi-track audio mapping when exporting to a Matroska (MKV) container in Kdenlive allows you to preserve independent audio channels—such as voiceovers, game audio, and background music—within a single video file. This guide outlines the exact steps required to prepare your timeline tracks, configure render settings, and output a Matroska file containing distinct audio streams for advanced playback or post-production needs.

1. Organize Audio Tracks in the Timeline

Ensure that each audio source you want as an independent stream is assigned to its own audio track in the timeline (e.g., Track A1 for microphone commentary, Track A2 for system audio, Track A3 for music). Unmute all tracks that should be included in the final export, and ensure none are set to "Solo" unless you specifically intend to isolate them.

2. Open the Render Menu

Navigate to the top menu and select Project > Render, or press the keyboard shortcut Ctrl + Return (or Cmd + Return on macOS) to open the export dialogue.

3. Choose the Matroska (MKV) Preset

In the Render window, locate the format presets on the left side:

  1. Open the Generic (HD, UHD, etc.) category.
  2. Select a profile that utilizes the Matroska (.mkv) container, such as Matroska / FFV1 or an H.264 / Matroska preset.

4. Enable Multi-Track Audio Export

  1. Click the More Options checkbox in the bottom-left area of the Render dialogue to expose advanced parameters.
  2. Navigate to the Audio section or tab within the expanded settings.
  3. Check the box labeled Export audio tracks as separate audio streams (in some versions, this appears under advanced audio mapping options).
  4. Ensure the audio codec selected (such as AAC, Opus, or PCM) supports multi-stream encoding without downmixing into a single master stereo track.

5. Render the Project

  1. Choose an output destination path and enter your preferred filename with the .mkv extension.
  2. Click Render to File.

6. Verify the Output

After rendering finishes, open the resulting .mkv file in a media player that supports multi-stream audio, such as VLC Media Player. Go to the player's audio track menu to verify that each timeline track appears as an independently selectable audio channel.
