How to Use Kdenlive Audio Phaser for Space Effects

The Audio Phaser filter in Kdenlive is a powerful tool for modulating sound frequencies, splitting an audio signal and altering its phase to create sweeping, otherworldly soundscapes. By manipulating parameters such as depth, feedback, and frequency rate, you can transform simple ambient drones, wind recordings, or synth pads into dynamic, swirling space textures suitable for science fiction and experimental video projects.

Step 1: Prepare Your Source Audio

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and import the audio you want to process.
  2. Drag the audio file onto an audio track in the timeline.
  3. For the best space texture results, use continuous sounds such as white noise, wind, low synthesizer drones, or sustained vocal notes.

Step 2: Add the Audio Phaser Effect

  1. Open the Effects panel (go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  2. Click on the Audio Effects category or use the search bar at the top of the panel to search for Phaser.
  3. Drag and drop the Phaser (or Audio Phaser) effect directly onto your target clip on the timeline, or onto the entire track header if you want to affect all clips in that track.

Step 3: Configure Parameters for Space Textures

Select the audio clip and navigate to the Effect/Composition Stack panel to tweak the Phaser settings:

  • Frequency / LFO Rate (Speed): Set this to a low value (between 0.1 Hz and 0.5 Hz) to create a slow, gradual sweeping motion that mimics the movement of celestial bodies or cosmic winds.
  • Depth / Modulation: Increase this parameter toward 70%–100% to maximize the intensity and perceived width of the phase sweep.
  • Feedback / Resonance: Turn this up to introduce a sharp, resonant, metallic edge to the phasing effect, which gives the texture its distinct sci-fi tone.
  • Stages: Increase the number of stages (typically 4 to 8 or more) to deepen the filtering notches and make the swirling effect more complex.
  • Dry/Wet Mix: Adjust the mix toward wet (60%–90%) so the modulated signal dominates the final output while retaining enough dry signal to preserve clarity.

Step 4: Preview and Fine-Tune

  1. Play back the timeline over the affected section to listen to the modulation in real time.
  2. Use keyframing on the frequency or depth parameters if you want the swirling effect to intensify dynamically during specific scenes.
  3. Optional: Add a Reverb or Delay effect directly after the Phaser in the effect stack to place the swirling texture into a deep, vast acoustic space.
