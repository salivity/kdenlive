Pan Mono Audio with Audio Balance in Kdenlive

This article provides a direct guide on how to position a monophonic dialogue track across stereo channels in Kdenlive. By applying the "Audio Balance" effect, you can control the spatial placement of mono voice recordings between the left and right stereo channels, whether you need a static pan to match an on-screen subject or dynamic panning using keyframes.

1. Place the Audio Clip on the Timeline

Import your monophonic dialogue clip into your project and drag it onto an audio track in the timeline. Ensure your project's master audio output is configured for stereo so panning changes can be properly heard and rendered.

2. Add the "Audio Balance" Filter

  1. Open the Effects tab in the interface (go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  2. Use the search bar to find Audio Balance (located under the Audio Correction or Channels category).
  3. Drag and drop the Audio Balance effect directly onto your dialogue clip in the timeline, or select the clip and double-click the effect.

3. Adjust the Pan Position

Select the clip to open its settings in the Effect/Composition Stack panel:

  • Center (Neutral): A balance value of 0 leaves the mono audio centered equally across both left and right stereo speakers.
  • Pan Left: Drag the slider toward the negative values (down to -1000 or -100%) to shift the audio toward the left channel.
  • Pan Right: Drag the slider toward the positive values (up to 1000 or 100%) to shift the audio toward the right channel.

4. Animate Panning Across the Stereo Field (Optional)

If your subject moves across the video frame, animate the balance over time:

  1. In the Effect/Composition Stack, click the Keyframe icon to enable keyframing for the Audio Balance filter.
  2. Move the playhead to the starting position of the movement and set the initial balance value.
  3. Move the playhead forward in the timeline to where the movement finishes, then adjust the balance slider to the new target position. Kdenlive will automatically create a new keyframe and interpolate the pan smoothly between the points.

5. Monitor and Verify

Play back your timeline using stereo headphones or studio monitors to ensure the mono dialogue pans accurately to match the visual placement of your subject without causing audio clipping.
