Kdenlive Audio Reactive Visuals Light Show Tutorial

This article provides a step-by-step guide on how to use the "Light Show" effect in Kdenlive to create dynamic, audio-reactive visuals. You will learn how to set up your project timeline, apply the effect to a video or color clip, link it to your audio track, and customize the settings so your project's visuals pulse and flash in perfect sync with the beat of your music.

Step 1: Set Up Your Timeline

To begin, you need both an audio track and a visual element on your timeline.

  1. Import your audio file (such as an MP3 or WAV) and your video clip (or a simple Color Clip) into your Project Bin.
  2. Drag your audio file onto an audio track (e.g., A1).
  3. Drag your video clip, image, or Color Clip onto a video track directly above the audio (e.g., V1). Using a black or solid color clip is highly recommended for clean, high-contrast light flashes.

Step 2: Apply the "Light Show" Effect

The Light Show effect analyzes the audio frequencies of a chosen track and translates them into visual opacity or brightness changes.

  1. Go to the Effects tab (usually located in the bottom-left or center panel).
  2. Type "Light Show" into the search bar.
  3. Drag and drop the Light Show effect onto the video or color clip on your timeline (not the audio clip).

Step 3: Link the Effect to Your Audio

For the effect to react, you must tell Kdenlive which audio track to analyze.

  1. Click on the video clip in your timeline to select it.
  2. Open the Effect/Composition Stack properties panel.
  3. Locate the Audio track parameter within the Light Show effect settings.
  4. Set this parameter to match the exact track number where your audio is located (e.g., if your music is on track A1, select 1).

Step 4: Fine-Tune the Frequency and Threshold

To make the light react to specific parts of your music, such as the bass line or the snare drum, adjust the frequency settings:

  • Low Frequency / High Frequency: To make the light flash to the bass or kick drum, set the frequency range low (e.g., between 20 Hz and 150 Hz). To make it react to vocals or high-pitched instruments, set the range higher (e.g., 1000 Hz to 5000 Hz).
  • Threshold: This determines the volume level required to trigger the light. If the light is constantly on, raise the threshold. If the light barely flashes, lower the threshold.
  • Gain: Increase the gain to make the visual pulses brighter and more intense.

Step 5: Preview and Render

Press the spacebar to play your timeline. Watch the preview window to see how the clip flashes in sync with the audio beat. Adjust the attack and decay settings if you want the light to fade out gradually rather than shutting off instantly. Once you are satisfied with the responsiveness, proceed to render your video.
