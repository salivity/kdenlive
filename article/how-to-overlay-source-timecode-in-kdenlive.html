How to Overlay Source Timecode in Kdenlive

Overlaying source clip timecodes onto your video tracks is an essential task for multi-camera synchronization, logging, client reviews, and archival workflows. In Kdenlive, this is achieved using the built-in Dynamic Text filter, which reads the embedded metadata directly from your imported media and generates an accurate, customizable timecode counter on top of your video.

Step 1: Import and Place Your Clip

  1. Open Kdenlive and create or open your project.
  2. Drag your video file from the Project Bin onto a video track in the Timeline.
  3. Left-click the clip on the timeline to select it.

Step 2: Add the Dynamic Text Filter

  1. Go to the Effects panel (if not visible, enable it via View > Effects).
  2. Use the search bar at the top of the Effects panel and type Dynamic Text.
  3. Double-click the Dynamic Text effect, or drag and drop it directly onto your selected clip on the timeline.

Step 3: Configure Source Timecode Display

  1. Open the Effect/Composition Stack panel to view the settings for the applied Dynamic Text filter.
  2. In the Text input field, insert the timecode keyword: #timecode#.
  3. To ensure the filter reads the clip's original recording timecode rather than the project timeline's timecode, select the source timecode option or choose Source Timecode from the preset/type dropdown if prompted in your specific version.
  4. Set the Timecode Format (e.g., HH:MM:SS:FF) to match your preferred frame-accurate display standard.

Step 4: Customize Position and Appearance

  1. Font and Size: Adjust the font family, font size, and text color to ensure readability against your footage.
  2. Background: Enable an outline, shadow, or a solid background box behind the text to maintain contrast over light and dark scenes.
  3. Positioning: Adjust the Horizontal Alignment (Left, Center, Right) and Vertical Alignment (Top, Bottom), or use the coordinate/geometry handles in the project monitor to drag the timecode overlay to your desired position (such as the bottom-right or top-left corner).

Step 5: Verify the Playback

  1. Play the timeline in the Project Monitor.
  2. Verify that the timecode starts at the original media's recorded starting point and updates frame by frame as the clip plays.
