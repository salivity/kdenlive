Remove Wind Noise in Kdenlive with High-Pass Filter

Wind noise often introduces a low-frequency rumble that can muddy speech and distract listeners from dialogue tracks. This guide outlines the straightforward process of applying and tuning a high-pass audio filter within Kdenlive to strip away unwanted low-end wind frequencies while preserving the clarity and natural tone of your spoken audio.

Step 1: Open Your Project and Select the Audio Clip

  1. Launch Kdenlive and open the project containing your dialogue.
  2. If your audio is embedded in a video clip, you can apply the effect directly to the video clip or right-click the clip on the timeline and choose Split Audio to manage the track independently.
  3. Click on the dialogue clip in the timeline to select it.

Step 2: Locate the High-Pass Audio Filter

  1. Go to the Effects panel (usually docked next to the Project Bin; if not visible, enable it via View > Effects).
  2. Click the Audio tab or use the search bar at the top of the Effects panel.
  3. Type High Pass into the search box.
  4. Locate the High Pass (or High Pass Filter) effect from the results.

Step 3: Apply the Filter to the Clip or Track

  1. Drag the High Pass effect directly onto the selected audio clip on the timeline.
  2. Alternatively, if all clips on a specific track suffer from wind noise, you can drag the effect onto the Track Header to apply it across the entire track.

Step 4: Configure the Filter Settings

  1. Open the Effect/Composition Stack panel to view the controls for the High-Pass filter.
  2. Locate the Cutoff Frequency (sometimes labeled Frequency).
  3. Set the initial cutoff value:
    • 80 Hz – 100 Hz: Ideal for light wind noise; removes sub-audible rumble without affecting deep vocal tones.
    • 120 Hz – 160 Hz: Standard range for moderate wind noise; removes most bass-heavy rumble while keeping standard speech clear.
    • Up to 200 Hz: Best for aggressive wind noise; use cautiously, as frequencies above 180 Hz may cause male voices to sound slightly thin.

Step 5: Preview and Fine-Tune

  1. Play the timeline audio while looping a section with both speech and wind noise.
  2. Slowly adjust the cutoff slider up until the wind rumble disappears, then stop before the speaker’s voice begins to lose warmth and body.
  3. Toggle the effect on and off using the eye or power icon in the Effect Stack panel to compare the filtered audio against the original.
