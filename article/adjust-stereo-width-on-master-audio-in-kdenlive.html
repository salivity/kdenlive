Adjust Stereo Width on Master Audio in Kdenlive

Adjusting the stereo width on the master audio track in Kdenlive allows you to control the perceived spatial separation of your entire mix before exporting. This process involves applying a spatial audio effect to the global audio output and fine-tuning the width parameters to achieve either a wider soundstage or a more focused, centered sound.

1. Access the Master Audio Track

To apply an effect across the entire project rather than individual clips or tracks, you must select the Master track:

  1. Ensure the Audio Mixer or Timeline is visible.
  2. Click on the Master audio track header located at the bottom or left of the timeline tracks, or select the Master channel in the Audio Mixer dock.
  3. Open the Effects panel (go to View > Effects if it is not open).

2. Apply a Spatial Audio Filter

Kdenlive provides several built-in audio effects and LADSPA plugins for spatial manipulation:

  1. In the Effects tab, navigate to the Audio category or use the search bar.
  2. Search for spatial effects such as Stereo Width, Stereo Tools, or Matrix Spatialiser.
  3. Drag the filter directly onto the Master track, or double-click the effect while the Master track is selected.

3. Adjust the Stereo Width Parameter

Once the effect appears in the Effect / Composition Stack panel for the Master track:

  1. Locate the Width (or Stereo Width) slider.
  2. Standard Stereo (Default/100%): Leaves the original balance and separation untouched.
  3. Wider Soundstage (>100%): Increases separation between the left and right channels, creating a broader, more immersive environment.
  4. Narrower Soundstage (<100%): Blends the left and right channels closer together. Setting this to 0% produces a pure mono output.

4. Monitor and Prevent Phase Issues

When expanding stereo width on the master track, always monitor your audio to ensure phase coherence:

  • Listen through both headphones and studio monitors to check for unnatural hollow sounds or frequency dropouts.
  • Test your mix in mono to verify that widening the stereo field does not cause phase cancellation, which can make central elements like dialogue or bass disappear.
