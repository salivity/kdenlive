How to Use Audio Reverb for Foley in Kdenlive

Integrating Foley sound effects into video requires matching the audio's acoustic space with the on-screen environment. In Kdenlive, the built-in Audio Reverb effects allow you to transform dry studio recordings into natural sounds that convincingly inhabit small rooms, vast halls, or outdoor locations. This guide outlines the exact steps to apply and configure reverb on your Foley tracks to achieve a cohesive, realistic mix.

Step 1: Add Foley to the Timeline

  1. Import your dry Foley audio clip into the Project Bin.
  2. Drag the audio clip onto an audio track directly below the corresponding video action.
  3. Use the timeline zoom to precisely align the sound effect (such as footsteps, cloth rustle, or object impacts) with the visual cue.

Step 2: Apply the Reverb Effect

  1. Open the Effects tab (typically located in the bottom-left panel or accessible via Menu > Settings > Layouts).
  2. Navigate to the Audio Correction or Audio Effects category.
  3. Locate the Reverb (or Freeverb) effect.
  4. Drag and drop the effect directly onto the Foley audio clip on the timeline, or onto the entire audio track header if you want all sounds on that track to share the same room acoustics.

Step 3: Configure Effect Parameters

Select the clip to open its settings in the Properties (or Effect/Composition Stack) panel. Adjust the core parameters to simulate the physical space seen on screen:

  • Room Size: Controls the perceived dimensions of the virtual space. Use low values (0.1–0.3) for small bedrooms or car interiors, medium values (0.4–0.6) for standard offices or classrooms, and high values (0.7–1.0) for warehouses, churches, or canyons.
  • Damp / Damping: Simulates how surfaces absorb high frequencies. Increase damping for rooms with soft materials (carpets, curtains, furniture) to create a warmer sound, or decrease it for reflective surfaces (tile, concrete, metal) for a brighter, echo-heavy sound.
  • Wet Level: Controls the volume of the reverberated sound. Keep this subtle (typically between 10% and 30%) so the original sound remains intelligible.
  • Dry Level: Controls the volume of the direct, unaffected sound. Keep this high (around 80% to 100%) to preserve the clarity and punch of the Foley action.
  • Width: Adjusts the stereo spread of the reverberation. Set higher for immersive wide spaces or lower for focused, narrow points of origin.

Step 4: Blend and Check the Mix

  1. Play back the sequence with the dialogue, Foley, and background ambience active.
  2. Ensure the Foley does not sound detached from the scene; adjust the Wet Level until the sound blends seamlessly with the room's visual depth.
  3. Check the Master Audio Mixer to ensure the added reverb tail does not cause audio clipping or overpower critical dialogue.
