How to Create Soft-Focus Diffusion in Kdenlive

A soft-focus diffusion effect creates a dreamy, cinematic glow by blooming the highlights of your video while maintaining sharp underlying details. In Kdenlive, you can achieve this classic aesthetic by duplicating your footage across two video tracks, applying a strong blur to the upper track, and blending it over the original footage using specific blend modes and opacity adjustments.

Step 1: Duplicate Your Footage

  1. Import your clip and place it onto the lower video track (e.g., V1).
  2. Right-click the clip, select Copy, and paste it directly onto the track above (e.g., V2).
  3. Ensure both clips are perfectly synchronized on the timeline so they align frame-for-frame.

Step 2: Apply a Blur Effect

  1. Select the clip on the upper track (V2).
  2. Open the Effects tab and search for a blur filter, such as Gaussian Blur or Dual Kawase Blur.
  3. Drag and drop the blur effect onto the V2 clip.
  4. Increase the blur radius significantly. The size of the blur determines the spread and softness of the diffusion glow.

Step 3: Blend the Tracks

  1. Select the top clip on V2 and go to the Effects panel to add a Composite and Transform effect (or use the built-in track compositing feature).
  2. In the effect settings, locate the Compositing / Blend Mode dropdown.
  3. Select an appropriate blending mode based on your desired look:
    • Screen: Ideal for creating a bright, glowing, and ethereal bloom across bright areas.
    • Soft Light: Adds subtle contrast along with a gentle, natural diffusion.
    • Overlay: Produces a more dramatic, high-contrast glow.

Step 4: Isolate the Highlights (Optional)

To prevent dark areas and midtones from becoming muddy:

  1. Add a Brightness and Contrast or Color Levels effect to the blurred V2 clip above the blur effect in the stack.
  2. Crush the shadows by raising the input black level or lowering the brightness, leaving only the brightest highlights visible to be blurred and blended.

Step 5: Adjust the Effect Intensity

  1. In the Composite and Transform effect on the V2 clip, locate the Opacity slider.
  2. Lower the opacity from 100% down to between 20% and 50% to subtly integrate the diffusion without overpowering the original sharpness of the base footage on V1.
