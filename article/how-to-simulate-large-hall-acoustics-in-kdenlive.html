How to Simulate Large Hall Acoustics in Kdenlive

Simulating large hall acoustics in Kdenlive is a straightforward process that transforms dry audio recordings into rich, spacious soundscapes. By applying an audio reverb effect and fine-tuning parameters such as room size, decay time, and wet/dry balance, you can create the realistic impression of a concert hall, cathedral, or large auditorium. This guide outlines the exact steps required to locate, apply, and configure the audio reverb filter in Kdenlive.

Step 1: Place Your Audio on the Timeline

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive.
  2. Drag your audio or video clip from the Project Bin onto an audio track on the Timeline.

Step 2: Locate the Reverb Effect

  1. Open the Effects tab (typically located in the central or left dock; if missing, enable it via View > Effects).
  2. Click the Audio Effects icon or select the audio category.
  3. In the search bar at the top of the Effects panel, type Reverb (or look for Freeverb / Plate Reverb, depending on your installed MLT/LADSPA plugins).

Step 3: Apply the Effect to the Audio

  1. Click and drag the Reverb (or Freeverb) effect directly onto your audio clip on the timeline.
  2. Alternatively, drag the effect onto the audio track header if you want the large hall acoustic applied to all clips on that track.

Step 4: Configure Parameters for Large Hall Acoustics

Select the clip on the timeline, then go to the Effect/Composition Stack panel to adjust the settings:

  • Room Size: Set between 75% and 90%. A higher room size extends the physical boundaries of the simulated space, creating the distinct echo characteristics of an expansive hall.
  • Damping (HF Damping): Set around 30% to 50%. Lower damping lets high frequencies bounce longer, simulating hard reflective surfaces like stone, marble, or polished wood commonly found in grand halls.
  • Pre-Delay: Set between 20 ms and 50 ms (if available). Adding a slight pre-delay separates the direct sound from the initial reflections, reinforcing the perception of distance between the sound source and surrounding walls.
  • Decay Time / Reverb Time: Increase to 2.0 – 3.5 seconds to allow sound tails to linger naturally across the space.
  • Wet / Dry Balance:
    • Set Dry Level to 80% – 100% to keep the original dialogue or music legible.
    • Set Wet Level to 25% – 40%. Increase the wet mix if you want the sound source to appear farther away from the listener.

Step 5: Preview and Fine-Tune

  1. Play back the timeline around your audio clip to hear the processed sound.
  2. Make minor adjustments to the Wet Level to ensure the reverb does not overwhelm the clarity of the primary audio.
  3. Once satisfied with the acoustic depth, continue editing or proceed to render your project.
