How to Apply Wave Effect in Kdenlive

The Wave effect in Kdenlive allows video editors to distort footage into undulating, fluid patterns that mimic the natural movement of water. By adjusting parameters such as amplitude, wavelength, and speed, you can create subtle ripples or intense aquatic distortions. This guide provides a direct, step-by-step walkthrough to help you locate, apply, and fine-tune the Wave effect on any video track in Kdenlive.

Step 1: Add Footage to the Timeline

  1. Launch Kdenlive and open your project.
  2. Import your video clip into the Project Bin.
  3. Drag and drop the clip onto a video track in the Timeline.

Step 2: Locate the Wave Effect

  1. Open the Effects tab (typically located in the workspace panels; if not visible, enable it via View > Effects).
  2. Type Wave into the search bar at the top of the Effects panel.
  3. Locate the Wave effect listed under the distortion/transform categories.

Step 3: Apply the Effect to the Clip

  1. Drag the Wave effect directly onto the clip in the timeline, or select the clip in the timeline and double-click the effect.
  2. The effect will now appear in the Effect/Composition Stack panel for that specific clip.

Step 4: Adjust the Wave Parameters

Select the clip to view its settings in the Properties / Effect Stack panel and modify the following controls to create your desired water pattern:

  • Amplitude: Controls the height and intensity of the wave crests. Higher values produce dramatic warping, while lower values generate subtle surface ripples.
  • Wavelength / Frequency: Dictates the distance between individual wave peaks. Lower wavelength values increase the number of ripples across the screen.
  • Speed / Phase: Determines how quickly the wave moves across the image over time. Increasing this value makes the water appear more turbulent.
  • Direction / Deform: Sets whether the distortion occurs horizontally, vertically, or in circular patterns across the frame.

Step 5: Animate with Keyframes (Optional)

  1. Click the clock icon next to a parameter to enable keyframing.
  2. Move the playhead to the beginning of the clip and set an initial value (such as a low amplitude).
  3. Move the playhead forward in time and increase the parameter values to create an evolving water surge or transition.

Step 6: Preview and Render

  1. Play back the timeline in the Clip Monitor or Project Monitor to evaluate the motion.
  2. Lower the playback resolution if the real-time preview lags due to distortion processing.
  3. Once satisfied with the water effect, click Render in the top toolbar to export your final video.
