Remove Background Noise in Kdenlive Using Audio Gate

This guide explains how to use the Audio Gate effect in Kdenlive to eliminate unwanted ambient background noise, room hum, and microphone hiss during pauses in dialogue. By configuring the gate threshold and release settings, you can automatically mute the audio signal whenever the speaker is silent, resulting in a cleaner and more professional mix.

Step 1: Add the Audio Gate Effect to Your Track

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and place your audio or video clip on the timeline.
  2. Open the Effects panel (go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  3. In the search bar of the Effects panel, type Audio Gate or Gate.
  4. Drag and drop the Audio Gate effect directly onto your audio clip, or apply it to the entire audio track header if you want it to affect all clips on that track.

Step 2: Identify the Ambient Noise Level

  1. Play a section of your timeline where there is no speaking, only background room noise.
  2. Observe the audio mixer meters to determine the decibel (dB) level of the ambient noise (usually between -40 dB and -60 dB).
  3. Play a section where the person is speaking quietly to find the lowest speech level.

Step 3: Configure the Gate Parameters

Select the clip and navigate to the Effect/Composition Stack panel to adjust the following settings:

  • Threshold: Set this slider slightly higher than your ambient noise level, but lower than the quietest parts of the spoken dialogue. For example, if room noise sits at -45 dB and speech starts at -25 dB, set the threshold around -38 dB to -35 dB.
  • Attack: Set this to a low value (between 1 ms and 10 ms). A fast attack ensures the gate opens instantly when the speaker begins talking, preventing the first syllables of words from being cut off.
  • Hold / Release (Decay): Set the release time between 100 ms and 300 ms. A gradual release prevents the audio from abruptly cutting off trailing word sounds, breaths, or natural sentence endings.
  • Range / Reduction: Adjust how much the volume drops when the gate closes. Setting this to full attenuation (-∞ dB) will create complete silence, while a moderate reduction (such as -18 dB to -24 dB) provides a more natural-sounding reduction of room tone.

Step 4: Preview and Fine-Tune

  1. Play back the entire dialogue passage to check for unnatural cutting or "breathing" artifacts.
  2. If the beginning of words are cut off, lower the Threshold or decrease the Attack time.
  3. If the gate cuts off the ends of sentences too abruptly, increase the Release time.
  4. If room noise cuts in and out too aggressively, reduce the overall Reduction amount for a smoother transition between speech and silence.
