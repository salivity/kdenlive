How to Import 3D LUTs in Kdenlive

Applying Look-Up Tables (LUTs) is a crucial step in color grading video footage to achieve a professional, cinematic look. This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step walkthrough on how to import and apply standard 3D LUT files (such as .cube files) using Kdenlive's built-in LUT 3D application tool.

Step 1: Prepare Your 3D LUT File

Before starting, ensure your 3D LUT file is in a compatible format. Kdenlive primarily supports the .cube format, which is the industry standard for 3D LUTs. Save your LUT files in an easily accessible folder on your computer.

Step 2: Add Your Video Clip to the Timeline

  1. Open Kdenlive and import your video footage into the Project Bin.
  2. Drag and drop the video clip from the Project Bin onto your timeline.

Step 3: Locate and Apply the "LUT 3D" Effect

  1. Open the Effects tab (if it is not visible, enable it via View > Effects in the top menu).
  2. In the search bar of the Effects panel, type LUT 3D.
  3. Drag the LUT 3D effect from the search results and drop it directly onto your video clip in the timeline.

Step 4: Load the LUT File into Kdenlive

  1. Click on the video clip in your timeline to select it.
  2. Navigate to the Effect/Composition Stack panel (usually located in the top-right or center-left of the interface) to view the LUT 3D effect settings.
  3. Click the file browser icon (the folder symbol) next to the "LUT file" path field.
  4. Locate your .cube file in the file explorer, select it, and click Open.

The color grade will immediately apply to your clip in the Project Monitor.

Step 5: Configure the Effect Settings

To get the best visual results, you can adjust the following settings within the LUT 3D effect panel:

  • Interpolation: Set this to Tetrahedral for the highest quality color mapping, or Trilinear if you need to prioritize system rendering performance.
  • Amount: Use this slider (if available in your Kdenlive version) to adjust the intensity of the LUT, allowing you to blend the graded look with the original footage.
