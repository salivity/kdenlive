How to Fix Barrel Distortion in Kdenlive

Ultrawide lenses and action cameras often introduce curved lines along the edges of your frame, commonly known as barrel or fisheye distortion. This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step method for removing barrel distortion from your footage inside Kdenlive using built-in video correction effects.

Method 1: Using the Lens Correction Effect

The Lens Correction effect is the standard tool in Kdenlive for manually flattening curved wide-angle footage.

  1. Add Your Clip to the Timeline: Import your wide-angle footage into your Kdenlive project and drag it onto an active video track.
  2. Open the Effects Panel: Go to the Effects tab (typically located alongside the Project Bin or accessible via View > Effects).
  3. Locate the Effect: Search for Lens Correction in the search bar (found under the Transform, Distort and Perspective category).
  4. Apply the Effect: Drag and drop the Lens Correction effect directly onto the video clip in your timeline.
  5. Adjust the Settings in Effect Stack:
    • Center X / Center Y: Keep these at 500 (or 0.5 depending on your version) to keep the distortion centered, unless your lens is off-axis.
    • Correction: Adjust this slider to reverse the barrel distortion. For barrel distortion, adjust the slider to bend the edges outward until straight lines (such as horizons, walls, or doorframes) appear flat.
    • Correction (Edges): Fine-tune the extreme outer borders of the frame.
  6. Compensate for Empty Edges: Correcting distortion often causes black curved borders to appear around the edges. To fix this, adjust the Zoom parameter inside the Lens Correction effect, or add a separate Transform effect to scale the footage up slightly until the borders are hidden.

Method 2: Using the Defish Effect

If you are working with extreme fisheye footage from action cameras like GoPros:

  1. Search for Defish in the Effects panel.
  2. Apply the effect to your timeline clip.
  3. Select the appropriate projection type (such as Linear or Equidistant) that matches your camera's lens profile.
  4. Tweak the Amount / Field of View (FOV) slider until the fisheye curvature is neutralized.

Quick Tips for Best Results

  • Use a Reference Line: Look for vertical or horizontal objects in the background (like buildings or horizon lines) as a visual reference while adjusting the sliders.
  • Save as Custom Effect: Once you find the exact values that correct your specific lens, click the Save Effect icon in the Effect Stack panel to create a preset for future footage shot on the same camera.
