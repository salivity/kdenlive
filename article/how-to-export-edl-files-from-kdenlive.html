How to Export EDL Files from Kdenlive

Exporting an Edit Decision List (EDL) from Kdenlive allows editors to transfer timeline cut data, reel names, and timecode references to other digital audio workstations or non-linear editors like DaVinci Resolve and Premiere Pro. While the standard EDL format historically focuses on single-track edits, Kdenlive provides tools to output edit data from complex timelines. The following guide outlines the exact steps required to prepare, configure, and export an EDL file with multi-track cut references in Kdenlive.

Step 1: Prepare the Multi-Track Timeline

Before generating an EDL, ensure your timeline is organized to prevent reference conflicts:

  • Flatten or Label Tracks: Ensure each video and audio track is clearly organized. Standard EDL specifications (such as CMX 3600) typically read one video track and up to four audio channels per event.
  • Unmute and Unlock: Ensure all tracks you want to export are unmuted and unlocked. Hidden or muted tracks will be ignored during the export process.
  • Verify Timecodes: Go to Project > Project Settings and verify that your project frame rate matches the intended delivery frame rate.

Step 2: Open the EDL Export Interface

  1. Click on the Project menu in the top menu bar.
  2. Select Export EDL (or navigate to File > Export > Export EDL depending on your Kdenlive version).

Step 3: Configure Export and Track Settings

In the export dialogue box:

  • Track Selection: Choose whether to export specific tracks or the active sequence. If you require cut references across multiple video or audio tracks, select the relevant track layers.
  • Format Specification: Select the target EDL standard (commonly CMX 3600) to ensure broad compatibility with other NLEs.
  • Timecode and Frame Rate: Verify that the project start timecode (e.g., 00:00:00:00 or 01:00:00:00) matches your master reference.

Step 4: Export and Save the File

  1. Click the Browse or File Path button to choose the destination folder.
  2. Enter a filename with the .edl extension.
  3. Click Save or Export to generate the file.

Step 5: Verify the EDL Data

Open the generated .edl file in a plain text editor to ensure that cuts across your tracks have been properly indexed. Each cut event should list the event number, reel name, track identifier (e.g., V, A, A2), transition type, source in/out timecodes, and timeline in/out timecodes.
