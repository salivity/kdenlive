How to Apply Luma Key in Kdenlive for Overlays

This guide provides a straightforward walkthrough on applying a luma key effect to composite overlay animations in Kdenlive. By isolating luminance values—typically removing solid black or white backgrounds—you can cleanly blend graphic elements, matte transitions, and animated effects over your base video tracks without requiring an alpha channel.

1. Arrange Your Timeline Tracks

  1. Open Kdenlive and ensure your project contains at least two video tracks.
  2. Place your main background video footage on the lower track (e.g., Video 1).
  3. Place your overlay animation clip directly above it on the upper track (e.g., Video 2).

2. Add the Luma Key Effect

  1. Open the Effects tab in the interface (or select Menu > View > Effects if not visible).
  2. Search for Luma Key or Luminance Key in the search bar.
  3. Drag and drop the effect directly onto the overlay clip on the upper track.

3. Configure the Keying Parameters

  1. Select the overlay clip to reveal its settings in the Effect/Composition Stack panel.
  2. Adjust the threshold sliders based on your overlay type:
    • Black Background Overlays: Increase the Low Threshold until the dark areas become completely transparent, leaving only the bright animated elements visible.
    • White Background Overlays: Decrease the High Threshold (or enable the Invert option) to eliminate white areas while preserving dark animation elements.
  3. Adjust the Slope or Softness parameter to smooth out harsh pixel edges and prevent color fringing along the borders of the animation.

4. Refine Alignment and Compositing

  1. If the overlay animation requires resizing or repositioning, add a Transform effect below the Luma Key effect in the stack.
  2. Use the on-screen handles or coordinate controls within the Transform effect to scale and position the overlay as desired.
  3. If necessary, adjust the overall opacity to blend the animation naturally into the background scene.

5. Preview and Render

  1. Move the timeline playhead to the beginning of the overlay clip and press the Spacebar to preview the composite in real-time.
  2. Make any final threshold tweaks to eliminate noise or flickering in semi-transparent zones.
  3. Click the Render button in the main toolbar to export your final composited video.
