How to Use Kdenlive Chroma Hold to Isolate Color

The Chroma Hold effect in Kdenlive allows you to create a selective color effect—often referred to as a "color splash"—by preserving a specific primary color while converting the rest of your video frame to grayscale. This guide provides a direct, step-by-step walkthrough on how to locate the Chroma Hold effect, pick the target color from your footage, and adjust its parameters to achieve a clean isolation effect.

Step 1: Add Your Clip to the Timeline

  1. Open Kdenlive and import your video clip into the Project Bin.
  2. Drag and drop the clip onto a video track in the Timeline.

Step 2: Apply the Chroma Hold Effect

  1. Open the Effects panel (go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  2. Use the search bar to look up Chroma Hold, or navigate to the Color and Image correction category.
  3. Drag the Chroma Hold effect directly onto your video clip in the timeline, or double-click the effect with the clip selected.

Step 3: Pick the Target Color

  1. Select the clip in the timeline and open the Effect/Composition Stack panel.
  2. Locate the Chroma Hold settings.
  3. Click the Color Picker (eyedropper icon) next to the Color property.
  4. Click on the Project Monitor to sample the exact color you want to keep visible (for example, a red dress, a yellow taxi, or blue eyes).

Step 4: Adjust the Variance

  1. Locate the Variance slider beneath the color swatch.
  2. Drag the slider to expand or restrict the range of colors detected:
    • Increase Variance: Keeps a wider range of similar shades and tints of your chosen color.
    • Decrease Variance: Restricts the effect to only the exact shade sampled, turning everything else to black and white.
  3. Adjust the slider until the subject is fully colored and unwanted background elements remain completely desaturated.

Step 5: Clean Up Artifacts

If non-target areas with similar color values bleed through:

  • Use the Color Picker to re-sample a more representative pixel from your subject.
  • Lower the Variance slightly to eliminate background noise.
  • If necessary, apply a mask or use the Rotoscoping effect on a duplicate track to isolate the subject if the background shares the exact same hue.
