Create a Sin City Color Effect in Kdenlive

This article provides a step-by-step guide on how to isolate a single vibrant color while turning the rest of your video black and white using the "Color Hold" effect in Kdenlive. This popular technique mimics the iconic, high-contrast visual style of the film Sin City, allowing you to draw immediate viewer attention to specific subjects like a red dress, bright yellow umbrella, or glowing neon sign.

Step 1: Prepare Your Footage

Import your video clip into Kdenlive and drag it onto the timeline. For the best results, choose footage that features a highly saturated, distinct color against a relatively neutral background. If the color you want to keep is too similar to other elements in the frame, the effect may bleed into unwanted areas.

Step 2: Apply the Color Hold Effect

  1. Navigate to the Effects tab (usually located in the bottom-left or top-left panel of your workspace).
  2. In the search bar, type Color Hold.
  3. Drag and drop the "Color Hold" effect directly onto your video clip in the timeline.

Step 3: Select the Target Color

Once the effect is applied, look at the Properties panel (usually on the right side of the screen) to adjust the effect settings:

  1. Locate the Color parameter, which features a color preview box and an eyedropper icon.
  2. Click the eyedropper tool.
  3. Move your cursor to the Project Monitor (the video preview window) and click directly on the color you want to isolate (for example, the red lips or a blue tie).

The rest of the video should immediately turn grayscale, leaving only your chosen color in its original hue.

Step 4: Adjust the Tolerances

Rarely will a single click capture the color perfectly. You will need to fine-tune the parameters in the Properties panel:

  • Color Distance (or Tolerance): Drag this slider to the right to include more shades of your chosen color, or to the left to restrict it. Adjust this until the entire subject is colored without letting the color bleed into the background.
  • Softness: Use this slider to smooth the transition edges between the isolated color and the black-and-white background, preventing harsh, pixelated borders.

Step 5: Enhance the Contrast (Optional)

To truly capture the gritty, graphic-novel aesthetic of Sin City, the black-and-white portions of your video should have high contrast.

  1. Search for the Lift/Gamma/Gain or Curves effect in the Effects tab.
  2. Drag it onto your clip below the Color Hold effect in the stack.
  3. Increase the shadows (Lift) and boost the highlights (Gain) to create deep blacks and bright whites, making your isolated color pop even more dramatically.
