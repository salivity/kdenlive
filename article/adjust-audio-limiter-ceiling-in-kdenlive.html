Adjust Audio Limiter Ceiling in Kdenlive

Digital audio clipping occurs when signal levels exceed 0 dBFS, resulting in harsh distortion and degraded audio quality in video projects. In Kdenlive, you can eliminate this issue by applying an Audio Limiter effect to individual clips, entire tracks, or the master output and configuring its ceiling parameter. This guide walks you through locating the limiter effect, adjusting the ceiling to a safe threshold, and verifying your audio levels to ensure a clean, professional mix.

Understanding the Ceiling (Limit) Parameter

The ceiling—often labeled as Limit or Ceiling in Kdenlive's audio plugins—defines the absolute maximum peak level that the audio signal is permitted to reach. By setting this threshold just below 0 dBFS (typically between -1.0 dB and -0.1 dB), you create headroom that prevents both direct digital clipping and inter-sample peaks during export and playback compression.

Step 1: Add the Limiter Effect

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive.
  2. Decide where to apply the effect:
    • Individual Clip: Click on the specific audio or video clip in the timeline.
    • Track/Master Output (Recommended): Click on the track header or open the Master effects panel to apply the limiter to the entire project mix.
  3. Open the Effects panel (go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  4. Search for Limiter in the search bar.
  5. Drag and drop the Limiter (or Fast Limiter) effect onto your selected clip, track, or the Master stack.

Step 2: Configure the Ceiling / Limit Parameter

  1. Open the Effect/Composition Stack panel to view the active limiter settings.
  2. Locate the parameter labeled Limit, Ceiling, or Output Limit depending on the specific limiter plugin installed (such as LADSPA or Calf limiters).
  3. Set the ceiling value to -1.0 dB (or -0.5 dB for general video platforms):
    • Type the numerical value directly into the input field for precision, or drag the slider.
    • A setting of -1.0 dB provides adequate safety margin against true-peak distortion after rendering to formats like AAC or MP3.
  4. Set the Release time (if available) between 50 ms and 100 ms to maintain a natural dynamic response without pumping artifacts.

Step 3: Test and Monitor Playback

  1. Play the loudest sections of your timeline.
  2. Observe the Audio Signal meter in Kdenlive.
  3. Verify that the audio meters do not reach 0 dBFS and turn red.
  4. If the audio sounds overly compressed or quiet, reduce any pre-gain applied before the limiter rather than raising the ceiling beyond -0.1 dB.
