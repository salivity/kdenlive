Apply Glitch Filter with Frequency in Kdenlive

This guide explains how to apply and customize the Glitch video effect in Kdenlive to create stylized visual distortions. By adjusting parameters such as frequency, block height, and chromatic shifting, you can control the intensity and timing of visual artifacts across your video clips. You will also learn how to use keyframes to modulate disruption frequency dynamically for transitions or targeted visual effects.

Step 1: Add Your Clip to the Timeline

  1. Launch Kdenlive and open your project.
  2. Drag your video footage from the Project Bin onto a video track in the Timeline.
  3. Select the clip on the timeline so it is highlighted.

Step 2: Locate and Apply the Glitch Effect

  1. Open the Effects panel (go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  2. Type Glitch into the search bar, or navigate to the Stylize or Distort categories.
  3. Drag the Glitch effect directly onto the selected clip in the timeline, or double-click the effect to apply it.

Step 3: Configure Frequency Disruptions

With the clip selected, look at the Effect/Composition Stack panel to adjust the Glitch properties:

  • Frequency: Controls how often visual disruptions occur. A lower value produces occasional, subtle glitches, while a high value creates continuous, aggressive distortion.
  • Block Height / Horizontal Shift: Determines the scale of the distorted blocks and how far image slices shift horizontally across the frame.
  • Color / Chromatic Shift: Separates the RGB color channels during disruptions to simulate analog signal loss or digital video tearing.

Set the Frequency slider to match the desired rhythm and severity of the artifacts for your scene.

Step 4: Parameterize Frequency with Keyframes

To create varying frequency disruptions over time rather than a static glitch:

  1. Click the Clock / Keyframe icon next to the Glitch effect parameters in the Effect Stack to enable the keyframe timeline.
  2. Move the playhead to the point where you want the disruption to begin.
  3. Add a keyframe and set the Frequency to a baseline level (e.g., 0 for no distortion).
  4. Advance the playhead to the peak moment of the disruption, add another keyframe, and increase the Frequency to maximum.
  5. Move forward a few frames and add a final keyframe, returning the Frequency back to 0 to restore normal playback.

Step 5: Preview and Refine

  1. Use the Project Monitor to preview the glitch timing in real time.
  2. Fine-tune keyframe positions and parameter values until the frequency disruption aligns precisely with your video's audio cues or scene transitions.
