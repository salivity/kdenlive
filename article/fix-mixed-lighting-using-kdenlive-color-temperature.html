Fix Mixed Lighting Using Kdenlive Color Temperature

This guide explains how to correct footage shot under mixed tungsten and daylight lighting conditions using Kdenlive. By applying and adjusting the Color Temperature effect, along with secondary masking techniques, you can neutralize unwanted color casts and create a natural, cohesive look across your scene.

Understanding Mixed Lighting in Video

Mixed lighting occurs when your scene contains light sources with different color temperatures—typically warm tungsten light (around 3200K) and cool daylight (around 5600K). This creates contrasting orange and blue color casts in different parts of your frame. Kdenlive provides built-in color correction tools to help balance these discrepancies.

Step 1: Add Your Clip to the Timeline

  1. Open Kdenlive and import your video clip into the Project Bin.
  2. Drag and drop the clip onto a video track in the Timeline.
  3. Select the clip by clicking on it.

Step 2: Apply the Color Temperature Effect

  1. Navigate to the Effects tab in the interface (or go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  2. In the search bar at the top of the Effects panel, type Color Temperature.
  3. Locate the Color Temperature effect under the Color and Image Correction category.
  4. Drag the effect onto your clip in the timeline, or double-click the effect while the clip is selected.

Step 3: Adjust Global Color Balance

  1. With the clip selected, open the Effect/Composition Stack panel to view the Color Temperature settings.
  2. Locate the Temperature slider:
    • If the overall scene is too orange/warm (tungsten dominant): Shift the temperature slider toward the cooler (blue) end to neutralize the warm cast.
    • If the overall scene is too blue/cold (daylight dominant): Shift the temperature slider toward the warmer (orange) end.
  3. Use the Vectorscope and RGB Parade (found under View > Vectorscope / RGB Parade) to visually confirm that skin tones align along the standard skin-tone line and that neutral whites are evenly distributed.

Step 4: Handle Split Mixed Lighting with Masks

When one side of the frame is heavily illuminated by tungsten light and the other by daylight, a single global adjustment will not fix the issue. Use masking to balance the regions independently:

  1. Balance the entire image for the primary light source using the first Color Temperature effect.
  2. Add a Rotoscoping mask effect above a second Color Temperature effect on the same clip:
    • In the Effects panel, search for and apply Rotoscoping.
    • Draw a mask around the area illuminated by the secondary light source (e.g., the tungsten-lit portion of the subject).
    • Adjust the Feather settings in the Rotoscoping panel to create a smooth, invisible transition between the areas.
  3. Apply a second Color Temperature effect below the Rotoscoping mask and adjust the slider specifically to balance the masked area with the rest of the frame.
