Adjust Threshold and Softness in Kdenlive Chroma Key

The "Chroma Key: Advanced" effect in Kdenlive provides precise control for removing green or blue screen backgrounds from video footage. Adjusting the threshold and softness parameters is crucial for achieving a clean matte, preventing jagged edges, and ensuring the subject remains fully opaque while the background becomes entirely transparent. This guide walks you through the step-by-step process of configuring these two key parameters to get optimal results in your video projects.

Step 1: Apply the Chroma Key: Advanced Effect

  1. Drag and drop your green or blue screen clip onto a video track in the Kdenlive timeline.
  2. Go to the Effects panel, search for Chroma Key: Advanced, and drag it onto your clip.
  3. Open the Properties / Effect Stack panel to view the effect's controls.
  4. Use the color picker (eyedropper tool) under the effect settings to click on the most representative area of your background color in the preview monitor.

Step 2: Adjust the Threshold Parameter

The Threshold (often referred to as tolerance or distance) defines how wide of a color range around your selected key color will be removed.

  1. Locate the Threshold or Color Distance slider in the effect settings.
  2. Gradually increase the value if parts of the background remain visible.
  3. If parts of your subject begin to turn transparent, decrease the value until the subject is fully solid.
  4. To verify accuracy, switch the output view mode in the effect settings to Alpha or Black and White (if available) so you can see a high-contrast matte: the subject should be solid white and the background solid black.

Step 3: Adjust the Softness Parameter

The Softness (or slope) parameter controls the transition area between the keyed background and the opaque subject, effectively feathering the edges.

  1. Locate the Softness slider directly below or adjacent to the threshold control.
  2. Increase the softness slightly to smooth out pixelated, harsh, or jagged borders around your subject.
  3. Keep the value relatively low; setting the softness too high will cause the edges of your subject to appear semi-transparent and blurry, allowing the background to bleed through.
  4. Switch the view mode back to the normal composite output to ensure the edges blend naturally over your background layer.

Step 4: Fine-Tune for Best Results

  • Always set the Threshold first to establish a solid matte, and use Softness second to refine the perimeter.
  • If uneven lighting creates noise in the background, make small incremental adjustments to the threshold rather than relying solely on softness.
