How to Adjust Kdenlive Audio Levels with Shortcuts

This article explains how to configure custom keyboard shortcuts in Kdenlive to rapidly adjust audio track and clip levels directly from your timeline. By mapping dedicated keys to volume commands, you can streamline your audio editing workflow and make real-time gain adjustments without manually opening menus or adjusting effect sliders for every clip.

Step 1: Open the Keyboard Shortcut Manager

  1. Launch Kdenlive and open your project.
  2. Click on Settings in the top menu bar.
  3. Select Configure Keyboard Shortcuts... (or press Ctrl + Alt + , depending on your operating system defaults).

Step 2: Search for Audio and Volume Commands

In the search bar at the top of the shortcut configuration window, type one of the following terms to locate audio commands:

  • "Volume" or "Gain": Displays clip-level volume adjustment actions.
  • "Audio Track": Displays track-level controls and mixer parameters.
  • "Fade": Displays quick controls for adding audio fade-ins or fade-outs.

Step 3: Assign Custom Keybindings

  1. Locate the specific action you want to map, such as Increase Volume or Decrease Volume.
  2. Click on the action row to select it.
  3. Select the Custom radio button in the shortcut options at the bottom of the window.
  4. Click the input button (which typically displays None) and press the key combination you wish to use (for example, Alt + Up Arrow to raise volume and Alt + Down Arrow to lower volume).
  5. If the shortcut conflicts with an existing command, Kdenlive will prompt you to reassign or cancel. Confirm the reassignment if desired.

Step 4: Apply and Save Settings

  1. Click OK or Apply to save your custom shortcut mappings.
  2. The shortcut configuration window will close, and your new keybindings will be active immediately.

Step 5: Adjust Audio on the Fly in the Timeline

  1. Select the audio clip or target track in the timeline.
  2. Use your assigned keyboard shortcuts to increment or decrement the audio level in real time.
  3. Monitor your changes using the Audio Mixer panel (View > Audio Mixer) to verify that the decibel (dB) levels are adjusting as expected.
