How to Color Tag Clips in Kdenlive

Organizing your media assets is essential for maintaining an efficient video editing workflow, especially in complex projects. This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step walkthrough on how to assign, customize, and utilize color tags in the Kdenlive Project Bin. By the end of this article, you will be able to visually categorize your footage, audio, and graphics to find exactly what you need in seconds.

Step 1: Locate Your Clip in the Project Bin

Open your project in Kdenlive and ensure the Project Bin panel is visible. If you cannot see it, enable it by going to the top menu and selecting View > Project Bin. Locate the video, audio, or image clip you wish to categorize.

Step 2: Assign a Color Tag

To apply a color tag to a single asset or multiple assets:

  1. Right-click on the clip in the Project Bin.
  2. Hover your cursor over the Color Tag option in the context menu.
  3. Select one of the available colors (such as Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Purple, or Grey) from the submenu.

To tag multiple clips at once, hold down Ctrl (or Cmd on macOS) while clicking the desired clips, right-click, and choose your color.

Step 3: Customize Color Tag Labels

By default, the tags are named after their respective colors. You can customize these labels to represent specific categories (e.g., renaming "Red" to "B-Roll" or "Green" to "A-Roll"):

  1. Go to Settings > Configure Kdenlive (on macOS, go to Kdenlive > Preferences).
  2. In the configuration window, select the Project Bin tab on the left.
  3. Locate the Color Tags section.
  4. Click on the text fields next to each color and type in your custom category names.
  5. Click Apply and then OK to save your changes.

Step 4: Sort and Filter by Color Tags

Once your clips are tagged, you can easily organize your Project Bin using these visual markers. Click on the Column Headers in the Project Bin (such as "Name" or "Date") and ensure the "Color Tag" column is enabled. You can click the Color Tag column header to group all clips of the same color together, making it easy to manage large volumes of media.
