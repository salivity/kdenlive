How to Apply Audio Compressor in Kdenlive

In video editing, unexpected audio spikes can cause clipping, distortion, and an inconsistent listening experience for your audience. This guide explains how to add and configure the built-in Compressor audio effect in Kdenlive, allowing you to tame dynamic range spikes, smooth out speech, and achieve a balanced, professional sound mix across your project.

Step 1: Add the Compressor Effect

  1. Open your Kdenlive project and ensure your audio timeline is visible.
  2. Open the Effects tab (if not visible, go to Settings > Manage Layouts or enable it via View > Effects).
  3. Search for Compressor in the search bar, located under the Audio correction or Audio Effects category.
  4. Drag and drop the Compressor effect directly onto the audio clip with volume spikes.
    • Tip: To apply compression to an entire track instead of individual clips, drag the effect onto the track header on the left side of the timeline.

Step 2: Configure Key Compressor Settings

Select the clip (or track) and look at the Effect/Composition Stack panel to adjust the parameters:

  • Threshold: Sets the decibel (dB) level at which the compressor begins to attenuate audio. Set this just below the peak levels where spikes occur (typically between -18 dB and -12 dB for dialogue).
  • Ratio: Determines how aggressively the volume is reduced once it exceeds the threshold. A ratio between 2:1 and 4:1 works best for natural speech, while 6:1 or higher acts as a limiter for extreme spikes.
  • Attack Time: How quickly the compressor reacts to a volume spike. A fast attack (between 5 ms and 20 ms) catches sudden loud peaks immediately.
  • Release (Decay) Time: How quickly the compressor lets go after the volume drops below the threshold. A setting between 50 ms and 200 ms prevents noticeable "pumping" or unnatural volume breathing.
  • Knee Radius: Controls the smoothness of the transition into compression. A softer knee (higher value) creates a more gradual, transparent effect.
  • Gain / Make-up Gain: Increases the overall volume of the track to compensate for the level lost during peak reduction.

Step 3: Monitor and Refine the Audio Mix

  1. Open the Audio Mixer via View > Audio Mixer.
  2. Play back the section containing the loudest volume spikes.
  3. Observe the audio levels: standard video dialogue should consistently peak between -12 dB and -6 dB without entering the red zone (0 dB).
  4. If dynamic peaks still distort, lower the Threshold or increase the Ratio. If the audio sounds muffled or squashed, raise the threshold or lower the ratio.
