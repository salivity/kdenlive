How to Denoise Video in Kdenlive

Low-light videography often introduces unwanted digital noise and grain, reducing the professional quality of your footage. This guide provides a straightforward, step-by-step walkthrough on how to locate, apply, and configure the denoise effects in Kdenlive to clean up grainy video clips and restore visual clarity to your projects.

Step 1: Import and Add Your Footage to the Timeline

Open Kdenlive and import your noisy, low-light video clip into the Project Bin. Drag and drop the clip onto your video track on the timeline.

Step 2: Locate the Denoise Effect

  1. Navigate to the Effects tab (usually located in the bottom-left or center-left panel). If you do not see it, enable it by going to the top menu and selecting View > Effects.
  2. In the search bar of the Effects panel, type Denoise.
  3. Kdenlive offers a few denoising options depending on your operating system and installed plugins. The most common and reliable built-in effect is hqdn3d (High Quality 3D Denoise). You may also see an effect simply named Denoise.

Step 3: Apply the Effect to Your Clip

Click on the hqdn3d (or Denoise) effect and drag it directly onto your grainy clip on the timeline. Alternatively, select your clip on the timeline first, then double-click the effect in the Effects panel to apply it.

Step 4: Adjust the Denoise Parameters

With the clip selected on the timeline, look at the Properties / Effect Stack panel (usually on the top-right). You will see the parameters for the denoise filter. Adjust the following sliders to balance noise reduction and image sharpness:

  • Spatial Luma (Y): Controls the noise reduction for brightness details within individual frames. Increase this to smooth out the grain, but keep it low enough to avoid making the video look like a blurry painting.
  • Spatial Chroma (U/V): Controls color noise reduction. Low-light footage often has distracting red, green, or blue color specks. Increasing this helps neutralize color noise.
  • Temporal Luma / Chroma: Controls noise reduction by comparing consecutive frames. This is highly effective for static noise but can cause "ghosting" or motion blur on fast-moving objects if set too high.

Step 5: Preview and Render

Denoising is a CPU-intensive process, which may cause your timeline preview to lag.

  1. Use the Timeline Preview rendering feature (press Shift + R after defining a zone) to render a small portion of your timeline for smooth playback.
  2. If the footage looks too soft, slightly lower the Spatial values. If the grain is still highly visible, incrementally raise the Temporal values.
  3. Once satisfied, export your video as usual.
