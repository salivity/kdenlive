Kdenlive Vectorscope for Broadcast Legal Saturation

The Vectorscope display in Kdenlive is an essential color monitoring tool designed to measure hue and chrominance (saturation) within a video signal. This article explains how the Vectorscope functions, how to read its circular graticule to identify illegal color levels, and how video editors use it to maintain broadcast-compliant saturation limits according to international transmission standards.

Understanding the Vectorscope Display

The Vectorscope maps color information visually onto a circular 360-degree plot based on traditional chrominance principles:

  • Hue (Color Tint): The angle around the circle dictates the specific hue. Points distributed radially correlate to primary colors (Red, Green, Blue) and secondary colors (Yellow, Cyan, Magenta).
  • Saturation (Chrominance Intensity): The distance from the absolute center represents the intensity of the color. The center of the circle represents zero saturation (grayscale/monochrome), while the outer edges represent maximum saturation.

Monitoring Broadcast Legal Chrominance

Broadcast standards, such as ITU-R BT.709 and EBU R103, mandate strict boundaries on signal levels to prevent signal distortion, audio buzz, and visual artifacts on legacy transmitters and display equipment.

When monitoring for broadcast legality in Kdenlive, the Vectorscope fulfills several critical functions:

  1. Visualizing Saturation Thresholds: The Vectorscope display contains standard target boxes (graticules) for primary and secondary colors, typically calibrated to 75% or 100% saturation levels. Broadcast compliance generally requires signals to stay within the 75% target boundary markers.
  2. Detecting Over-Saturation: If the color trace expands past the target boundary boxes toward the outer perimeter of the scope, the footage is considered "hot" or illegal. This immediately alerts the editor that chrominance levels will fail quality control checks.
  3. Assessing Skin Tone and White Balance: Alongside saturation limits, the Vectorscope features an integrated skin tone line (I-bar), allowing editors to verify that human skin falls along the correct hue axis regardless of ethnic background, ensuring that saturation adjustments do not compromise natural color rendering.

Practical Workflow in Kdenlive

To ensure video assets are broadcast safe using Kdenlive:

  • Open the Scope: Access the Vectorscope via the top menu by selecting View > Scopes > Vectorscope.
  • Analyze the Trace: Play through the timeline and observe areas where vibrant colors (such as neon lights, bright graphics, or saturated reds) push the trace beyond the target boxes.
  • Correct Chrominance: Apply color correction effects, such as the Saturation, Color Balance, or Lift/Gamma/Gain tools, to pull the trace back inside the permissible target areas.
