Adjusting High-Pass Cutoff Frequency in Kdenlive

Cleaning up low-frequency rumble, wind, and microphone handling noise is essential for clear dialogue in video editing. In Kdenlive, you can easily clean dialogue channels by applying a High-Pass Filter and dialing in the cutoff frequency using the built-in audio effect stack. This guide outlines the exact steps to add the filter to a clip or track and set the ideal cutoff frequency for vocal tracks.

Step 1: Select the Audio Clip or Track

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and navigate to the Timeline.
  2. To apply the effect to a single piece of audio, click directly on the dialogue clip.
  3. If you want the high-pass filter to apply to all dialogue across an entire track, click the Track Header (the area to the left of the timeline for that audio track) to open the track's properties.

Step 2: Add the High-Pass Filter

  1. Open the Effects tab (typically located in the workspace panels; if not visible, enable it via View > Effects).
  2. Click the Audio category icon or use the search bar at the top of the Effects panel.
  3. Search for High Pass (or High-pass Filter).
  4. Drag and drop the filter onto your selected clip or double-click it while the clip/track is selected.

Step 3: Access the Effect Stack

  1. Open the Effect/Composition Stack panel (found under View > Effect Stack if hidden).
  2. Ensure your target clip or track is selected so its active effects are displayed.
  3. Locate the High-pass Filter in the stack list.

Step 4: Adjust the Cutoff Frequency

  1. In the High-pass Filter settings, locate the Frequency or Cutoff Frequency slider.
  2. Enter a specific numerical value or drag the slider to adjust the threshold:
    • 80 Hz: Standard baseline for deep male voices to remove sub-bass rumble without altering the natural tone.
    • 100 Hz – 120 Hz: Ideal standard range for general dialogue and female voices to cut mud and low-end room resonance.
    • 150 Hz or higher: Useful for extreme background rumble, though setting it too high can make voices sound thin or tinny.
  3. If available, adjust the Roll-off / Poles parameter (such as 12 dB/oct or 24 dB/oct) to determine how steeply frequencies below the cutoff are reduced.

Step 5: Preview and Fine-Tune

  1. Play back the timeline over the dialogue section.
  2. Toggle the effect on and off using the eye or power icon next to the filter name in the Effect Stack to compare the processed audio with the original.
  3. Fine-tune the frequency until the background rumble disappears while the dialogue remains full and clear.
