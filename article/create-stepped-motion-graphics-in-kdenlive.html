Create Stepped Motion Graphics in Kdenlive

Adjusting keyframe interpolation in Kdenlive allows you to switch from smooth, continuous motion to instant, stepped transitions, which is essential for creating stop-motion looks, retro UI animations, and snappy motion graphics. By default, Kdenlive applies linear or smooth interpolation between keyframe values. This guide explains how to change the interpolation type to discrete mode, forcing properties like position, scale, and rotation to jump instantaneously between frames rather than gradually shifting over time.

Understanding Keyframe Interpolation Types

Kdenlive uses three primary types of keyframe interpolation within its effect stack:

  • Linear: Moves between values at a constant, steady speed.
  • Smooth: Gradually eases in and eases out of keyframe values for fluid motion.
  • Discrete: Holds the current value until the playhead reaches the next keyframe, then immediately snaps to the new value without any transitional blending.

Steps to Enable Stepped Motion

  1. Apply a Transform Effect: Select the clip or graphic on your timeline. Open the Effects panel and add a transform effect, such as Transform or Position and Zoom, to the clip.
  2. Create Keyframes: In the Effect/Composition Stack panel, move the playhead to the desired point in time and create keyframes for your movement by adjusting the X/Y coordinates, scale, or rotation values.
  3. Change the Interpolation Mode:
    • Locate the keyframe ruler inside the effect controls.
    • Click on the specific keyframe you want to adjust to select it.
    • Look for the Interpolation dropdown menu located directly beneath or alongside the keyframe navigation bar.
    • Change the setting from Linear or Smooth to Discrete.
  4. Apply to Subsequent Keyframes: Repeat this process for each keyframe in the sequence. Each keyframe holding the "Discrete" type will retain its state entirely until the next keyframe is encountered.

Refining the Stepped Effect

  • Control the Rhythm: Since discrete keyframes hold their values without easing, the perceived speed depends entirely on the spacing between keyframes. Space keyframes uniformly across regular frame intervals (for example, every 4 or 6 frames) to emulate lower frame-rate animation styles like 2D cutouts or classic anime.
  • Combine with Posterize Time: To make an entire graphic animate at a lower frame rate automatically without placing dozens of individual discrete keyframes, you can pair smooth keyframes with a Posterize Time (or Motion / Speed) effect set to a reduced frame rate (such as 12 fps).
