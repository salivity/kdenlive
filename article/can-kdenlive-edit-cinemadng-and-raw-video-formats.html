Can Kdenlive Edit CinemaDNG and Raw Video Formats?

This article explores Kdenlive's compatibility with raw camera formats, specifically focusing on CinemaDNG. You will learn whether this popular open-source video editor can natively import and edit these high-quality files, the technical limitations of its FFmpeg-based engine, and the best workflows to successfully use raw footage in your Kdenlive projects.

The Short Answer: No Native Support

Kdenlive cannot natively import and edit raw camera formats like CinemaDNG with the level of control expected by professional colorists. Unlike professional color grading suites like DaVinci Resolve, Kdenlive does not feature a dedicated raw debayering engine. This means you cannot access non-destructive camera metadata settings—such as ISO, color temperature, exposure, and white balance adjustment—directly within the timeline or effect panel.

Why Kdenlive Struggles with Raw Formats

Kdenlive relies on the MLT Framework and FFmpeg to read and decode video files. While FFmpeg is incredibly versatile and can decode a vast array of compressed formats, it is not built to act as a raw development engine.

While you can technically import a CinemaDNG folder into Kdenlive by importing it as an image sequence (since CinemaDNG is essentially a folder of individual DNG images), this method has significant drawbacks:

  • No Raw Controls: You lose the ability to manipulate the raw sensor data before it is compressed into a display color space.
  • Performance Issues: Playing back an uncompressed sequence of high-resolution DNG images requires immense CPU and storage bandwidth, leading to severe timeline lag.
  • Color Handling: Kdenlive may not interpret the color space of the DNG sequence correctly, resulting in washed-out or heavily distorted colors.

The Recommended Workflow: Transcoding

To use CinemaDNG or other raw camera footage in Kdenlive, the industry-standard workaround is to transcode the footage before importing it.

Step 1: Develop the Raw Files

Use a dedicated raw development tool to adjust your exposure, white balance, and contrast. Excellent free and open-source options include:

  • DaVinci Resolve (Free Version): Outstanding for importing CinemaDNG, applying a primary color grade, and exporting.
  • RawTherapee or Darktable: Good for processing individual frames or sequences, though less streamlined for video.

Step 2: Export to an Edit-Friendly Codec

Export your developed footage into a high-quality, visually lossless intermediate format. The best choices for Kdenlive are:

  • ProRes (HQ or 422): Highly compatible with Kdenlive on Linux, macOS, and Windows.
  • DNxHR: An excellent Avid-designed alternative that performs exceptionally well in Kdenlive.

Step 3: Import and Edit in Kdenlive

Once transcoded into ProRes or DNxHR, the files will import instantly into Kdenlive. The editing process will be smooth, responsive, and free of the lag associated with raw image sequences.
