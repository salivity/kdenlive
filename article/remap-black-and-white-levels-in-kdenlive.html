Remap Black and White Levels in Kdenlive

This guide explains how to use the "Levels (RGB)" effect in Kdenlive to remap absolute black and white thresholds for your video clips. By adjusting input and output thresholds, you can correct improper exposure, expand dynamic range, lift crushed shadows, or clamp highlights to meet broadcast standards.

Step 1: Add the Levels (RGB) Effect

  1. Open your project in Kdenlive and select the clip in your timeline that requires tonal adjustment.
  2. Open the Effects panel (go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  3. Search for Levels (RGB) in the search bar or locate it under the Color and Image Correction category.
  4. Drag and drop the Levels (RGB) effect onto your timeline clip or double-click it while the clip is selected.

Step 2: Open Analysis Scopes

To accurately monitor your thresholds while making adjustments, enable Kdenlive's video scopes:

  • Go to View > Waveform or RGB Parade.
  • The waveform displays your luminance values from absolute black (0) at the bottom to absolute white (255) at the top.

Step 3: Configure the Levels (RGB) Parameters

In the Properties panel for the selected clip, adjust the following parameters to remap your thresholds:

  • Input Black (Min Input):
    • Increase this value above 0 to define a new absolute black point. Any pixel values below this threshold will be crushed to pure black (0). Use this if your shadows look washed out.
  • Input White (Max Input):
    • Decrease this value below 255 to define a new absolute white point. Any pixel values above this threshold will be blown out to pure white (255). Use this to brighten dull highlights.
  • Output Black (Min Output):
    • Increase this value above 0 to remap the lowest luminance point. Setting this higher (e.g., to 16 for standard broadcast limits) lifts the deepest blacks, turning pure black into dark gray.
  • Output White (Max Output):
    • Decrease this value below 255 to remap the highest luminance point. Setting this lower (e.g., to 235 for standard broadcast limits) clamps pure whites, preventing highlight clipping.
  • Gamma:
    • Adjust the slider to redistribute the midtones between your newly defined black and white limits without shifting the absolute thresholds.

Step 4: Fine-Tune Per Channel (Optional)

If your footage has a color cast in the highlights or shadows:

  1. Switch the channel selection within the effect from RGB (All) to Red, Green, or Blue.
  2. Remap the input and output thresholds for individual color channels to balance color levels independently.
