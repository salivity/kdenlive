Adjust High-Frequency Attenuation in Kdenlive

This guide explains how to control and reduce high-frequency audio in Kdenlive using the built-in Lowpass Filter effect. By applying this audio filter, you can cut harsh treble, remove high-pitched background hiss, and soften your overall sound mix directly on the timeline.

1. Add the Audio Clip to the Timeline

Import your audio or video file into your Kdenlive project and drag it onto an active track in the timeline. Make sure the clip is selected before applying effects.

2. Apply the Lowpass Filter Effect

  1. Open the Effects tab (typically located in the bottom-left or top-left panel, or access it via Menu > Settings > Audio > Effects).
  2. Type Lowpass in the search bar.
  3. Locate the Lowpass Filter (or Low Pass) effect under the Audio category.
  4. Drag and drop the effect directly onto your audio clip, or onto the entire audio track header if you want to apply the attenuation to all clips on that track.

3. Open the Effect Stack

Click on the clip containing the filter and open the Effect Stack (or Properties) panel to access the parameter controls.

4. Adjust the Cutoff Frequency

The primary parameter for adjusting high-frequency attenuation is the Cutoff Frequency (measured in Hertz, Hz):

  • Higher Frequency (e.g., 10,000 Hz – 20,000 Hz): Allows most of the audio spectrum through, attenuating only extreme high-end noise.
  • Mid-Range Frequency (e.g., 2,000 Hz – 5,000 Hz): Substantially cuts high frequencies, creating a warmer, muffled, or "behind-the-wall" sound effect.
  • Lower Frequency (e.g., below 1,000 Hz): Eliminates all clarity and treble, leaving only bass and low-mid tones.

Drag the frequency slider or type a specific numeric value to set the point where the high-frequency roll-off begins.

5. Configure Filter Steepness (If Applicable)

Depending on the specific backend filter used by your Kdenlive version (such as Avfilter or LADSPA):

  • Poles / Order: Select higher pole counts (e.g., 2-pole or 4-pole) for a sharper, more aggressive drop-off in high frequencies past the cutoff point.
  • Q / Resonance: Adjust the resonance factor to control whether there is a slight boost right at the cutoff point before attenuation begins.

6. Preview and Fine-Tune

Play back the timeline while adjusting the frequency slider in real time. Use headphones to ensure you eliminate unwanted high-frequency noise without making dialogue or key audio elements sound overly dull or unintelligible.
