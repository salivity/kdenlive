How to Use RGB Parade for Color Balance in Kdenlive

The RGB Parade scope in Kdenlive is an essential color-grading tool that displays separate waveform traces for the red, green, and blue color channels of video footage. This article explains the primary function of the RGB Parade, how it visualizes color distribution across shadows, midtones, and highlights, and how video editors use it to achieve seamless visual consistency when color-matching adjacent clips on the timeline.

Understanding the RGB Parade

The RGB Parade splits an image into its three primary color components (Red, Green, and Blue) and displays them side-by-side on a graphical scale.

  • Horizontal Axis: Represents the horizontal position across the video frame from left to right.
  • Vertical Axis: Represents brightness levels, typically measured from 0 (pure black/shadows) to 255 or 100 IRE (pure white/highlights).

By viewing the channels side-by-side, you can instantly see where each color channel sits across the tonal spectrum of a specific shot.

Identifying Color Casts

In a neutrally balanced shot, elements that should be pure black, neutral gray, or pure white will show identical signal levels across all three channels.

  • Highlights: If the top of the Red waveform is noticeably higher than the Green and Blue waveforms in a bright area, the highlights carry a red/warm color cast.
  • Shadows: If the bottom of the Blue waveform rests higher than the others near the baseline (0), the shadows contain elevated blue levels, creating a cool or tinted black point.
  • Midtones: Variations in the center portions of the waveforms indicate color bias in skin tones and neutral mid-tone areas.

Matching Color Balance Across Adjacent Clips

When cutting between two clips shot under different lighting, angles, or camera settings, the RGB Parade provides an objective measurement to make them visually cohesive.

  1. Establish a Reference Clip: Identify the primary clip on your timeline that represents the target color profile and look. Observe its baseline (black point), ceiling (white point), and general mid-tone balance across the Red, Green, and Blue traces.
  2. Analyze the Adjacent Clip: Move the playhead to the adjacent clip you need to match. Compare its RGB Parade profile to the reference clip.
  3. Align the Shadows (Lift/Offset): Apply a color correction effect (such as Lift/Gamma/Gain or Color Balance) to the uncorrected clip. Adjust the Lift/Black levels until the bottom traces of the R, G, and B channels align evenly with the black levels of the reference clip.
  4. Align the Highlights (Gain): Adjust the Gain/White levels so the top peaks of the RGB channels match the highlight heights and balance of the reference clip.
  5. Adjust the Midtones (Gamma): Tweak the Gamma to match exposure and skin tone levels between both shots. Ensure that neutral objects maintain equal channel height across the parade.

Using the RGB Parade eliminates guesswork caused by monitor inaccuracies or eye fatigue, ensuring consistent color temperature, exposure, and tone across every cut in your Kdenlive project.
