Kdenlive Alpha Shapes: Rapid Graphic Masking Guide

The "Alpha Shapes" effect in Kdenlive is a powerful tool designed for quick and efficient graphic masking. This article explains the core function of the Alpha Shapes effect and provides a step-by-step guide on how to apply and adjust it to crop, isolate, or highlight specific areas of your video footage without the need for complex, manual rotoscoping.

What is the Alpha Shapes Effect?

The Alpha Shapes effect is a generator that creates simple geometric masks directly on the alpha (transparency) channel of a video clip. Instead of manually drawing paths, you can choose from predefined shapes—such as rectangles, ellipses, triangles, and diamonds—to instantly mask out portions of a clip. It is highly optimized for performance, making it the ideal choice for rapid UI highlights, censorship blurs, picture-in-picture shaping, or simple transition wipes.

Key Parameters of Alpha Shapes

To use the effect effectively, it helps to understand its key settings in the Effect Properties panel:

  • Shape: Selects the geometry of the mask (Rectangle, Ellipse, Triangle, Diamond).
  • Position X & Y: Moves the shape across the frame.
  • Size X & Y: Adjusts the width and height of the shape independently.
  • Tilt: Rotates the shape.
  • Transition (Softness): Controls the feathering of the shape's edges. A higher value creates a soft, gradient edge, while 0 creates a hard, sharp edge.
  • Operation: Determines how the shape interacts with the existing alpha channel. The default "Write on Clear" clears the original frame transparency and replaces it with the shape. Other options like "Min" and "Max" allow you to combine multiple shapes or intersect them with existing transparency.

How to Use Alpha Shapes for Rapid Masking

Follow these steps to apply a rapid graphic mask to a clip in Kdenlive:

Step 1: Add your Footage

Drag the video or image clip you want to mask onto the timeline. If you plan to place another video or background behind the masked area, place your main clip on a higher track (e.g., V2) and the background clip on the track directly below it (e.g., V1).

Step 2: Apply the Effect

  1. Go to the Effects tab.
  2. In the search bar, type Alpha Shapes.
  3. Drag and drop the Alpha Shapes effect onto your timeline clip.

Step 3: Configure the Shape and Position

With the clip selected, look at the Effect/Composition Stack panel:

  1. Choose your desired shape from the Shape dropdown menu.
  2. Adjust the Size X and Size Y sliders to scale the mask to fit your target subject.
  3. Use the Position X and Position Y sliders (or drag the center crosshair directly in the Project Monitor) to position the mask over the area you want to isolate.

Step 4: Refine the Edges

To make the mask blend seamlessly with your background, adjust the Transition slider. Increasing this value feathers the edges of the shape, which is highly useful for soft vignettes or subtle face-blurring masks.

Step 5: Invert or Layer (Optional)

If you want to cut a hole out of your image rather than keeping only the inside of the shape, change the Operation setting. Experimenting with options like Subtract allows you to customize how the alpha transparency behaves to suit your project's needs.
