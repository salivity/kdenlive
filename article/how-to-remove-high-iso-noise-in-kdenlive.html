How to Remove High-ISO Noise in Kdenlive

High-ISO camera settings often introduce distracting digital grain and chrominance artifacts that degrade overall video quality. This guide demonstrates how to apply and configure a spatial denoiser effect within Kdenlive to clean up grainy footage, eliminate color speckling, and maintain crisp image details.

Step 1: Add Your Footage to the Timeline

Open Kdenlive and import the noisy video clip into the Project Bin. Drag the clip onto an active video track in the timeline where you want to perform the cleanup.

Step 2: Locate and Apply the Denoiser Effect

  1. Navigate to the Effects panel (go to View > Effects if it is not visible).
  2. Type Denoise in the search bar at the top of the Effects panel.
  3. Locate the Denoise effect (powered by the spatial/temporal hqdn3d or Bilateral filters depending on your Kdenlive version).
  4. Drag and drop the effect directly onto your noisy clip in the timeline.

Step 3: Configure Spatial Denoise Settings

Select the clip in the timeline to view its parameters in the Effect / Composition Stack panel. Focus on the spatial controls to smooth individual frames:

  • Spatial Luma (Luminance): Controls the smoothing of brightness-based grain. Increase this value gradually to soften visible dark-and-light noise patterns. Avoid setting it too high, as excessive values will blur fine details and textures.
  • Spatial Chroma (Chrominance): Controls color noise (the red, green, and blue splotches common in high-ISO footage). Chroma noise can usually be filtered more aggressively than luma noise without a noticeable loss of sharpness.
  • Radius / Strength (if using Bilateral/Alternative filters): Increase the threshold just enough to detect and smooth pixel variance without causing edge bleed.

Step 4: Preview and Fine-Tune

Zoom into the Clip Monitor to 100% or 200% on a dark or flat-colored area of the frame where noise is most prominent. Toggle the effect on and off using the eye icon in the Effect Stack to ensure the noise is reduced while facial features, text, and hard edges remain sharp.
